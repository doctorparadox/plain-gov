HOW CAMPAIGN MONEY MOVES — five pipes from a donor to a federal candidate
time-slice: September 12, 2026 · post-NRSC v. FEC · limits = 2025–2026 cycle
★ = struck down or unenforced   ⚑ = the door money actually uses   ║ = a legal wall

■ STAGE 0 — THE TWO QUESTIONS THAT SORT EVERY DOLLAR
│
├─◇ Q1  May it be spent WITH the candidate?  ── This sets the ceiling.
│       └─▶ yes (coordinated) ─▶ historically capped ─▶ ★ for parties, capped no longer
│       └─▶ no  (independent) ─▶ never capped since Buckley (1976) / SpeechNow (2010)
│
└─◇ Q2  Must the original SOURCE be named?   ── This sets the visibility.
        └─▶ yes ─▶ FEC reports, filed and public
        └─▶ no  ─▶ IRS-side entity, no donor schedule ─▶ the money still arrives

◆ PIPE A — THE CANDIDATE'S OWN COMMITTEE            [hard money · fully coordinated]
│  ├─ individual ........ $3,500 per election   ($7,000 per cycle)
│  ├─ federal PAC ....... $5,000 per election   [stat. — unchanged since 1976]
│  ├─ party committee ... $5,000 per election    (plus a separate indexed Senate limit)
│  ├─ the candidate ..... unlimited from personal funds
│  ├─ corporation / union ... PROHIBITED as a direct contribution (Tillman Act, 1907)
│  ├─ foreign national ...... PROHIBITED, every pipe, no exception
│  └─ disclosure ........ every dollar, itemized above $200, quarterly / pre-election
│
│     ⚑ the joint fundraising committee: one $100k+ check, split at the back end into
│       lawful slices for the candidate, the party, and the party's special accounts
│
◆ PIPE B — THE PARTY COMMITTEE                      [hard money · ★ NOW UNCAPPED]
│  ├─ individual → main account ......... $44,300 per year
│  ├─ individual → convention account ... $132,900 per year  (RNC / DNC only)
│  ├─ individual → building account ..... $132,900 per year
│  ├─ individual → legal / recount ...... $132,900 per year
│  ├─ individual → state party (federal) . $10,000 per year  [stat.]
│  │     ↳ one donor, one year, one national committee: $354,000 lawful
│  ├─ party → candidate, direct ......... $5,000 per election  (unchanged)
│  └─ ★ party → COORDINATED EXPENDITURE for that candidate ...... UNLIMITED
│        was: a population-and-office formula, low five figures to low seven
│        now: struck down, NRSC v. FEC, June 30, 2026, overruling Colorado II (2001)
│        effect: ads written with the campaign, on the campaign's message, no ceiling
│
◆ PIPE C — THE TRADITIONAL PAC                      [hard money · may coordinate]
│  ├─ individual → PAC ........ $5,000 per year   [stat. — unchanged since 1976]
│  ├─ PAC → candidate ......... $5,000 per election [stat.]
│  ├─ corporate treasury ...... PROHIBITED in; solicits its own class instead
│  └─ disclosure .............. full, both ends
│
◆ PIPE D — THE SUPER PAC                            [independent-expenditure-only]
│  ├─ any amount, any source (person, corporation, union, LLC, nonprofit)
│  ├─ spends UNLIMITED sums for or against a named candidate
│  ├─ ║ WALL: may not coordinate with that candidate  ── the only wall of consequence left
│  │     permitted anyway: a former campaign manager running it · public
│  │     "redboxing" instructions on a campaign website · shared vendors ·
│  │     jointly attended fundraisers · the candidate soliciting up to $5,000
│  └─ discloses its donors — which may be an LLC formed eight weeks ago
│
◆ PIPE E — THE 501(c)(4) LAYER                      [feeds C and D · source hidden]
│  ├─ unlimited in, no donor schedule ever filed with the FEC
│  ├─ political activity must not be its "primary purpose" — an IRS test, not an FEC one
│  ├─ ⚑ transfers to a super PAC ─▶ the super PAC discloses the (c)(4), not the donor
│  └─ this is the whole mechanism behind the phrase "dark money"

■ THE WALLS STILL STANDING — and who is enforcing them
│
├─ 1  no coordination between a super PAC and a candidate ....... FEC  ★ unenforced
├─ 2  earmarking / conduit rule (a pass-through counts as direct)  FEC  ★ unenforced
├─ 3  disclosure of contributions and expenditures ............... FEC  (filings continue)
├─ 4  no foreign national money, direct or indirect .............. FEC + DOJ
└─ 5  no straw-donor or reimbursed contributions ................. DOJ  (criminal)
        note: walls 1 and 2 are the two the Supreme Court named in McCutcheon (2014)
        and again in NRSC (2026) as the adequate substitutes that made the struck
        limits unnecessary. They are administered by the body immediately below.

┌─ WHAT THE SHAPE SHOWS ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1  The wall that fell was the one being enforced; the wall left standing is the one     │
│    nobody is enforcing. Coordinated party limits were administered by filing.           │
│    The super-PAC coordination ban requires an investigation and four votes.             │
│ 2  The Court's own reasoning depends on the agency. McCutcheon and NRSC both hold        │
│    the struck limits unnecessary BECAUSE earmarking and disclosure rules suffice.       │
│    Both are enforced by a commission that has had no quorum for seventeen months.       │
│ 3  Money is flowing back toward the party, not around it. From 2010 to 2026 the party   │
│    was the weak pipe and the super PAC the strong one. Pipe B is now the highest-       │
│    capacity coordinated channel in the system — and it names its donors.                │
│ 4  A ceiling that is not indexed is a repeal on a delay. $5,000 in 1976 dollars is      │
│    roughly $28,000 today; Pipes A and C still say $5,000.                               │
│ 5  Nothing on this map required a vote of Congress. Four Supreme Court decisions,       │
│    one appellate ruling, one 2014 omnibus rider, and four unfilled seats.               │
