■ THERE IS NO FEDERAL POLICE FORCE
│   ● 133,798 officers · 89 reporting agencies · no common chain of command, no common standard
│   ⚑ NO GENERAL FEDERAL ARREST STATUTE EXISTS. Each agency's power comes from its own
│     section of the code, enacted separately, on its own terms. 18 U.S.C. ch. 203 collects
│     a handful of them; most of this tree is authorized somewhere else entirely.
│   ⚑ 42 of the 89 agencies are Offices of Inspector General — 47% of the agency count,
│     2.6% of the officers. The agency number in circulation is mostly an oversight number.

├─◆ THE OVERSIGHT LAYER — 42 OFFICES OF INSPECTOR GENERAL  ●  3,519   2.6% of ALL officers
│   │   Half the agency count. One fortieth of the force. And the only branch of this tree
│   │   whose arrest power belongs to somebody outside it.

│   ├─ Largest: U.S. Postal Service OIG ● 461 · HHS OIG ● 458 · War (DoD) OIG ● 382
│   │           TIGTA ● 253 · Social Security Admin. OIG ● 245 · DHS OIG ● 217
│   ├─ Smallest: Library of Congress OIG ● 3 · Pension Benefit Guaranty Corp. OIG ● 2
│   │
│   ├─ § THE AUTHORITY IS THE ATTORNEY GENERAL'S — 5 U.S.C. § 406(f)
│   │     (f)(1) An IG, an Assistant IG for Investigations, and supervised special agents
│   │            "may be authorized BY THE ATTORNEY GENERAL" to carry firearms, arrest
│   │            without warrant, and seek and execute warrants.
│   │     (f)(2) He may do so only on a three-part determination: the office is
│   │            "significantly hampered" without the powers · other agencies' help is
│   │            insufficient · adequate internal safeguards exist.
│   │     (f)(3) TWENTY-FIVE named offices are exempt from that initial determination.
│   │            Every other OIG — including the largest, the Postal Service's — is not.
│   │     (f)(5) He may RESCIND OR SUSPEND the powers: for a whole office, or for one
│   │            named individual agent.
│   │     (f)(6) ⚑ "A determination by the Attorney General under paragraph (2) or (5)
│   │            SHALL NOT BE REVIEWABLE IN OR BY ANY COURT."
│   │     (f)(8) None of it limits Marshals Service special deputation, which runs parallel.
│   │
│   └─ ⚑ So the investigators assigned to find wrongdoing inside the executive branch
│        carry guns and make arrests at the discretion of the executive branch's chief
│        prosecutor, under a decision no judge may look at. Down 6% since FY2020.

└─◆ ⚑ THE LINES MOVE WITHOUT CONGRESS — three doors between the branches above
    │
    ├─ 1. CROSS-DESIGNATION AS IMMIGRATION OFFICERS — 8 U.S.C. § 1103(a)(4), (6)
    │      The Secretary of Homeland Security may designate other federal officers as
    │      immigration officers. Used in April 2025 to designate Marshals Service, DEA,
    │      BOP and ATF personnel, and any other DOJ employee the Attorney General approves
    │      — stated as giving them the authority the FBI already had. One memo; no statute.
    │
    ├─ 2. MARSHALS SERVICE SPECIAL DEPUTATION — 28 U.S.C. § 566
    │      Federal arrest power extended to state, local, tribal and other federal officers
    │      for task-force work. The mechanism by which a city detective acts federally.
    │
    └─ 3. THE § 287(g) AGREEMENT — 8 U.S.C. § 1357(g)
           The reverse direction: state and local officers performing immigration
           enforcement functions under a written agreement with DHS.

    ⚑ Read together with the OIG branch: the arrest authority in this tree is far less
      fixed than a department-by-department chart suggests. Who may arrest whom, for what,
      is adjusted by memorandum at both ends — and at the oversight end, unreviewably.
