│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: THE ONE PROTECTION FEDERAL LAW GIVES — A FORWARDABLE
│  NOTICE WITH A RETURN CARD — IS MAILED TO THE ADDRESS THE TRIGGERING DATABASE
│  HAS ALREADY CONCLUDED IS WRONG, AND THE VOTER'S SILENCE IS THEN READ AS
│  CONFIRMATION OF THE MOVE. See Part B ▸ 3, then Part D before trusting any
│  description of list maintenance as a process with notice in it.

■ PART A — THE FOUR DOORS OUT  [§ 20507(a)(3): the list is exhaustive]
│
│  "shall not remove the name of a registrant ... except — (A) at the request of
│   the registrant; (B) as provided by State law, by reason of criminal
│   conviction or mental incapacity; or (C) as provided under subsection (a)(4)"
│
├─▸ DOOR 1. AT THE REGISTRANT'S OWN REQUEST .......... § 20507(a)(3)(A)
│     ⚡ a signed request    ◇ the registrar, no discretion
│     ⊘ no notice owed · ⊘ no waiting period · ⊘ EXEMPT from the 90-day rule
│
├─▸ DOOR 2. CRIMINAL CONVICTION ...................... § 20507(a)(3)(B)
│     ⚡ a conviction record, matched by the state       ◇ STATE LAW decides
│     ⊘ no federal notice owed · ⊘ no waiting period · ⊘ EXEMPT from 90 days
│     ✱ FEDERAL LAW SETS NO STANDARD HERE AT ALL — it only permits what state
│       law already provides, so the scope is 50 different answers and the
│       federal floor is zero. See Part C.
│
├─▸ DOOR 3. ADJUDICATED MENTAL INCAPACITY ............ § 20507(a)(3)(B)
│     ⚡ a court adjudication      ◇ STATE LAW decides; a court, not a registrar
│     ⊘ no federal notice owed · ⊘ no waiting period · ⊘ EXEMPT from 90 days
│
└─▸ DOOR 4. THE "GENERAL PROGRAM" ................... § 20507(a)(3)(C)+(a)(4)
      "a general program that makes a reasonable effort to remove the names of
       ineligible voters ... by reason of — (A) the death of the registrant; or
       (B) a change in the residence of the registrant"
      ⚡ a database match      ◇ the registrar, under §§ (b), (c) and (d)
      │
      ├─▶ 4A. DEATH ......... ⊘ no notice owed · ⊘ EXEMPT from 90 days
      │        ✱ THE ONLY DOOR THE VOTER CANNOT WALK BACK THROUGH ON ELECTION
      │          DAY. Part F's affirmation remedy is a change-of-address remedy;
      │          a living person removed as dead has no § 20507(e) route at all.
      │
      └─▶ 4B. CHANGE OF RESIDENCE ... ⛔ THE ONE GUARDED DOOR → Part B
               the notice procedure, two federal general elections, the 90-day
               quiet period, two years of public records, and a right to vote
               anyway. Everything in this map that looks like due process is here.
│
│  ✱ STRUCTURAL POINT OF PART A, AND THE INVERSION THE WHOLE MAP TURNS ON:
│    THE PROCEDURE RUNS INVERSELY TO THE FINALITY. The removal a voter can cure
│    at the polls with a signature (4B) carries a notice, a card, a grace period
│    of two federal general elections, a 90-day blackout and a public record.
│    The removals that are categorical and uncurable — dead, convicted,
│    adjudicated incompetent — carry NO federal notice, NO waiting period and an
│    EXPRESS EXEMPTION from the blackout.  § 20507(c)(2)(B)
│
│  ✱ AND WHAT IS NOT ON THIS LIST: NON-CITIZENSHIP. It is not an enumerated
│    federal ground. Every noncitizen-removal program of 2024–26 therefore has
│    to be either a state-law program or an (a)(4) "general program" — which is
│    the entire fight in RNC v. Mi Familia Vota.  ⚑ See Part G.
│
■ PART B — THE GUARDED DOOR: CHANGE OF RESIDENCE, STEP BY STEP
│
├─▸ 1. THE SENSOR FIRES ......................... § 20507(c)(1) and Part D
│     the Postal Service NCOA file is the ONE sensor the statute names and
│     blesses; every other sensor in Part D is a state-law invention
│     │
│     ├─▶ MOVED WITHIN the registrar's jurisdiction
│     │     ▶ the registrar CORRECTS the record and mails a notice
│     │     ▶ the voter stays registered — this is not a removal
│     │
│     └─▶ MOVED OUTSIDE the registrar's jurisdiction
│           ▶ go to step 2. There is no shortcut.      § 20507(c)(1)(B)(ii)
│
├─▸ 2. THE WRITTEN-CONFIRMATION SHORTCUT ........ § 20507(d)(1)(A)
│     ⚡ the registrant CONFIRMS IN WRITING a move outside the jurisdiction
│     ▶ IMMEDIATE REMOVAL. No notice, no waiting, no two elections.
│     ✱ This is the only fast path through the guarded door, and it requires
│       the voter's own signature. Everything else goes through step 3.
│
├─▸ 3. ⛔ THE NOTICE — THE WHOLE PROTECTION ..... § 20507(d)(2)
│     what the statute requires, exactly:
│       • sent by FORWARDABLE MAIL
│       • containing a POSTAGE-PREPAID, PRE-ADDRESSED RETURN CARD
│       • stating that if the voter did NOT move, or moved within the
│         jurisdiction, they should return the card by the next registration
│         deadline
│       • stating that if the card is NOT returned, the voter may be required to
│         affirm or confirm their address before voting in a federal election,
│         through the day after the SECOND federal general election following
│       • stating that failing to vote in that period results in removal
│     │
│     ├─▶ CARD RETURNED, no move → nothing happens. The voter is protected.
│     ├─▶ CARD RETURNED, moved out → removal under § 20507(d)(1)(A)
│     └─▶ CARD NOT RETURNED → step 4
│     │
│     ✱ ✱ THE FINDING. THE NOTICE IS MAILED TO THE ADDRESS ON FILE — the address
│       the sensor in step 1 has just asserted is no longer the voter's. The
│       statute answers this with the word "forwardable," which works only if
│       the Postal Service holds a forwarding order that has not expired. Where
│       the sensor is not the NCOA file but a vendor's guess, the notice is
│       mailed to the GUESS.  ⚑ Mississippi, 2025–26: Experian TrueTrace address
│       data, all 82 counties from July 2025, at least 50,000 voters made
│       inactive †, and per Mississippi Today affected voters "never received
│       the required notice, which was often mailed to incorrect Experian
│       addresses." Experian's own position on the product: "we don't verify
│       residency."  [Miss. HB 1310 (2023) is the state authority]
│     ✱ SO THE SILENCE THAT CONVICTS THE VOTER IS PRODUCED BY THE SAME ERROR THE
│       NOTICE EXISTS TO CATCH. Non-delivery and non-response are indistinguish-
│       able at the registrar's desk, and the statute assigns both to the voter.
│
├─▸ 4. INACTIVE STATUS — REGISTERED, BUT NOT COUNTED AS SUCH
│     ⚡ the card was not returned by the next registration deadline
│     ▶ the voter may be moved to INACTIVE. They are NOT removed. They may
│       still vote — see Part F — but in most states they drop out of the
│       "registered voters" denominator, out of absentee-mailing lists, and out
│       of party and campaign contact files.
│     ✱ THIS IS WHERE THE LARGE NUMBERS LIVE, AND WHY "PURGE" AND "REMOVAL"
│       UNDERSTATE THE EXPOSURE. Inactive status is invisible to the voter until
│       they try to vote, which is typically the first and only notice that
│       works.  ⚑ Mississippi's 50,000 were INACTIVATIONS, not removals, and
│       voters found out at the polls in the 10 March 2026 primary.
│
└─▸ 5. ⛔ REMOVAL, AFTER TWO FEDERAL GENERAL ELECTIONS .... § 20507(d)(1)(B)
      ⚡ the voter neither responded to the notice NOR voted or appeared to vote
         from the date of the notice through the day after the SECOND federal
         general election following it
      ▶ now the name may come off the list. Elapsed time: roughly four years.
      ✱ FOUR YEARS IS THE LONGEST MANDATORY WAITING PERIOD IN AMERICAN ELECTION
        LAW, AND IT PROTECTS ONLY THIS ONE DOOR. The other three remove on the
        day the record matches.
│
■ PART C — THE PROHIBITED GROUND THAT OPERATES AS THE SENSOR
│
│  § 20507(b)(2), one paragraph, two halves that point opposite ways:
│    HALF ONE ... any list-maintenance program "shall not result in the removal
│                 of the name of any person ... by reason of the person's
│                 failure to vote"
│    HALF TWO ... "except that nothing in this paragraph may be construed to
│                 prohibit a State from using the procedures described in
│                 subsections (c) and (d) to remove an individual from the
│                 official list ... if the individual — (A) has not either
│                 notified the applicable registrar ... or responded during the
│                 period described in subparagraph (B) to the notice ...; and
│                 (B) has not voted or appeared to vote ... in any election
│                 during the period beginning on the date of the notice and
│                 ending on the day after the date of the second general
│                 election for Federal office"
│
├─▸ HUSTED v. A. PHILIP RANDOLPH INSTITUTE ....... 584 U.S. ___ (2018)
│     decided 11 June 2018 · 5–4 · ALITO, with Roberts, Kennedy, Thomas, Gorsuch
│     ⚡ Ohio used FAILURE TO VOTE for two years as the trigger to SEND the card
│     ◇ held: lawful. Ohio "does not strike any registrant solely by reason of
│       the failure to vote"; it removes "only when they have failed to vote and
│       have failed to respond to a change-of-residence notice."
│     ✱ THE WORD CARRYING THE DECISION IS "SOLELY." Non-voting may not be the
│       whole reason, so a state adds a letter and non-voting becomes the
│       sensor instead of the ground. The prohibition survives intact and stops
│       nothing: after Husted, inactivity is the cheapest, broadest trigger
│       available, and it is aimed at precisely the registrants least likely to
│       return a card.

■ PART D — THE SENSORS: WHAT TELLS A STATE YOU MOVED  (⚑ this is the live layer)
│
│  the statute names ONE and permits the rest by silence
│
├─▸ 1. USPS NATIONAL CHANGE OF ADDRESS (NCOA) ...... § 20507(c)(1) — NAMED
│     the only sensor Congress wrote into the Act. A state that uses it
│     satisfies the (a)(4) duty by its terms.
│
├─▸ 2. ERIC — THE MULTISTATE CROSS-MATCH ........... membership, not statute
│     member-state obligations: request and ACT ON at least one list-maintenance
│     report a year (in-state movers · movers to another member state · the
│     deceased · duplicate registrations), AND mail notices to people found
│     eligible but unregistered — the one list-maintenance instrument in the
│     country that is required to ADD voters as well as subtract them.
│     ✱ WHICH IS WHY LEAVING IT CUTS BOTH WAYS, AND WHY NINE STATES LEFT:
│       LA (2022) · AL (Jan 2023) · FL · MO · WV · OH (Mar 2023) · IA (Jun 2023)
│       · VA (announced Aug 2023) · TX (Jul 2023)
│     ⚑ VIRGINIA REVERSED — executive order 25 March 2026 directing a return,
│       plus VA2026 S 57 signed in April 2026. The only re-entry so far.
│
├─▸ 3. STATE DMV / MOTOR VEHICLE RECORDS ........... state law
│     ⚑ the 2024 Virginia program ran on DAILY DMV feeds — see Part G.
│
├─▸ 4. DHS "SAVE" + SSA RECORDS .................... ⚑ REBUILT 2025–26
│     Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements, a DHS database in use
│     since 1986 for benefits and licence eligibility. Under EO 14248
│     (25 March 2025) it was rebuilt to: hold records of NATURAL-BORN CITIZENS ·
│     reach SOCIAL SECURITY ADMINISTRATION records including SSNs · permit

│     ⚑ FIVE STATES NOW MANDATE SAVE CHECKS OF ALL REGISTERED VOTERS:
│       IA · KS · LA · MS · TN.  NORTH CAROLINA requires annual checks by
│       ADMINISTRATIVE RULE of the State Board — not by statute.
│     ✱ THE TOOL WAS BUILT FOR INDIVIDUAL BENEFIT ELIGIBILITY AND IS NOW A
│       LIST-MAINTENANCE SENSOR, WHICH IS WHY THE "SYSTEMATIC" QUESTION IN
│       PART E IS NOW THE WHOLE GAME.

├─▸ 5. DEATH RECORDS .............. 52 U.S.C. § 21083 (HAVA § 303) coordination
│     the statewide database required by HAVA must coordinate with state death
│     records and state felony-conviction records. Door 4A and Door 2 run here.

├─▸ 6. JURY SUMMONS RETURNS, UNDELIVERABLE MAIL, TAX NOTICES ...... state law
│
└─▸ 7. ⚑ CONSUMER CREDIT DATA ..................... THE OUTER EDGE
      MISSISSIPPI: Experian TrueTrace address guesses, piloted in Lafayette

■ PART E — THE 90-DAY QUIET PERIOD, AND THE WORD IT TURNS ON
│
│  § 20507(c)(2)(A): a state "shall complete, not later than 90 days prior to
│  the date of a primary or general election for Federal office, any program
│  the purpose of which is to SYSTEMATICALLY remove the names of ineligible
│  voters from the official lists of eligible voters"
│
├─▸ FOR 3 NOVEMBER 2026, THE BOUNDARY WAS ⚑ 5 AUGUST 2026.
│     DOJ guidance treats the covered activities as including general mailings
│     and door-to-door canvasses.
│
├─▸ WHAT § 20507(c)(2)(B) EXPRESSLY EXEMPTS FROM THE DEADLINE:
│     • removal at the registrant's request ............ Door 1
│     • removal by reason of death ..................... Door 4A
│     • removal for criminal conviction or mental incapacity ... Doors 2 and 3
│     • CORRECTIONS to a registrant's record ........... unbounded by its terms
│     ✱ THREE OF THE FOUR DOORS ARE OPEN ALL YEAR. The blackout closes one.
│
└─▸ ✱ ✱ SO THE QUIET PERIOD PROTECTS A METHOD, NOT A VOTER. It is a limit on
      the SHAPE of a removal process, not on the number of people removed, and
      a state that can characterise its work as individualised rather than
      systematic is outside it. In 2026 the characterisation got easier:
      ⚑ DHS v. LEAGUE OF WOMEN VOTERS, No. 26A308, 25 SEPTEMBER 2026, 6–3,
        unsigned, emergency docket. The Court STAYED Judge Sparkle Sooknanan's
        (D.D.C.) injunction against the rebuilt SAVE system — she had found the
        administration "knowingly trampled on the privacy rights of American
        citizens" and that some states removed U.S. citizens from the rolls on
        inaccurate information; the D.C. Circuit had denied a stay earlier in
        September. SOTOMAYOR, KAGAN and JACKSON dissented; Jackson wrote that
        the harm of disenfranchising even a few lawful voters outweighs the
        government's — and noted that states "could still use the revamped
        system for individualized voter-roll maintenance."
      ✱ BULK SEARCH PLUS AN INDIVIDUALISED-INQUIRY CARVE-OUT IS A SYSTEMATIC
        PROGRAM THAT DOES NOT MEET THE DEFINITION OF ONE.

■ PART F — ELECTION DAY: WHAT A REMOVED OR INACTIVE VOTER CAN STILL DO
│
├─▸ 1. THE § 20507(e) AFFIRMATION — the statute's own second chance
│     a registrant who did not return the card may still vote:
│     ├─▶ (e)(1) moved WITHIN the same polling area → vote there after
│     │          affirming the new address, orally or in writing
│     ├─▶ (e)(2) moved to another polling area in the same jurisdiction AND
│     │          congressional district → vote at the FORMER polling place, at a
│     │          CENTRAL LOCATION, or (where state law allows) at the new
│     │          polling place; a state offering one need not offer the others
│     └─▶ (e)(3) records show a move OUT of the polling area → the registrant
│                may STILL VOTE THERE by affirming they live at the address on
│                file
│     ✱ THIS IS A REAL AND UNDERUSED REMEDY, AND IT IS WHY INACTIVE STATUS IS
│       NOT DISENFRANCHISEMENT ON PAPER. It fails in practice for one reason:
│       it requires a poll worker to know it exists and the voter to be told.
│       ⊘ NOTHING IN THE STATUTE REQUIRES THE VOTER TO BE INFORMED OF IT.
│
├─▸ 2. THE PROVISIONAL BALLOT — where the affirmation is not offered
│     ⚡ the voter is not on the roll, or is listed inactive, and no one invokes
│       (e). HAVA § 302 guarantees the ballot; nearly every consequential rule
│       about it is state law — the provisional ballot, covered on its own page.
│     ⚑ MISSISSIPPI, 10 MARCH 2026 PRIMARY: inactive voters discovered their
│       status at the polls; some were given affidavit ballots, some left
│       without voting, and one 12-year voter received no ballot to mark at all.
│
└─▸ 3. ⊘ AND IF THE DOOR WAS 4A, 2 OR 3 — NOTHING HERE APPLIES.
      § 20507(e) is a change-of-address remedy by its terms. A living voter
      removed as deceased, or removed on a conviction match that is wrong or
      belongs to someone else, has no statutory election-day cure. They are
      a new registrant, against a registration deadline that has passed.

■ PART H — WHAT NO POST-ELECTION INSTRUMENT REACHES
│
│  the series, laid against this process:
│    RECOUNT ......... re-counts ACCEPTED ballots .......... ⊘ cannot reach a removal
│    AUDIT ........... re-reads a SAMPLE of accepted ballots ⊘ cannot reach a removal
│    CANVASS ......... totals accepted ballots ............. ⊘ cannot reach a removal
│    CONTEST ......... attacks the RESULT in a tribunal .... ⚠ in principle only
│    TIE-BREAK ....... resolves equal totals ............... ⊘ cannot reach a removal
│
└─▸ ✱ THE CLOSING STRUCTURAL POINT. A REMOVAL IS DECIDED IN MARCH AND BECOMES
      UNREVIEWABLE IN NOVEMBER. Every instrument the vault has mapped for
      correcting an election operates on the ballots that were cast, and a
      removal's effect is a ballot that was never cast — which leaves no
      artifact to recount, no sample to audit, and no number to contest. The
      only in-election trace is a provisional ballot, and whether the voter is
      ever told it was rejected is a matter of state law. ⊘ IN NO STATE IS A
      LIST-MAINTENANCE ERROR A GROUND FOR THE ELECTION TO BE RERUN.
