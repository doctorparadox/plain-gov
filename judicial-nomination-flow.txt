■ 0. THE FLOOR  —  what the Constitution actually assigns, and to whom
│     │
│     ├─ APPOINTMENTS CLAUSE (Art. II, § 2, cl. 2) — the President "shall nominate, and by
│     │    and with the Advice and Consent of the Senate, shall appoint ... Judges of the
│     │    supreme Court".  TWO acts, not one: nomination is the President's alone;
│     │    appointment happens only after the Senate consents.  The House has no role.
│     │
│     ├─ ARTICLE III, § 1 — judges "shall hold their Offices during good Behaviour" and their
│     │    compensation "shall not be diminished during their Continuance in Office."
│     │    No term.  No mandatory retirement age.  No performance review.
│     │
│     └─ CONGRESS CREATES THE SEATS, BY ORDINARY STATUTE — not the Constitution
│          ├─ 9 Supreme Court justices ......... set by statute since 1869; NOT constitutional
│          ├─ 179 circuit judgeships ........... 28 U.S.C. § 44, allocated circuit by circuit
│          │      DC 11 · 1st 6 · 2d 13 · 3d 14 · 4th 15 · 5th 17 · 6th 16 · 7th 11
│          │      8th 11 · 9th 29 · 10th 12 · 11th 12 · Federal 12
│          ├─ 677 district judgeships .......... 28 U.S.C. § 133, allocated district by district
│          │      [677 includes 10 temporary judgeships and 4 Article IV territorial seats;
│          │       673 is the Article III district count — both figures are correct, they
│          │       answer different questions]
│          └─ 9 Court of International Trade ... also Article III, also life-tenured
│               [Total authorized Article III seats: 9 + 179 + 673 + 9]
│
├──▶ 1. A SEAT MUST OPEN   ◆ THE STAGE THAT ACTUALLY RATIONS THE APPOINTMENT POWER
│     │   Six doors, and the executive branch controls exactly one of them.
│     │
│     ├─ DEATH IN ACTIVE SERVICE ................................ nobody's decision
│     ├─ RESIGNATION or full retirement (28 U.S.C. § 371(a)) ..... the judge's decision
│     ├─ ELEVATION (district judge confirmed to a circuit) ....... creates a second vacancy
│     │      behind it — one confirmation, two seats to fill
│     ├─ REMOVAL BY IMPEACHMENT ................................. the ONLY removal route
│     │      House impeaches by majority · Senate convicts by two-thirds
│     │      [15 federal judges impeached since 1803; 8 convicted and removed;
│     │       4 acquitted; 3 resigned before a Senate vote.  Most recent: 2010]
│     ├─ NEW JUDGESHIP CREATED BY STATUTE ....................... requires an act of Congress
│     │      [NONE enacted 2025-26.  JUDGES Act of 2024 passed both chambers and was
│     │       VETOED Dec 2024.  Reintroduced as H.R. 1702 (65 permanent + 1 temporary
│     │       district seats): reported out of House Judiciary 16-11 on Mar 5, 2025 and
│     │       has had NO further action since]
│     │
│     └─◆ SENIOR STATUS (28 U.S.C. § 371(b)) — the door that supplies most vacancies
│          │   The judge keeps the office and the salary, retires from REGULAR ACTIVE
│          │   service, and the seat opens for a successor (§ 371(d)).  Voluntary.
│          │
│          ├─ ELIGIBILITY — the "RULE OF 80" (§ 371(c)), though the statute never says 80:
│          │      age 65 + 15 yrs · 66 + 14 · 67 + 13 · 68 + 12 · 69 + 11 · 70 + 10
│          │      Each pair sums to 80.  § 371(f): service on ANY Art. III court counts,
│          │      so an elevated district judge qualifies earlier than a direct appointee.
│          ├─ ELIGIBLE ≠ OBLIGED — a qualifying judge may stay in active service for life
│          ├─ CONDITIONAL TIMING — a judge may announce senior status effective on a date,
│          │      on a successor's confirmation, or not at all.  This makes the timing of
│          │      the vacancy a political act available to the judge, not to the President.
│          └─ KEEPING THE SALARY (§ 371(e)) — a senior judge draws the CURRENT salary with
│                 future raises only if certified annually by the chief judge of the circuit
│                 as carrying >= 3 months of an average active judge's caseload (or disabled);
│                 otherwise the pay freezes at the rate on the day of retirement.

├──▶ 5. ◇ THE BLUE SLIP GATE   ◆ THE LAST MINORITY VETO IN THE JUDICIAL PIPELINE
│     │   Each home-state senator receives a blue form.  Returned = proceed.  Withheld =
│     │   the chairman does not schedule a hearing, and the nomination simply never moves.
│     │   Not a rule.  Not a statute.  Not enforceable.  Entirely the chairman's policy.
│     │
│     ├─ CIRCUIT JUDGES ......... NO BLUE SLIP.  Abandoned in 2017.  Objections are noted
│     │                             and ignored.
│     ├─ DISTRICT JUDGES ........ BLUE SLIP APPLIES
│     ├─ U.S. ATTORNEYS ......... BLUE SLIP APPLIES
│     ├─ U.S. MARSHALS .......... BLUE SLIP APPLIES
│     │
│     ├──✕ WITHHELD ─▶ the nomination dies of never being scheduled.  No vote is ever taken,
│     │        so there is no record of it failing.
│     │        [LIVE 2026: the Senate Democratic leader has withheld slips on New York
│     │         district and U.S. Attorney nominees for over a year.  A U.S. Attorney
│     │         nomination in New Jersey collapsed on withheld slips in Dec 2025]
│     │        [AND IT CUTS BOTH WAYS: Michigan's and Pennsylvania's Democratic senators
│     │         RETURNED slips in 2026, which is the only reason two district nominees from
│     │         blue states got hearings on June 10, 2026]
│     │
│     └─ WHY IT SURVIVED: the President demanded publicly, repeatedly, from July 2025
│            onward that the chairman abolish it.  The chairman refused and, on June 18,
│            2026, said he will keep it as long as he chairs the committee.  The majority
│            leader backed him, citing his own use of a blue slip against a Biden nominee.
│            ⇒ It is held in place by the expectation of being in the minority again —
│              not by tradition, and not by any text.

├──▶ 6. COMMITTEE STAGE
│     ├─ HEARING — typically 4-5 district nominees on one panel; circuit and Supreme Court
│     │      nominees heard alone.  Questions for the Record follow in writing.
│     ├─ HELD OVER — any single member may hold a nomination over for one week at the first
│     │      markup it appears on.  Effectively automatic; costs one week.
│     ├─◇ COMMITTEE VOTE — simple majority.  Party-line is now the norm.
│     │      └──✕ voted down ─▶ dead (a chairman may still discharge, but does not)
│     └─ REPORTED ──▶ placed on the SENATE EXECUTIVE CALENDAR

│     │      package.  The point of order carving out the exceptions reads: all nominations

│     │      ├─ EXCLUDED: Supreme Court, Courts of Appeals, District Courts, Court of
│     │      │      International Trade — and the 21 Level I executive-schedule seats

│     │      [No Article III nominee has been confirmed by voice vote or unanimous consent

│     └──✕ RETURNED TO THE PRESIDENT — at the end of a session, or any adjournment or recess
│              of more than 30 days, every pending nomination is returned unless the Senate
│              agrees to hold it over (Senate Rule XXXI).  A returned nominee must be
│              RENOMINATED and, in practice, re-run through the committee.

└──▶ 8. APPOINTMENT AND SEATING   ★
      ├─ The President signs the COMMISSION — the appointment is complete at this act,
      │      not at the Senate vote.  The President may decline to sign; a nominee may
      │      decline to accept.
      ├─ TWO OATHS, both required before taking office:
      │      constitutional oath ... 5 U.S.C. § 3331 ("support and defend the Constitution")
      │      judicial oath ......... 28 U.S.C. § 453 ("administer justice without respect
      │                              to persons, and do equal right to the poor and to the rich")
      ├─ Investiture; the judge takes the bench
      └─ TENURE BEGINS AND DOES NOT END — no term, no reappointment, no review, no recall.

  FIVE STRUCTURAL FACTS
  1. THE VACANCY IS THE SCARCE RESOURCE, NOT THE VOTES.  With a friendly Senate and no
     filibuster, the appointment power still stalls, because the only large supply of
     vacancies comes from sitting judges volunteering to leave — and the timing of that
     is theirs.  Zero circuit vacancies in Sept 2026 is not a Senate outcome.
  2. NEARLY EVERY SENATE RULE HERE IS PRECEDENT, NOT TEXT.  Majority cloture (2013, 2017),
     the 2-hour district clock (2019), and the en bloc procedure (2025) were all created by
     a majority overturning the presiding officer.  None is in the Standing Rules.  Each can
     be undone the same way, in one afternoon, by whoever next holds 51 seats.
  3. THE BLUE SLIP IS THE ONLY REMAINING MINORITY VETO, AND IT IS A COURTESY.  It survives
     on the majority's expectation of someday being the minority.  One chairman's decision
     ends it — and the chairmanship is unsettled, since the senator next in line for the
     Republican slot died in July 2026.
  4. THE SLOW LANE FOR JUDGES IS DELIBERATE, AND IT WAS BUILT BY THE JUDGES' OWN ALLIES.
     The 2025 en bloc carve-out for Article III seats was written into the text of the point
     of order.  A majority that wanted to batch-confirm judges could have; it chose not to.
  5. THE EXIT IS NARROWER THAN THE ENTRANCE.  Confirmation takes 51 votes.  Removal takes
     a House majority and 67 senators, and has succeeded 8 times in 220 years.  Everything
     upstream of Stage 1 is contested annually; Stage 8 is effectively permanent.
