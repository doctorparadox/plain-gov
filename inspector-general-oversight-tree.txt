INSPECTOR GENERAL OVERSIGHT TREE — THE INTERNAL-WATCHDOG LAYER (as of September 6, 2026)
│   Inspector General Act of 1978, as amended (5 U.S.C. §§ 401–424) · ~74 statutory IGs
│   ★ = presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed    ◇ = appointed by the agency head
│   (acting) = no confirmed IG    [bracketed] = 2025–26 change-notes    ──▶ = reporting line

■ THE DESIGN — ONE OFFICE, TWO MASTERS
│   Every IG sits INSIDE the agency it audits and reports OUTSIDE it at the same time.
│
│                    ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
│                    │   OFFICE OF INSPECTOR GENERAL    │
│                    └──────────────┬───────────────────┘
│                        ┌──────────┴──────────┐
│           "general supervision"        semiannual reports · 7-day letters
│                        ▼                     ▼
│                  AGENCY HEAD              CONGRESS  (both chambers; oversight
│     (may not prevent or prohibit any       and appropriations committees)

├─◆ THE TWO APPOINTMENT TYPES (roughly half and half)
│   ├─ ★ ESTABLISHMENT IGs (~37) — nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate
│   │     Removal: by the President only · 30 days' written notice to BOTH chambers with a
│   │     case-specific, substantive rationale (Securing IG Independence Act, Dec 2022) ·
│   │     no fixed term · no for-cause protection
│   │     Seats: the 15 Cabinet departments (Treasury has two: Treasury OIG + TIGTA) · EPA ·
│   │     NASA · SSA · GSA · OPM · SBA · NSF · NRC · Intelligence Community (ICIG, 2010) · ...
│   └─ ◇ DESIGNATED FEDERAL ENTITY IGs (~37) — appointed and removable by the entity head
│         Same 30-day notice-to-Congress rule since 2022 · Amtrak · FEC · FCC · FTC · NLRB ·
│         SEC · Peace Corps · Smithsonian · National Archives · CPB · EEOC · Legal Services ·
│         Federal Reserve Board + CFPB (one office, appointed by the Fed Chair) · ...
│
├─◆ THE POWERS (what makes the office more than an internal auditor)
│   ├─ Access to "all records" of the agency ............ § 406(a)(1) · IG Empowerment Act 2016
│   ├─ Subpoena power over documents — not testimony ..... § 406(a)(4)
│   ├─ Law-enforcement authority for criminal agents ...... § 406(f) · refer prosecutions to DOJ
│   ├─ Semiannual report ──▶ agency head ──▶ Congress within 30 days, transmitted UNALTERED
│   ├─ 7-day letter — "particularly serious or flagrant" ──▶ agency head ──▶ Congress in 7 days
│   └─ Whistleblower intake — OIG hotline · a Whistleblower Protection Coordinator in every OIG
│         (retaliation claims run to Office of Special Counsel ──▶ Merit Systems Protection Board)
│

├─◆ THE REMOVAL TRACK — HOW AN IG ACTUALLY LEAVES (statute vs. 2025–26 practice)
│   │
│   │   STATUTE:   President decides ──▶ 30-day written notice to both chambers, with case-
│   │              specific reasons ──▶ removal ──▶ acting IG must be the first assistant or a
│   │              senior official of another OIG (2022 Act) ──▶ nomination ──▶ confirmation
│   │
│   │   PRACTICE:  1/24/25 — 17 IGs removed overnight by e-mail citing "changing priorities";
│   │              no notice, no reasons ──▶ Storch v. Hegseth (D.D.C.) ──▶ 9/24/25 Judge Ana
│   │              Reyes: the firings were UNLAWFUL; reinstatement DENIED (no irreparable harm —
│   │              the President could re-fire them after 30 days' notice)
│   │              ──▶ 19 IGs fired by Mar 2026 [Government Executive count]; acting IGs swapped
│   │              at 5 agencies; ★ vacancies peaked at 39 [CREW]; 28 ★ seats open in Oct 2025
│   │              with nominations pending for only 5
│   │              ──▶ fill-rate: 5 confirmed en bloc 53–43 (Dec 2025) · 8 total by Apr 2026 ·
│   │              DOJ in Aug 2026 makes at least 9 — most had served in a Trump administration
│   │              ──▶ FY27 budget request: Cabinet OIGs −12% vs FY24 on average; Interior and
│   │              DOJ OIGs −28%; staffing −9% projected on top of −10% in 2025 [Partnership for
│   │              Public Service] · IG workforce down ~12% overall [Public Citizen, Mar 2026]
│   │
│   └─ THE PAYOFF: the 30-day rule is a NOTICE requirement, not a CAUSE requirement. The court
│         found the violation and supplied no remedy; the emptied seats were refilled from inside
│         the administration; the council's money now runs through OMB one quarter at a time.
│         The office survives with the same statute, a smaller staff, and a different master.
