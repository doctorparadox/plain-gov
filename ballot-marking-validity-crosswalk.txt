BALLOT MARKING VALIDITY CROSSWALK — what counts as a vote, who decides, and why
"a majority of the oval" and "20% of the oval" are both the law  (as of 6 October 2026)
│  Legend:  ▸ tier / column   ◉ instrument   % express numeric threshold
│           ✓ counted   ✗ not counted   ◇ who decides   ⊘ does not exist
│           ⚑ changed for or after Nov 2026   ◈ definitional artifact
│           † primary instrument over tracker
│
│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: THE STANDARD IS WRITTEN FOR A HUMAN AND THE FIRST READER
│  IS A MACHINE. One state regulates the machine's threshold; the rest leave it to
│  the vendor. Read Part A, then Part B before trusting any "what counts as a vote"
│  summary — including the forty-eight-state count in Part E.
│
■ PART A — THE FEDERAL COMMAND, IN FULL
│
├─▸ 52 U.S.C. § 21081(a)(6) — ONE SENTENCE, QUOTED ENTIRE:
│     "Each State shall adopt uniform and nondiscriminatory standards that define
│      what constitutes a vote and what will be counted as a vote for each category
│      of voting system used in the State."
│     ✱ IT COMMANDS THE EXISTENCE OF A RULE AND NOT ITS CONTENT. Compare § 21081(a)(5),
│       which freezes the permitted ERROR RATE by cross-reference to FEC standards
│       "in effect on October 29, 2002" — Congress was willing to fix a number for
│       the machine's accuracy and unwilling to fix one for what the machine is
│       reading. The accuracy standard is quantitative and twenty-four years stale;
│       the validity standard is qualitative and perpetually current.
│     ✱ "FOR EACH CATEGORY OF VOTING SYSTEM" is the sleeper clause. A state running
│       ballot-marking devices in person and hand-marked paper by mail owes TWO
│       standards, and the mark rules are inert as to the first category, because a
│       device-printed ballot cannot be ambiguous. See Part F.
│
├─▸ ⊘ WHAT § 21081(a)(6) DOES NOT REQUIRE: a fill threshold · a list of acceptable
│     marks · uniformity ACROSS states · uniformity between a state's own categories
│     of system · any particular instrument · publication · any review of the
│     standard's adequacy by any federal body. The Election Assistance Commission
│     certifies equipment and does not approve or collect these standards.
│
└─▸ ◇ WHO ENFORCES IT: the Attorney General, by civil action for declaratory or
      injunctive relief  [52 U.S.C. § 21111]. There is no express private right of
      action in Title III. No reported enforcement action against a state's (a)(6)
      standard was located in this build — recorded as [unconfirmed] rather than as
      a finding of absence. See Note 10.
│
■ PART B — THE THRESHOLD, AND THE READER IT IS ADDRESSED TO
│
├─▸ THE MACHINE THRESHOLD, WRITTEN INTO LAW ... ONE STATE
│     GEORGIA ........ % scanners "shall be configured" so that:
│                        ≥20% fill of the target .......... IS a vote
│                        <10% fill of the target .......... is NOT a vote
│                        ≥10% and <20% fill ............... FLAGGED for adjudication
│                      and a flagged ballot counts if, "in the opinion of the vote
│                      review panel, the voter has clearly and without question
│                      indicated the candidate"
│                      [Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 183-1-15-.02, eff. 13 Nov 2003,
│                       last amended 14 Oct 2020; auth. O.C.G.A. §§ 21-2-2, 21-2-31,
│                       21-2-133, 21-2-439, 21-2-483 — promulgated by the
│                       Georgia State Election Board, not the legislature]
│                      ✱ THE ONLY PLACE IN AMERICAN ELECTION LAW FOUND HERE WHERE THE
│                        STATE TELLS THE SCANNER WHAT A VOTE IS. Everywhere else the
│                        state tells a human what a vote is and the scanner decides
│                        which humans ever get asked.
│
├─▸ THE HUMAN THRESHOLD, EXPRESS ... TWO STATES
│     KENTUCKY ....... % "the majority of an oval, box, or space designating a
│                      candidate or question choice is filled in"
│                      [31 KAR 6:030; auth. KRS 117.015(1)(a) AND 52 U.S.C.
│                       § 21081(a)(6) — the rule cites the federal command as its
│                       own authority, which is how most of these were written]
│     FLORIDA ........ % "fills in more than half of the vote target" satisfies the
│                      General Rule; a target filled entirely or more than half is a
│                      valid vote NOTWITHSTANDING stray marks elsewhere
│                      [DS-DE 420, r. 1S-2.027, Fla. Admin. Code]
│
└─▸ ⊘ NO EXPRESS THRESHOLD OF ANY KIND ... THE REST, INCLUDING EVERY LARGE STATE
      BUT FLORIDA AND GEORGIA
      → WASHINGTON ..... marks in the target "shall be counted as valid votes, with
                         the exceptions below"; a mark partly outside counts "if most
                         of the mark is in the target area and intent can clearly be
                         determined"  [WAC 434-261-086]
      → NEW YORK ....... "properly filled in voting position targets" — the word
                         "properly" doing all the work, with no number behind it
                         [9 NYCRR 6210.13]
      → CALIFORNIA ..... "consistently filling in less than the entire voting
                         position target" is valid — an express rule that a partial
                         fill counts and an express refusal to say how partial
                         [2 CCR §§ 20280.1–20284]
      → MINNESOTA ...... "a ballot shall not be rejected for a technical error that
                         does not make it impossible to determine the voter's intent"
                         [Minn. Stat. § 204C.22 subd. 1]
      ✱ SO THE FOUR NUMBERS IN AMERICAN LAW ON THIS SUBJECT ARE 10%, 20%, "MORE THAN
        HALF" AND "A MAJORITY" — a factor of five between the smallest and the
        largest — AND THE TWO SMALL ONES GOVERN A MACHINE WHILE THE TWO LARGE ONES
        GOVERN A PERSON. A 30%-filled oval is a counted vote in Georgia on the
        machine's own reading and is below the stated threshold in Kentucky, where it
        has to qualify instead under one of the alternative-mark clauses in Part C.
│
■ PART C — THE MARK TYPES, AND WHERE THE STATES SPLIT AT THE CENTER
│
├─▸ THE CASE THAT DIVIDES THEM: ONE SMALL MARK INSIDE ONE TARGET, NOTHING ELSE ON
│   THE BALLOT TO COMPARE IT TO
│     WASHINGTON ..... ✓ a mark in the target area is a valid vote, excepting
│                        "obvious stray marks, hesitation marks, parts of written
│                        notes"  [WAC 434-261-086]
│     NEW YORK ....... ✗ "a hesitation mark such as a dot in the voting position
│                        target area shall not be considered a valid mark unless it
│                        is demonstrated that the voter consistently marked his or
│                        her ballot in such a manner"  [9 NYCRR 6210.13]
│     MICHIGAN ....... ✗ a stray mark within the target area is invalid; the official
│                        "must compare disputed marks with other marks on the ballot"
│                        [Bureau of Elections instruction, citing MCL 168.799a(3),
│                         168.803(2)]
│     ✱ ALL THREE RULES RESOLVE THE CASE BY CONSISTENCY, AND THE CASE IS DEFINED BY
│       HAVING NOTHING TO BE CONSISTENT WITH. A single-contest ballot, a ballot where
│       the voter voted once and skipped the rest, and a recount of one contest are
│       all this case. Washington's default is count; New York's and Michigan's
│       default is do not count. That is the whole disagreement, in the modal
│       contested ballot, between three states that all recite voter intent.
│
├─▸ ✓ COUNTED ALMOST EVERYWHERE, WITH ONE RECURRING CONDITION — CONSISTENCY
│     X · CHECK MARK · CROSS · PLUS SIGN · ASTERISK · STAR, inside a single target
│       FLORIDA ...... ✓ if "the voter consistently uses" that mark form
│       KENTUCKY ..... ✓ "an 'X', a check mark, a plus sign, an asterisk, a star, or
│                        any other mark indicating the intent"  — no consistency
│                        condition stated
│       CALIFORNIA ... ✓ "a distinctive mark, such as (X) or (√) or (←), inside the
│                        associated voting position target", consistently placed
│       NEW YORK ..... ✓ "cross mark 'X', a checkmark, circles, completed open arrow,
│                        or any other clear indication of the voter's choice"
│       MICHIGAN ..... ✓ "a cross or checkmark appearing in the predefined target
│                        area is not considered a stray mark and is a valid vote EVEN
│                        IF the voter does not consistently mark the entire ballot"
│                      ✱ MICHIGAN IS THE INVERSION AND IT IS DELIBERATE: a check mark
│                        in the oval needs no consistency, a faint dot in the same
│                        oval is a stray mark and needs the whole ballot. The state
│                        is sorting by the mark's FORM, not by its darkness, which is
│                        the opposite of what the scanner in front of it is doing.
│
├─▸ ✓ THE CIRCLED OR UNDERLINED NAME — the mark a scanner cannot see at all
│       FLORIDA ...... ✓ circling or underlining the target, the candidate's NAME, or
│                        the party abbreviation, applied consistently
│       KENTUCKY ..... ✓ the oval circled or underlined, OR "the candidate's name or
│                        the question choice is circled or underlined"
│       NEW YORK ..... ✓ "circles" among the valid marks
│       MICHIGAN ..... ⊘ not addressed in the bureau instruction located here
│       GEORGIA ...... ⊘ not addressed as a category in r. 183-1-15-.02
│       ✱ A CIRCLED NAME PUTS ZERO DARK PIXELS IN THE TARGET. In the three states
│         above it is unambiguously a vote and in every state it is unreadable by the
│         tabulator, so it is a vote that EXISTS ONLY IF A HUMAN LOOKS — and in a
│         precinct-count jurisdiction the humans who look are looking at the pile the
│         scanner rejected, which this ballot joins as an undervote rather than as an
│         error. See Part D.
│
├─▸ ✓ LINES THROUGH THE TARGET ... FLORIDA and KENTUCKY, in nearly identical words:
│     a diagonal, horizontal or vertical line intersecting two points on the target,
│     without crossing into another target. Two states, one drafting ancestor.
│
├─▸ ✗ THE MARK THAT ENTERS TWO TARGETS
│     FLORIDA ...... ✗ "when a mark enters more than one vote target, it is an
│                      overvote and it cannot be determined whether the voter has
│                      made a definite choice"
│     ✱ THE ONE RULE ON THIS MAP THAT CONVERTS A GENEROUS STANDARD INTO A STRICT ONE.
│       A long check mark or a sweeping circle — exactly the marks Part C counts — is
│       an overvote if it strays a millimetre. The more expressive the permitted
│       mark, the larger the geometric target for failing this test.
│
└─▸ THE CROSSED-OUT OVAL WITH A CORRECTION ELSEWHERE — four rules, four results
      CALIFORNIA ... ✓ "correction tape, strikeover, white-out or generic written
                       note of instruction" is honoured where another choice in the
                       same contest is marked; "a mark crossed out by the voter" is
                       not valid and signals a declined option
      MINNESOTA .... ✓ where two candidates are marked and one mark is partially
                       erased, "a vote shall be counted for the remaining marked
                       candidate"  [§ 204C.22 subd. 11]
      FLORIDA ...... ✗ a strikethrough ALONE is not intent: "a voter's failure to
                       positively indicate voter intent through some type of mark is
                       not a clear indication that the voter has made a definite
                       choice"
      MICHIGAN ..... ⚠ corrections that cause a false tabulator reading are not valid
                       votes and the ballot must be DUPLICATED — the only rule here
                       whose remedy is a new piece of paper rather than a reading
      ✱ CALIFORNIA AND MINNESOTA READ THE SUBTRACTION AS PART OF THE EXPRESSION;
        FLORIDA READS ONLY ADDITIONS. A voter who fills one oval, crosses it out, and
        fills another has cast a vote in California and Minnesota. In Florida the
        crossed-out oval may still be "more than half" filled and therefore a valid
        vote under Part B, which with the second oval makes an overvote — the strict
        and the generous rules interacting to void a contest the voter plainly meant
        to decide.
│
■ PART D — THE WRITE-IN, WHERE THE RULES ARE AT THEIR MOST GENEROUS
│
├─▸ NAME ON THE LINE, TARGET UNMARKED
│     WASHINGTON ..... ✓ "if a name is written on a write-in line, it shall be counted
│                        as a valid write-in vote REGARDLESS of whether the
│                        corresponding target area is marked"
│     NORTH CAROLINA . ✓ write-ins without a filled oval count IN A HAND COUNT if
│                        intent is determinable  [Numbered Memo 2019-07]
│                      ✱ NORTH CAROLINA'S RULE IS CONDITIONED ON THE COUNTING METHOD,
│                        WHICH IS THE PART-A PROBLEM STATED OUTRIGHT BY A STATE: the
│                        same ballot is a vote or not a vote depending on whether a
│                        machine or a person read it.
│
├─▸ A PRINTED CANDIDATE'S NAME WRITTEN INTO THE WRITE-IN LINE
│     WASHINGTON ..... ✓ "shall not be tallied as an overvote and shall be counted as
│                        a valid vote for the printed candidate"
│     NORTH CAROLINA . ✓ duplicate votes for the same candidate count once
│
├─▸ MISSPELLINGS AND PARTIAL NAMES
│     FLORIDA ........ ✓ "even if a voter abbreviates, misspells, uses only the first
│                        or last name, or varies the form of the name"
│     WASHINGTON ..... ✓ "no attempt to vote for a candidate shall be rejected due to
│                        the misspelling of a candidate's name, unless it is
│                        impossible to clearly determine the voter's intent"
│     TEXAS .......... ✓ "if the voter misspells the write-in candidate's name but the
│                        judge can determine the intent, the vote is counted"
│                        [Tex. Elec. Code § 65.007 and SOS Election Advisory 2014-19]
│
└─▸ ✱ THE WRITE-IN RULES ARE THE MOST PERMISSIVE PROVISIONS IN THIS WHOLE AREA OF
      LAW, AND THEY GOVERN THE SMALLEST NUMBER OF BALLOTS. The provisions governing
      the largest number of ballots — the partial fill, the faint mark, the stray dot
      — are the ones with no numbers in them. The legal effort has gone where the
      votes are not.
│
■ PART E — WHERE THE RULE LIVES, WHICH IS THE COLUMN NOBODY PUBLISHES
│
├─▸ ◉ STATUTE — passed by a legislature, changeable only by one
│     MINNESOTA ...... Minn. Stat. § 204C.22, thirteen subdivisions, the most detailed
│                      statutory treatment located: intent "ascertained only from the
│                      face of the ballot" (subd. 2) · uniform non-standard marks
│                      count (subd. 10) · partial erasure (subd. 11) · "slightly
│                      soiled or defaced" no bar (subd. 12) · an IDENTIFYING mark
│                      voids the ENTIRE ballot (subd. 13)
│     TEXAS .......... Tex. Elec. Code § 65.007 — failure to mark "in strict conformity
│                      with the voting instructions does not void the ballot";
│                      overvote exceptions at § 65.007(c)–(d); an individual mark
│                      overrides a straight-party mark
│     NORTH CAROLINA . N.C.G.S. § 163-182.1(a)–(b) — "no official ballot shall be
│                      rejected because of technical errors in marking it, unless it
│                      is impossible to clearly determine the voter's choice"
│
├─▸ ◉ ADMINISTRATIVE RULE — adopted by an agency or board under rulemaking, revisable
│   without a legislature
│     GEORGIA ........ Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. r. 183-1-15-.02  [State Election Board]
│     FLORIDA ........ r. 1S-2.027 / form DS-DE 420  [Department of State]
│     CALIFORNIA ..... 2 CCR §§ 20280.1–20284  [Secretary of State]
│     NEW YORK ....... 9 NYCRR 6210.13  [State Board of Elections]
│     WASHINGTON ..... WAC 434-261-086  [Secretary of State]  ⚑ see below
│     KENTUCKY ....... 31 KAR 6:030  [State Board of Elections]
│
├─▸ ◉ DIRECTIVE, GUIDANCE OR MANUAL — not rulemaking at all
│     MICHIGAN ....... Bureau of Elections instruction, "Determining the Validity of
│                      Ballot Markings" — a PDF on the department's website, citing
│                      MCL 168.799a(3) and 168.803(2) for authority
│     NORTH CAROLINA . Numbered Memo 2019-07, which supplies the operative sub-rules
│                      the statute above does not
│     TEXAS .......... SOS Election Advisory 2014-19, same relationship to § 65.007
│     PENNSYLVANIA ... "Standards for What Constitutes a Vote," adopted 1 July 2003 by
│                      the Voting Standards Development Board constituted under Act
│                      2002-150 § 204, approved by the Secretary of the Commonwealth
│                      and filed 1 August 2003, published in the Pennsylvania
│                      Bulletin  [33 Pa.B., Doc. No. 03-1538]
│                      ✱ A ONE-TIME BOARD, CONVENED TO DISCHARGE § 21081(a)(6), WHOSE
│                        PRODUCT IS A NOTICE IN A BULLETIN FROM 2003. Pennsylvania's
│                        standard for optical scan is "blackening the oval or filling
│                        in the blank space in an arrow or line," and the board
│                        recorded that standards may vary BETWEEN categories of system
│                        while remaining uniform WITHIN each — the Part A clause, read
│                        correctly, twenty-three years ago, in a county-administered
│                        state. Mail voting arrived in 2019 and this map does not
│                        establish what, if anything, replaced the 2003 text for it.
│                        [unconfirmed — see Note 11]
│
├─▸ ◉ NOTHING ... LOUISIANA, the only state with neither statute nor rule  † NCSL
│     ✱ AND THE ABSENCE IS EXPLICABLE RATHER THAN A GAP: Louisiana ran the November
│       2026 election on machines described in its own 2026 legislative record as
│       thirty-five years old and lacking a paper trail. A state whose equipment
│       produces no voter-marked paper has nothing to interpret, and § 21081(a)(6)
│       asks for a standard "for each category of voting system used in the State" —
│       of which it has one, and that one cannot produce an ambiguous mark.
│     ⚑ THE EXEMPTION EXPIRES. Vendor certification reopened in June 2026, selection
│       was targeted for the end of 2026, and deployment begins in some parishes in
│       2027 over a two-to-three-year transition, with auditable paper the one fixed
│       requirement. Louisiana will need this rule before it needs anything else the
│       new system brings, and it has no draft of one on this map.
│
└─▸ ⚑ THE INSTRUMENT TIER IS WHY A STANDARD CAN MOVE IN AN ELECTION YEAR — AND ONE
      DID. Washington filed WSR 26-12-029 on 27 May 2026 amending WAC 434-261-086,
      the statewide standard on what is a vote, expressly for § 21081(a)(6)
      compliance: "races and issues" becomes "offices and measures," "can" becomes
      "may," "easily be discerned" becomes "CLEARLY BE DETERMINED," the write-in and
      overvote exceptions are clarified, and a reference to "the manual" is replaced
      by "this rule." Effective date: 1 JANUARY 2027.
      ✱ SO THE 3 NOVEMBER 2026 ELECTION IN WASHINGTON RUNS ON THE 2018 TEXT
        [WSR 18-10-003, eff. 20 May 2018], AND THE CLARIFIED STANDARD ARRIVES TWO
        MONTHS AFTER THE ELECTION IT CLARIFIES. The substantive edit is the evidentiary
        one — "easily discerned" to "clearly determined" is a different question put to
        a canvassing board — and any 2026 Washington recount or contest is argued under
        the old words while the state's own considered view of the right words is on
        the books and not yet in force.

■ PART F — WHAT NO MARK STANDARD ON THIS MAP REACHES
│
├─  THE REFERRAL DECISION, WHICH IS THE WHOLE BALLGAME. Outside Georgia, no standard
│   here states the pixel-density value at which a tabulator treats a target as
│   marked. The NIST study of marginal marks reports those values are proprietary,
│   that scanners "count the number of dark pixels in a voting target," that "no two
│   sensors are identical and the behavior can further be affected by environmental
│   conditions," and that marginal marks "are neither clearly countable as votes or
│   as non-votes by optical mark scanners." Its five marginal categories — check
│   marks, crosses, partially filled, lightly filled, overfilled — are exactly the
│   categories Part C legislates for the human and nobody legislates for the machine.
│
├─  THE BALLOT-MARKING DEVICE, BY ITS OWN NATURE. A device-printed ballot cannot be
│   ambiguously marked, so every rule above is inert as to it, and the questions that
│   replace them are different in kind: whether the tabulator reads the printed names
│   or the barcode, and whether the voter checked the printout. North Carolina's memo
│   answers the first — "the printed names must always be used in counting the votes,
│   rather than coordinates or barcodes" — because that is what the voter verified.
│   See Ballot Marking Device and Hand-Marked Paper Ballot.
│
├─  UNIFORMITY ACROSS STATES, WHICH NOTHING REQUIRES AND BUSH v. GORE DID NOT
│   REACH. The holding was that a recount run under a bare intent standard with no
│   sub-rules offends equal protection WITHIN a state. Part C is that problem one
│   level up and outside its reach: a faint dot in an oval is a vote in Washington and
│   not a vote in New York, and no federal instrument has anything to say about it.
│
├─  ⊘ FEDERAL REVIEW OF ADEQUACY. Nobody grades these standards. The EAC certifies
│   equipment; it does not collect, publish or approve the fifty definitions, and the
│   § 21111 civil action is the only lever. A state satisfies (a)(6) by having
│   adopted something.
│
├─  THE ACCEPTANCE DECISION, AGAIN. Every rule here governs a ballot already in the
│   count. Whether the envelope was opened at all — signature, deadline, cure — is
│   upstream and untouched, exactly as Recount Trigger Crosswalk found for the
│   recount. A mark standard cannot reach a ballot nobody read.
│
└─  THE ONE PLACE THE HUMAN STANDARD GOVERNS EVERYTHING: THE FULL HAND COUNT. In a
    hand recount or a hand-count audit there is no scanner and no referral, so Part C
    applies to every ballot rather than to the rejected pile — which is why Georgia's
    SB 3EX full statewide hand recount for eight offices at 0.5% is the one 2026
    procedure in which a state's mark rules decide a whole election rather than a
    remainder. Georgia's hand-count teams apply r. 183-1-15-.02's "clearly and
    without question" standard, and its 20% machine threshold becomes irrelevant at
    the moment it matters most.
