HOW THE CENSUS APPORTIONS CONGRESS — ENUMERATION TO SEATS
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path   └─▶ branch/attack surface   ◇ decision   ★ operative   ✕ closed
│   Time-slice: law as of Sept 1, 2026 · dates shown are the 2030 cycle
│
■ 0. THE FLOOR — TWO NUMBERS THAT DO NOT MOVE, ONE THAT DOES
│     Art. I, § 2, cl. 3 — an "actual Enumeration" every ten years, "in such Manner as
│     they shall by Law direct." THE POWER IS CONGRESS'S. Congress handed the count to
│     the Secretary of Commerce by statute (13 U.S.C. § 141(a)). The President appears
│     exactly once in this pipeline, at step 5, as a courier.
│       ├─ 14th Am., § 2 — seats apportioned "counting the whole number of persons in
│       │    each State." Superseded the three-fifths clause. The only textual carve-out,
│       │    "Indians not taxed," has had no practical effect for a century.
│       │
│       ├─ ★ FIXED #1 — 435 SEATS. This is a STATUTE, not the Constitution. Capped at
│       │    435 by the Apportionment Act of 1911 and made permanent — the size frozen and
│       │    reapportionment made automatic — by the Permanent Apportionment Act of 1929.
│       │    A simple majority of Congress could change it tomorrow.
│       ├─ ★ FIXED #2 — ONE SEAT PER STATE MINIMUM. This one IS constitutional.
│       ├─ ✕ NOT IN THE POOL — D.C., Puerto Rico, and the territories. No voting seats,
│       │    so their residents are counted but not apportioned. (D.C. gets 3 electoral
│       │    votes under the 23rd Am.; that is a separate mechanism.)
│       └─ ◆ WHAT MOVES — which states gain and lose. Nothing else in this block does.
│
├─◆ 1. WHO COUNTS, AND WHERE — the residence rule does more work than any other step
│     │    APPORTIONMENT POPULATION = resident population + overseas federal employees
│     │    allocated back to a home state. That sum, and only that sum, enters the formula.
│     ├─ ★ USUAL RESIDENCE — where a person lives and sleeps most of the time. Not
│     │    citizenship. Not voter eligibility. Not age. Newborns count; noncitizens count.
│     ├─ GROUP QUARTERS — counted where the facility is:
│     │      ├─ prisons ...... at the prison, not the last home address
│     │      │     (the origin of the prison-gerrymandering problem downstream)
│     │      ├─ college dorms  at the school
│     │      └─ nursing homes · shelters · barracks ... at the facility
│     ├─ OVERSEAS — military and civilian federal employees and dependents living with
│     │    them, allocated to a home state from employer administrative records. Private
│     │    citizens living abroad are NOT counted at all.
│     │
│     └─▶ ◇ ATTACK SURFACE #1 — CHANGE WHO IS IN THE BASE
│            ├─ 2019: an EO directed agencies to build citizenship data for the 2020 count
│            ├─ Jul 2020: a presidential memo ordered persons without lawful status
│            │     excluded from the apportionment base — the first such attempt ever
│            ├─ Jan 2021: EO 13986 revoked both and restored the whole-persons rule
│            ├─ Jan 20, 2025: EO 14148 revoked EO 13986 [live — no replacement policy
│            │     has been issued, so the operative rule is the statute, not an EO]
│            └─ ✕ NEVER ADJUDICATED ON THE MERITS. The Court blocked the 2020 citizenship
│                 QUESTION as pretextual but has never ruled on whether the President may
│                 exclude anyone from the apportionment BASE. That door is untested, not shut.
│
├─◆ 2. THE QUESTION CLOCK — the deadlines that decide 2030, four years early
│     │    13 U.S.C. § 141(f). Whatever is on the form must clear Congress first, in writing.
│     ├─ ★ SUBJECTS REPORT — due 3 years before the census date ....... by Apr 1, 2027
│     ├─ ★ QUESTIONS REPORT — due 2 years before the census date ...... by Apr 1, 2028
│     ├─ [live] THIS IS THE REAL 2030 DEADLINE. An immigration-status question that is
│     │    not in the subjects report is a question with a statutory problem before it has
│     │    a constitutional one. Seven months out as of this build.
│     └─ ◇ and the reverse: a question reported in 2027 can still be pulled by a later
│            administration or by Congress before the forms are printed in 2029
│
├─◆ 3. THE COUNT ITSELF  [census date: Mon Apr 1, 2030]
│     ├─ address canvassing ──▶ self-response (internet · phone · paper)
│     │      ──▶ NONRESPONSE FOLLOWUP (enumerators in the field)
│     │      ──▶ administrative records and imputation for what remains
│     ├─ ✕ SAMPLING IS BARRED FOR APPORTIONMENT. 13 U.S.C. § 195 permits statistical
│     │    sampling everywhere in Title 13 EXCEPT the apportionment determination, and the
│     │    Court so held in 1999. Sampling remains lawful for redistricting data and
│     │    funding formulas — the split that makes the apportionment number the least
│     │    correctable number the Bureau produces.
│     ├─ ✓ IMPUTATION IS NOT SAMPLING. Utah v. Evans (2002) upheld hot-deck imputation
│     │    for the counts that fed apportionment — a distinction of a few thousand people
│     │    and, that year, one House seat.
│     ├─ POST-ENUMERATION SURVEY — measures the differential undercount AFTER the fact.
│     │      ★ It never changes the apportionment number. It is a report card, not a fix.
│     └─ DISCLOSURE AVOIDANCE — noise injected into PUBLISHED tables to protect
│            confidentiality. State apportionment totals are published as enumerated.
│            [live: a 2026 Commerce order changed the method; the fight is over the
│             granularity of race, ethnicity, and small-area data, not the seat math]
│
├─◆ 4. ★ TABULATION → SECRETARY → PRESIDENT  [due Tue Dec 31, 2030]
│     │    13 U.S.C. § 141(b) — the tabulation of total population by state, "as required
│     │    for the apportionment," completed within 9 MONTHS of the census date and
│     │    reported by the Secretary of Commerce to the President.
│     └─ ◆ THIS IS WHERE JUDICIAL REVIEW ATTACHES. The Secretary is an agency head doing
│            a statutory act. Everything after this step is much harder to sue over.
│
╞═════════ 5. ★ THE MATH — METHOD OF EQUAL PROPORTIONS (2 U.S.C. § 2a) ══════════════════
│     Adopted 1941, used after every census since 1940. Also called Huntington-Hill.
│     The ONLY inputs are the fifty state numbers from step 4. There is no discretion here.
│
│     STEP A ──▶ every state gets 1 seat first .......... 50 assigned · 385 remain
│     STEP B ──▶ for each state, compute a PRIORITY VALUE for its nth seat:
│                     priority(n) = state population ÷ √(n × (n−1))
│                (the divisor is the geometric mean of the seat it has and the seat it wants)
│     STEP C ──▶ rank every priority value nationwide, largest to smallest
│     STEP D ──▶ ★ the top 385 values take the remaining seats. Done.
│
│     WHY THE MARGINS ARE THE STORY — 2020 result, for scale:
│       ├─ average district after 2020 ........... 761,169 people (up 7.1% from 2010)
│       ├─ gained ... TX +2 · CO, FL, MT, NC, OR +1        (7 seats moved)
│       ├─ lost ..... CA, IL, MI, NY, OH, PA, WV −1
│       └─ ★ THE 435TH SEAT WENT TO MINNESOTA. NEW YORK MISSED IT BY 89 PEOPLE.
│              A differential undercount of one mid-sized apartment block is a seat.
│
├─◆ 6. ★ THE PRESIDENT TRANSMITS  [within 1 week of the new session opening — Jan 2031]
│     │    2 U.S.C. § 2a(a) — a statement to Congress showing the whole number of persons
│     │    in each state and the number of Representatives each is entitled to.
│     ├─ ✕ FRANKLIN v. MASSACHUSETTS (1992) — the President is not an "agency," so the
│     │    transmittal is not final agency action reviewable under the APA. The courthouse
│     │    door at THIS step is closed; it was open one step earlier.
│     └─▶ ◇ THE PRESIDENT TRANSMITS A NUMBER OTHER THAN THE WHOLE NUMBER OF PERSONS
│            ├─ tried once, in 2020, by presidential memorandum
│            ├─ ✕ Trump v. New York (2020) — vacated as UNRIPE and premature. The Court
│            │     never reached whether it may be done. The 2020 count ran late, the
│            │     term ended, and the successor revoked the memo. The question survives.
│            └─ ⚠ the combination is the structural hazard: an act with no APA review at
│                 step 6, resting on a question the Court has twice declined to answer
│
├─◆ 7. ★ THE CLERK CERTIFIES TO THE STATES  [within 15 days of receipt]
│     ├─ 2 U.S.C. § 2a(b) — the Clerk of the House sends each governor the number of
│     │    Representatives that state is entitled to. Ministerial. No discretion.
│     └─ 2 U.S.C. § 2a(c) — the DEFAULT RULES if a state has not redistricted in time:
│            a state that gained seats elects the extra members AT LARGE; a state that
│            lost seats elects its whole delegation at large. Rarely reached — but it is
│            the reason a state legislature that misses its own deadline still has a map.
│
└─◆ 8. ──▶ HANDOFF TO REDISTRICTING
      ├─ 13 U.S.C. § 141(c) — block-level tabulations (the P.L. 94-171 file) go to each
      │    governor and redistricting body WITHIN ONE YEAR of the census date [Apr 2031]
      └─ ──▶ from here the process is fifty separate state processes. See the redistricting map.

╞═════════════════ THE THREE LEVERS — EVERY FIGHT IS ONE OF THESE ════════════════════════
│
├─◆ LEVER 1 — CHANGE WHO IS COUNTED  (attacks step 1)
│     ├─ an immigration-status or citizenship question on the form
│     ├─ excluding a class of residents from the apportionment base after the count
│     └─ ◇ the constitutional hook is the phrase "whole number of persons," and it has
│            never been construed by the Court in this posture
│
├─◆ LEVER 2 — CHANGE HOW WELL THEY ARE COUNTED  (attacks step 3, and leaves no fingerprints)
│     ├─ outreach funding · field-office count · length of the response window ·
│     │    a question that suppresses response in specific communities
│     ├─ ★ THE UNDERCOUNT IS NOT RANDOM. It concentrates in the same populations every
│     │    decade, so degrading accuracy has a predictable direction.
│     └─ ✕ and there is no correction downstream — sampling is barred at step 3 and the
│            post-enumeration survey only measures the error it cannot fix
│
└─◆ LEVER 3 — CHANGE THE NUMBER OF SEATS  (attacks step 0)
      ├─ 435 is an ordinary statute. Enlarging the House is the one lever that requires
      │    no constitutional argument at all — and the one nobody with power wants.
      ├─ ✕ THE MID-DECADE ROUTE IS CLOSED BY STATUTE. 13 U.S.C. § 141(d) authorizes a
      │    mid-decade census in years ending in 5 — but § 141(e)(2) says its information
      │    "shall not be used for apportionment of Representatives in Congress among the
      │    several States, nor ... in prescribing congressional districts." An off-cycle
      │    recount can be run. It cannot move a seat without Congress amending that clause.
      └─ ◇ a bill could do all of this — change the base, the size, or the timing — which
             is why the live proposals are legislative and not merely executive

╞═════════════════════════════ FIVE STRUCTURAL FACTS ═════════════════════════════════════
│
│  1. THE FORMULA IS THE ONLY HONEST STEP, WHICH IS WHY EVERY FIGHT IS UPSTREAM OF IT.
│       Steps 5 through 7 have no discretion in them at all. Every contested decision
│       happens at steps 1, 2, and 3 — who is in the base, what is on the form, and how
│       hard anyone tries to find people.
│
│  2. THE POWER IS CONGRESS'S AND HAS BEEN DELEGATED, NOT SURRENDERED.
│       Article I gives the enumeration to Congress "in such Manner as they shall by Law
│       direct." Everything the executive does here — the count, the method, the deadlines,
│       the ban on sampling — it does because a statute says so, and Congress can rewrite
│       any of it by simple majority.
│
│  3. THE APPORTIONMENT NUMBER IS THE LEAST CORRECTABLE NUMBER THE GOVERNMENT PRODUCES.
│       Sampling is barred, the post-enumeration survey is diagnostic only, and no statute
│       provides for revising an apportionment once transmitted. An error found in 2032
│       is an error the country lives with until 2033.
│
│  4. THE REVIEW WINDOW IS ONE STEP WIDE.
│       The Secretary's report is agency action and reviewable. The President's transmittal
│       is not (Franklin). The Clerk's certification is ministerial. Anyone who wants a
│       court to look at an apportionment has to be in front of one before December 31.
│
│  5. 435 IS NOT IN THE CONSTITUTION.
│       The cap is a 1911 statute made permanent in 1929, and the average district has
│       grown from about 210,000 people to about 761,000 since. Every zero-sum state-vs-
│       state fight in this map exists because one number stopped moving in 1929.
