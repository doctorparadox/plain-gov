POST-ELECTION AUDIT CROSSWALK — the four designs, and the two columns that decide
whether any of it binds                                      (as of 4 October 2026)
│  Legend:  ▸ design / column   ⚡ trigger   → what it checks   ⏱ timing vs. the
│           certificate   ⚖ does the finding bind   ⊘ duty with no remedy
│           ⚑ changed for November 2026   ◈ count is a definitional artifact
│
│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: all 51 jurisdictions audit, and in most of them the audit
│  cannot change anything. TIMING AND TEETH ARE INDEPENDENT — see Part C.
│
■ PART A — THE FOUR DESIGNS  (what is actually counted)
│
├─▸ 1. TRADITIONAL FIXED-PERCENTAGE ............ 36 states + DC   [the modal design]
│     ⚡ the statute, automatically — no margin condition, no official's discretion
│     → hand-count a FIXED share of precincts, machines or ballots drawn at random,
│       and compare that hand tally against the machine tally for the same units
│     → the share is written in advance and does NOT move with the margin:
│       1% (Arizona, New York) · 3% (West Virginia) · 5% (Alaska) · 10% (Hawaii,
│       Connecticut)
│     ✱ THE DEFECT IS ARITHMETIC, NOT POLITICAL. A fixed percentage is wasted work
│       in a landslide and far too little in a close race — the only case where the
│       answer could change an outcome. A 1% sample does not resolve a 0.2% margin
│       at any confidence, and no amount of good faith fixes that.
│     ⚖ mostly advisory; the escalation thresholds that give some of them teeth are
│       in Part D
│
├─▸ 2. RISK-LIMITING AUDIT (RLA) ............... ◈ 7 require · 6–9 permit or pilot
│     ⚡ the statute, and then the MARGIN sets the workload rather than a fixed share
│     → draw paper ballots at random and keep drawing until the evidence reaches a
│       stated confidence that the reported winner really won — the RISK LIMIT, the
│       maximum chance a wrong outcome survives the audit uncorrected
│     → if that confidence is never reached, the audit ESCALATES TO A FULL HAND COUNT
│       by its own logic, with no official deciding to escalate it
│     → three methods:  BALLOT-POLLING (tally a sample, test the lead)
│                       BALLOT-COMPARISON (match each sampled paper ballot to the
│                         system's record of that ballot — needs a cast vote record)
│                       BATCH-COMPARISON (hand-count whole batches against subtotals)
│     ⚑ risk limits are not uniform and are moving: Georgia set SIX PERCENT for 2026,
│       dropping to five percent or less thereafter  [SB 3EX, 2026 special session]
│     ✱ THIS IS THE ONLY DESIGN ON THE MAP WHOSE WORKLOAD SCALES WITH THE STAKES,
│       and the only one that can reach a full hand count without anybody's consent.
│     ◈ the require/permit counts are contested — see Notes 2 and 3
│
├─▸ 3. PROCEDURAL AUDIT ........................ 7 states
│       Indiana · Michigan · Mississippi · Nebraska · New Hampshire · Oklahoma ·
│       South Carolina
│     ⚡ the statute, on a fixed cycle
│     → checks whether the PROCESS was followed: forms completed, seals logged,
│       reconciliation performed, chain-of-custody paperwork present
│     → DOES NOT RECOUNT VOTES and is not designed to detect a wrong tally
│     ✱ A PROCEDURAL AUDIT IS AN AUDIT OF THE PAPERWORK ABOUT THE BALLOTS, NOT OF
│       THE BALLOTS. It is a real control — it is how a broken seal or a missed
│       reconciliation surfaces — but a state with only this design has no
│       statutory procedure that compares paper to machine at all.
│     ⚖ advisory by construction; there is no tally for a finding to correct
│
└─▸ 4. FULL HAND COUNT ......................... not a sampling design at all
      ⚡ in most states, a RECOUNT trigger or a candidate's request — not an audit
      ⚑ → GEORGIA MADE IT AN AUTOMATIC STATEWIDE DUTY FOR NOVEMBER 2026. For eight
        statewide offices (Governor · Lieutenant Governor · Secretary of State ·
        Attorney General · State School Superintendent · Insurance Commissioner ·
        Agriculture Commissioner · Labor Commissioner), where the office appears
        FIRST OR SECOND on the ballot and the margin is 0.5% OR LESS, the Secretary
        of State MUST order a full hand recount of every ballot cast statewide,
        replacing the machine recount counties have used. Counties finishing within
        17 days are reimbursed by the state, and THE HAND-COUNTED RESULT BECOMES
        THE OFFICIAL ONE.  [SB 3EX, signed June 2026]
      ✱ THE DESIGN THE AUDIT MOVEMENT'S OPPONENTS PREFER, AND THE ONE STATISTICIANS
        TRUST LEAST. A full hand count is slower and less accurate per ballot than a
        well-run sample, and it returns the count to rooms full of partisan
        observers. Georgia's 2026 version is narrow and margin-gated, which is why
        it reads as a recount rule rather than as the hand-count-everything demand
        the denial movement has pressed since 2020.
│
■ PART B — COLUMN ONE: TIMING AGAINST THE CERTIFICATE
│
│  The question the certification map raises and does not answer. An audit that
│  reports after the certificate is issued is reporting about a result that is
│  already, in most states, the operative one.
│
├─▸ BEFORE CERTIFICATION .................... 31 states + DC
│     Alaska · Arizona · California · Colorado · Connecticut · Georgia · Hawaii ·
│     Idaho · Illinois · Indiana · Kansas · Kentucky · Maryland · Massachusetts ·
│     Minnesota · Missouri · Montana · Nevada · New Jersey · New York ·
│     North Dakota · Oregon · Pennsylvania · Rhode Island · South Carolina ·
│     Tennessee · Utah · Virginia · Washington · West Virginia · Wyoming
│     ⏱ the audit is a PRECONDITION of the certificate, and in the strongest
│       versions the canvass can be held open until the audit finishes
│
├─▸ AFTER CERTIFICATION ..................... 9 states
│     Arkansas · Delaware · Florida · Iowa · New Hampshire · New Mexico · Ohio ·
│     Vermont · Wisconsin
│     ⏱ the certificate issues first; the audit reports into a settled result
│     ⚠ [Wisconsin is listed here by NCSL and BEFORE by Verified Voting — its
│        timing is set by COMMISSION POLICY rather than statute. See Note 4.]
│
└─▸ NO SPECIFIED DEADLINE ................... 10 states
      Alabama · Louisiana · Maine · Michigan · Mississippi · Nebraska ·
      North Carolina · Oklahoma · South Dakota · Texas
      ⊘ the state commands an audit and names no date by which it must be done,
        which means nothing in the statute prevents it from landing after the
        certificate — or, in principle, long after
      ⚑ [Texas's 2026 risk-limiting audit is scheduled for 19–20 NOVEMBER 2026, and
         the hand-count audit that follows it must begin no later than the first
         business day after the Secretary of State certifies the RLA complete and
         finish by the 30th day after the election. The sequence is pinned to the
         ELECTION DATE, not to the certificate.]
      ◈ [NCSL's own prose labels these buckets 10-after and 9-no-deadline while its
         lists name 9 and 10. The lists are used here. See Note 1.]
│
■ PART C — COLUMN TWO: TEETH, AND THE FOUR QUADRANTS
│
│  ✱ THE FINDING. Timing does not predict teeth. These are two independent columns,
│    and all four combinations exist in the United States right now. The worked
│    example in each quadrant is given with its citation; the quadrant populations
│    are not published anywhere and are NOT asserted here.
│
├─▸ QUADRANT 1 — BEFORE + BINDING .............. the audit as an actual check
│     ▪ COLORADO .............. Colo. Rev. Stat. § 1-7-515; 8 CCR 1505-1:25
│         → statewide BALLOT-COMPARISON RLA; the audit unit is the INDIVIDUAL BALLOT
│         → target contests selected by the Secretary of State by the Friday after
│           election day; the RANDOM SEED is drawn at a PUBLIC MEETING on day 13
│         ⏱ county audit boards must report by the day before the canvass deadline,
│           and the Secretary of State MAY DIRECT COUNTIES TO DELAY THE CANVASS
│           until the audit is done
│         ⚖ the audit runs until the risk limit is met OR A FULL HAND COUNT RESULTS;
│           where it produces a recount, THE RECOUNT RESULT IS BINDING
│         ✱ the only design on this map where the certificate waits on the evidence
│     ▪ MARYLAND ............. Md. Code, Elec. Law § 11-309  [HB 426 / SB 313, 2025]
│         → RLA of at least one statewide contest after every statewide election
│         ⏱ must be COMPLETED BEFORE certification by the Board of State Canvassers
│         ⚖ BINDING: on a discrepancy the state administrator must direct the
│           relevant board of canvassers to investigate immediately "to determine an
│           accurate election result"
│         ⚑ newest mandate on the map — enacted 2025, so November 2026 is the first
│           federal general election it governs
│
├─▸ QUADRANT 2 — BEFORE + ADVISORY ............. good clock, no remedy
│     ▪ WISCONSIN ............ Wis. Stat. § 7.08(6)
│         → ~10% of REPORTING UNITS drawn at random, at least one audit in each of
│           the 72 counties, at least five units per type of voting equipment;
│           three contests drawn at random plus the top-of-ballot contest
│         → the audit measures VOTING-SYSTEM ERROR RATE, which is what it was
│           written to measure
│         ⚖ "not binding upon election results and cannot be expanded to a full
│           recount." On a discrepancy the commission may ask the EQUIPMENT
│           MANUFACTURER to investigate.
│         ⊘ the finding has no addressee who can act on the result
│         ✱ A STATE CAN DO THE AUDIT AT THE RIGHT TIME AND STILL BUILD NO REMEDY.
│
├─▸ QUADRANT 3 — AFTER + BINDING ............... remedy with no clock
│     ▪ OHIO ................. Ohio Rev. Code § 3505.331; Election Official Manual
│                             ch. 11 § 11.03
│         → choice of design: RLA, a 5%-of-ballots percentage audit, or an
│           alternative approved by the Secretary of State; at least three contests
│         ⏱ begins NO SOONER than 6 business days and NO LATER than 21 days AFTER
│           the official certification of results
│         ⚖ escalate if county accuracy is under 99.5% at margins of 1% or more, or
│           under 99.8% at margins below 1%
│         ⚖ AND THE CERTIFICATE IS NOT FINAL AGAINST IT: where the audit differs
│           from the certified result, "the official certified results are to be
│           amended to reflect the results of the audit"
│         ✱ THIS IS THE BLOCK THAT BREAKS THE PREMISE OF THIS MAP. Ohio audits after
│           the certificate and can move it anyway, because the statute wrote an
│           AMENDMENT MECHANISM. Timing was never the load-bearing column — the
│           existence of a mechanism to change the number is.
│
└─▸ QUADRANT 4 — NO DEADLINE + ADVISORY ........ the modal American audit
      → a fixed-percentage or procedural audit, on no stated deadline, whose finding
        is reported and filed
      ⊘ no clock, no amendment mechanism, no escalation threshold, no addressee
      ✱ THE HONEST SUMMARY OF THE SYSTEM: the universal post-election audit law is
        real, and in the typical state it produces a document rather than a remedy.
│
■ PART D — THE REMAINING COLUMNS  (where the variance actually lives)
│
├─▸ WHO PICKS THE CONTEST, AND WHETHER THE METHOD IS PUBLIC
│     → the Secretary of State alone — the most common design
│     → county or local boards, drawing their own random selection (Ohio)
│     → a PUBLIC RANDOM SEED drawn in a noticed meeting (Colorado, day 13), the
│       strongest transparency design on the map because the sample can be
│       recomputed by anyone afterward
│     ⚑ → GEORGIA 2026: one additional audited contest is chosen by MAJORITY VOTE OF
│       FIVE OFFICIALS — the Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Speaker of the House,
│       Senate Minority Leader and House Minority Leader — on the Thursday after
│       election day, and if they do not agree in time the chair of the State
│       Election Board decides alone.  [SB 3EX, 2026]
│     ✱ A BIPARTISAN FIVE-MEMBER PANEL WITH A SINGLE-OFFICIAL FALLBACK IS A
│       DEADLOCK-TO-DISCRETION DESIGN. Failing to agree is how the choice moves to
│       one person, and nothing requires anyone to agree.
│
├─▸ WHAT GETS AUDITED
│     → the TOP-OF-BALLOT contest, always, in nearly every design
│     ⚑ → Georgia 2026 expanded the audited set from the top contest plus one to the
│       top contest PLUS President, U.S. Senate and U.S. House where applicable
│       PLUS Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State and Attorney General
│       PLUS one more chosen as above  [SB 3EX, 2026]
│     → three contests minimum (Ohio, Wisconsin — Wisconsin's three drawn at random)
│     → at least one statewide contest (Maryland)
│     ⊘ DOWN-BALLOT AND LOCAL CONTESTS ARE THE UNAUDITED MAJORITY of races on an
│       American ballot, in every state, under every design on this map
│
├─▸ WHAT THE AUDIT UNIT IS — and it decides how much work the audit is
│     → the INDIVIDUAL BALLOT ...... ballot-comparison; needs a cast vote record
│     → the BATCH ................. batch-comparison; coarser, no CVR needed
│     → the PRECINCT or REPORTING UNIT ... the fixed-percentage designs
│     → the MACHINE ............... the device-level audits
│
└─▸ THE ESCALATION THRESHOLDS  (where a fixed-percentage audit grows teeth)
      → WEST VIRGINIA ... a discrepancy over 1% triggers a FULL HAND RECOUNT
      → MINNESOTA ....... a difference over 0.5% EXPANDS the audit's scope
      → OHIO ............ accuracy under 99.5% (or 99.8% in a close race) requires a
                          SECOND audit sample
      ✱ THESE ARE THE THREE SHAPES A THRESHOLD CAN TAKE — jump straight to a full
        count, widen the sample, or draw again — and a state with none of them has
        written an audit that reports a discrepancy and stops.
│
■ PART E — WHAT NO AUDIT ON THIS MAP REACHES
│
├─  ELIGIBILITY. Every design audits the TALLY OF BALLOTS THAT WERE COUNTED. None
│   audits whether the right ballots were counted — the acceptance decisions on
│   mail and provisional ballots sit upstream and are reviewed, if at all, by a
│   different procedure in a different statute.
│
├─  THE CHAIN OF CUSTODY. An audit presumes the paper in the box is the paper that
│   came out of the machine. That presumption is the subject of a different map, and
│   where it fails, a clean audit is a clean audit of the wrong objects.
│
├─  BALLOTS NEVER CAST. Nothing in an audit speaks to the voter turned away, the
│   mail ballot that did not arrive, or the registration cancelled in August.
│
├─  THE DOWN-BALLOT MAJORITY. See Part D; it is not a gap in one state's design, it
│   is the design everywhere.
│
└─  FEDERAL LAW. ⊘ THERE IS NO FEDERAL POST-ELECTION AUDIT REQUIREMENT. The Help
    America Vote Act regulates the voting SYSTEM — Title III standards, the paper
    record, the error-rate ceiling — and commands no audit of any result. The
    Election Assistance Commission publishes audit guidance and runs a voluntary
    standards program. The entire audit layer of American elections is state law,
    which is why this is a crosswalk and not an org chart.
