HOW AN ACTING OFFICIAL IS INSTALLED                                  (as of 26 October 2026)
│  ──▶ main path  └─▶ branch  ■ stage  ◆ instrument  ◇ decision  ⊘ foreclosed by statute
│  ◎ the hinge: the Act stops and something else decides   ☐ notice owed to Congress
│  ✕ a check that is not one   ⚑ a duty with no due date   ★ used or decided in 2025-26
│  ◈ a count or reading made here, published nowhere      Offices here are plain text.
│  Section numbers are 5 U.S.C. unless marked otherwise.
│
■ STAGE 0 · IS THIS OFFICE INSIDE THE ACT AT ALL?            (asked before anything else)
│
│   The Act reaches an office in an Executive agency that requires appointment by the
│   President by and with the advice and consent of the Senate.  Three provisions take
│   an office back out again, and each one is a different kind of exit.
│
│   ⊘ § 3349c · OUT OF §§ 3345-3349b ENTIRELY.  No acting rules, no clock, no report:
│       (1) a member of a multimember board, commission or similar entity, appointed
│           with the Senate's consent, that governs an independent establishment or a
│           Government corporation
│       (2) a Commissioner of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission
│       (3) a member of the Surface Transportation Board
│       (4) a judge appointed with the Senate's consent to an Article I court
│
│   ⊘ § 3347(a)(1) · A MORE SPECIFIC STATUTE DISPLACES THE ACT, where that statute
│       expressly (A) authorises the President, a court, or the head of an Executive
│       department to designate someone to act temporarily, or (B) names the person.
│       ★ 28 U.S.C. § 546 is the live example, and the one place the two regimes have
│         been argued against each other in 2025-26.
│
│   ⊘ § 3347(a)(2) · A RECESS APPOINTMENT is not governed by this Act at all.
│       Largely closed in practice since the pro-forma-session holding of 2014.
│
│   ◎ § 3347(b) · AND THE ONE THING THAT IS NOT AN EXIT.  A statute that merely gives
│     an agency head general power to delegate statutory duties, or to reassign them
│     among agency personnel, is NOT a § 3347(a)(1) exception.  One sentence, and it
│     is the whole battleground of 2025-26.
│
■ STAGE A · THE TRIGGER                                        (what starts the clock)
│
│   ◆ death      ◆ resignation      ◆ "otherwise unable to perform the functions and
│                                     duties of the office"
│   ◆ § 3345(c)(2) · THE EXPIRY OF A TERM is deemed an inability.  A term that simply
│     ran out starts the same clock as a chair someone walked out of.
│   ⊘ § 3346(a) · A vacancy caused by SICKNESS has no time limit at all.
│
│   ──▶ The clock attaches to the VACANCY, not to the person filling it.  Replacing one
│       acting official with another restarts nothing.
│
■ STAGE B · THE THREE DOORS (§ 3345(a))                   (only one opens by itself)
│
├─◆ DOOR 1 · THE FIRST ASSISTANT — § 3345(a)(1)
│    Performs the duties on an acting basis automatically, with no presidential act.
│    ⚑ The Act never defines "first assistant."  The agency defines it, in a document
│      the agency writes and can rewrite.
│    ✕ Only the first assistant IN PLACE WHEN THE VACANCY AROSE takes the seat this
│      way.  Being moved into the post afterwards is not the same thing.
│
├─◆ DOOR 2 · ANOTHER SENATE-CONFIRMED OFFICER — § 3345(a)(2)
│    The President ALONE may direct any person already holding a different office
│    filled by appointment with the Senate's consent.  No subject-matter limit: a
│    confirmed officer of one agency may be directed into a vacancy in another.
│
└─◆ DOOR 3 · A SENIOR EMPLOYEE OF THAT SAME AGENCY — § 3345(a)(3)
     The President ALONE may direct, notwithstanding Door 1, an officer or employee of
     that agency who meets BOTH tests:
       ◈ SERVICE · at least 90 days in a position in that agency during the 365 days
                   before the death, resignation or onset of inability
       ◈ PAY     · that position paid at or above the minimum rate for GS-15
     ⚑ Neither test asks what the person did.  Both ask only where they sat and what
       the seat paid, which is why a 90-day parking place qualifies as well as a
       career appointment does.
│
■ STAGE C · THE DISQUALIFIER (§ 3345(b))
│
│   ◇ Has the President submitted THIS PERSON'S nomination for THIS office?
│     │
│     ├─ no  ──▶ carry on to the clock.
│     │
│     └─ yes ──▶ ⊘ the person MAY NOT ACT — unless they served as the office's first
│                  assistant for at least 90 of the preceding 365 days, or hold the
│                  first-assistant post itself by Senate confirmation.
│                ✕ Withdrawing the nomination does not lift the bar.  A withdrawal
│                  necessarily comes after the submission, and the submission is the
│                  trigger.  The bar runs for the rest of the vacancy.
│
■ STAGE D · THE CLOCK (§ 3346 · § 3349a · § 3348(c))
│
│   ──▶ 210 days, beginning on the date the vacancy occurs.                 § 3346(a)(1)
│   ──▶ Service may continue for as long as a FIRST or SECOND nomination for the office
│       is pending in the Senate, however long that is.                     § 3346(a)(2)
│   └─▶ If that nomination is rejected, withdrawn or returned, a NEW 210 days begins
│       from that event.                                              § 3346(b)(1), (2)
│   ⊘ A third nomination adds nothing.  There are three periods and no more.
│   ◎ § 3348(c) · If the last day of a 210-day period falls on a day the Senate is not
│     in session, it moves to the second day the Senate next sits and receives
│     nominations.  The only point in the Act where the Senate's calendar matters.
│

■ STAGE E · WHEN THE TIME RUNS OUT (§ 3348(b))
│
│   ──▶ The office is simply VACANT.  Nobody is acting in it.
│   ──▶ For any office other than the head of the agency, ONLY THE HEAD OF THE AGENCY
│       may then perform its functions and duties.  Not the deputy, not the person who
│       was acting the day before, not a designee of either.
│   ⚑ Nothing in the Act requires the seat to be filled, announced or explained, and
│     nothing sets a date by which a nomination must be sent.
│
■ STAGE F · THE REMEDY, AND THE FIVE OFFICES IT DOES NOT REACH (§ 3348(d), (e))
│
│   § 3348(d)(1) · An action taken by a person not serving in compliance with the Act,
│   in performing a FUNCTION OR DUTY of the vacant office, HAS NO FORCE OR EFFECT.
│   § 3348(d)(2) · And it MAY NOT BE RATIFIED.  No one can sign it afterwards.
│
│   ◎ THE HINGE IS THE DEFINITION.  § 3348(a)(2) counts a function or duty only if it
│     is established by statute, or by a regulation in effect at some point in the 180
│     days before the vacancy, AND is required to be performed by "the applicable
│     officer (and only that officer)."  A duty the agency may lawfully assign to
│     somebody else is outside the remedy no matter who performed it.
│
│   ⊘ § 3348(e) · AND THE SECTION DOES NOT APPLY TO FIVE OFFICES AT ALL:
│       (1) the General Counsel of the National Labor Relations Board
│       (2) the General Counsel of the Federal Labor Relations Authority
│       (3) any Inspector General appointed with the advice and consent of the Senate
│       (4) any Chief Financial Officer appointed with the advice and consent of the
│           Senate
│       (5) an office of an Executive agency — the Executive Office of the President
│           included, the Government Accountability Office excepted — where a statute
│           expressly prohibits the head of the agency from performing its duties
│
│   ✕ In those five, an acting official who served unlawfully leaves behind decisions
│     that stand.  The clock still applied.  The sanction never did.
│
■ STAGE G · WHO IS TOLD (§ 3349)
│
│   ☐ The head of the agency must notify the Comptroller General AND EACH HOUSE OF
│     CONGRESS, "immediately upon the occurrence," of four things: the vacancy and its
│     date · the name of whoever is serving in an acting capacity and the date that
│     service began · the name of any nominee and the date of submission · the date any
│     nomination was rejected, withdrawn or returned.
│
│   ☐ If the Comptroller General DETERMINES that an officer has served beyond the
│     210-day period, the determination goes immediately to six places: the Senate and
│     House oversight committees, both Appropriations Committees, the committees of
│     jurisdiction, the President, and the Office of Personnel Management.
│
│   ✕ ⚑ But nothing requires the Comptroller General to MAKE the determination.  The
│       input is self-reported by the agency; the output is discretionary.
│   ★ 17 SEPTEMBER 2026 · A determination was made, against an acting inspector general
│     who had used the title thirteen days past the end of lawful service.  The letter
│     records that a clerical error is no defence — and that § 3348(e)(3) had already
│     removed that office from the voiding provision, so the question of consequence
│     did not need to be reached.  The letter was the consequence.
│
■ STAGE H · THE ONLY TEST ANY OF THIS EVER GETS
│
│   ──▶ A defendant in a criminal case, or a party to an agency action, raises the Act
│       as a defence.  There is no body that reviews an acting designation before it
│       takes effect, and no office inside the executive branch with a reason to ask.
│   ✕ So the Act is construed almost entirely in litigation about something else, and
│     the remedy runs to whoever happened to be prosecuted or regulated that year.
│   ★ 1 DECEMBER 2025 · A court of appeals held that delegating ALL the powers of a
│     Senate-confirmed office to one person violates § 3347 exclusivity, because a
│     general delegation statute is not an exception to the Act — otherwise the
│     delegate could serve as the de facto officer indefinitely.  The same opinion
│     expressly left open whether delegating the same powers to SEVERAL people would
│     be lawful.
│   ◈ Which leaves the route around this Act a question of arithmetic rather than
│     principle: not whether a Senate-confirmed office can be run without the Senate,
│     but across how many people it must be spread before the Act stops noticing.
│

║  THE PATTERN                                                                            ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  Read forwards, the Act is a limit.  Three named doors, one disqualifier, a 210-day     ║
║  clock, a reporting duty owed to both chambers, and acts that are void if any of it     ║
║  is broken.                                                                             ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  Read backwards, from the remedy, the shape is different.  The voiding provision does   ║
║  not reach the inspector general.  It does not reach the chief financial officer.  It   ║
║  does not reach the two general counsels who decide what their agencies will take to    ║
║  court.  Those are four of the offices a statute would protect first if the object      ║
║  were to stop an unconfirmed official doing lasting damage, and they are four of the    ║
║  five the section names in order to leave out.                                          ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  What is left of this Act in those seats is a clock, a filing, and a letter.            ║
