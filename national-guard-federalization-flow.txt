NATIONAL GUARD FEDERALIZATION FLOW  —  three statuses, four doors, one criminal statute
│   Legend: ──▶ status change  ◇ decision  ★ operative act  ✕ closed path  ⚑ contested claim
│   Process evergreen · 2025–26 worked examples verified 2026-09-02 · no officeholders by design
│
■ 0. THE FLOOR  —  one soldier, two commissions, and a switch between them
│
│   Art. I §8 cl.15–16 (the Militia Clauses): CONGRESS holds the power "to provide for
│   calling forth the Militia to execute the Laws of the Union, suppress Insurrections
│   and repel Invasions," and to organize and arm it — the STATES keep officer
│   appointment and training authority.
│   Art. II §2: the President commands the militia only "when called into the actual
│   Service of the United States."
│   ⚑ The sequence is the whole design. The call-forth power is LEGISLATIVE; the command
│     power is executive and CONDITIONAL on a call Congress authorized by statute.
│
│   Since the Militia Act of 1903 and the 1933 amendments every Guard member holds a
│   DUAL ENLISTMENT — a state militia commission AND a commission in the National Guard
│   of the United States, a federal reserve component. Federalization therefore does not
│   transfer a soldier anywhere. It flips which commission is active.
│   Perpich v. Dep't of Defense (1990, 9-0) settled that Congress may activate the
│   federal commission for overseas training over a governor's express refusal.
│
├──▶ STATUS 1 ── STATE ACTIVE DUTY (SAD)  —  state law, top to bottom
│      Commander ......... the Governor
│      Payer ............. the State
│      Posse Comitatus ... DOES NOT APPLY — these are not federal forces at all
│      Typical use ....... hurricanes, floods, wildfires, state-law policing support
│      Federal role ...... none
│
├──▶ STATUS 2 ── TITLE 32  —  federally funded, state commanded      ◀── the workhorse
│      Commander ......... the Governor  (32 U.S.C. §328 — the GOVERNOR issues the order)
│      Payer ............. the United States  (32 U.S.C. §502(f), "training or other duty")
│      Posse Comitatus ... DOES NOT APPLY — §1385 reaches the FEDERAL armed forces, and a
│                          Title 32 soldier remains under state command and control
│      Consent ........... the GOVERNOR's. Under §502(f)(2)(A) the President or SecDef may
│                          only REQUEST such duty. A governor may simply decline.
│      ⚑ This status does most of the real work, and it is the quiet one. Federal money,
│        federal mission, federal request — and none of the federal restriction. It is
│        what the Brennan Center calls the Posse Comitatus loophole, and closing it has
│        been proposed in every recent NDAA cycle and enacted in none.
│      ⚑ Consented Title 32 deployments generate almost no federal litigation, because
│        there is no federalization to challenge. Memphis (Oct 2025) and New Orleans
│        (Dec 2025) both ran here at the governors' own request.
│
└──▶ STATUS 3 ── TITLE 10  —  federalized
       Commander ......... the President → SecDef → the combatant command (NORTHCOM)
       Payer ............. the United States
       The Governor ...... CUT OUT. Not asked, not required, cannot recall the unit.
       Posse Comitatus ... APPLIES. 18 U.S.C. §1385 makes it punishable by up to 2 years
                           to willfully use the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force or
                           Space Force "to execute the laws," except as expressly
                           authorized by the Constitution or an Act of Congress.
                           [ Navy, Marines and Space Force added by the FY22 NDAA,
                             Pub. L. 117-81 §1045(a), 12/27/2021 — the "Army and Air
                             Force only" description of the PCA is now five years stale ]
       ⚑ So: the fight was never about whether troops appear on a street. It is about
         which STATUS puts them there, because the status decides whether §1385 is in
         the room — and §1385 is the premise of every injunction that has issued.

◆ THE FOUR DOORS INTO TITLE 10  —  and they are not interchangeable
│
├─ DOOR A ── 10 U.S.C. §12406   "National Guard in Federal service: call"
│    ◇ Predicate — the President must find ONE of three:
│        (1) the U.S. "is invaded or is in danger of invasion by a foreign nation"
│        (2) "there is a rebellion or danger of a rebellion against the authority of
│            the Government of the United States"        [ "rebellion" is UNDEFINED ]
│        (3) the President "is unable with the regular forces to execute the laws of
│            the United States"
│    ★ Orders "shall be issued THROUGH the governors of the States."
│      ⚑ Executed since June 2025 as a notification, not a request. No court has held
│        the clause gives a governor a veto — and none has held it does not.
│    ✕ What §12406 does NOT do: it federalizes, and that is all. It does not authorize
│      law enforcement. §1385 attaches to the Title 10 force the moment it is created.
│      The government's repeated conflation of these two is what it kept losing on.
│    ⚑ No numeric cap. No duration limit. No report to Congress. Compare Door D.
│
├─ DOOR B ── the INSURRECTION ACT   (10 U.S.C. §§251-255; renumbered from 331-335 in 2016)
│    §251  insurrection against a STATE government ...... REQUIRES that state's request
│                                                          (legislature, or governor if
│                                                          it cannot be convened)
│    §252  obstruction makes it "impracticable to           NO state request needed
│          enforce the laws of the United States ... by
│          the ordinary course of judicial proceedings"
│    §253  deprivation of a class's constitutional          NO state request needed — and
│          rights the state cannot or will not protect,     the verb is "SHALL take such
│          OR obstruction of federal law or justice         measures as he considers
│                                                           necessary"
│    §254  ★ PROCLAMATION TO DISPERSE — required first, whenever the chapter is used
│    ⚑ This is the door that switches Posse Comitatus OFF, because §1385 excepts what an
│      Act of Congress expressly authorizes. It is also the only door with a public,
│      dated, signed instrument at its threshold — and that visibility is the deterrent.
│      THREATENED REPEATEDLY IN 2025-26. NEVER INVOKED.
│
├─ DOOR C ── the D.C. EXCEPTION  —  no governor to route an order through
│    D.C. Code §49-409 (Act of Mar. 1, 1889): "The President of the United States shall
│    be the Commander-in-Chief of the militia of the District of Columbia."
│    Delegated by EO 11485 (1969): President → SecDef → Secretary of the Army.
│    §12406's orders clause routes through "the commanding general of the National Guard
│    of the District of Columbia" instead of a governor.
│    ⚑ Consequence: the D.C. Guard can be deployed under direct presidential command
│      WITHOUT being federalized — which is the government's argument that §1385 never
│      attaches at all. It is the only structural argument it has so far won on.
│
└─ DOOR D ── the §1230x MOBILIZATION AUTHORITIES  —  a door Congress bolted shut
     §12301(a)  full mobilization — war or emergency declared by CONGRESS
     §12302     partial mobilization — presidential emergency; ≤24 months, ≤1,000,000
     §12304     Presidential Reserve Call-Up — ≤365 days, ≤200,000, 24-hour report to
                Congress
     ✕ §12304(c)(1) EXPRESSLY FORBIDS using that authority to perform "any of the
       functions authorized by chapter 15 or section 12406 of this title."
     ⚑ Read Doors A and D together and the asymmetry is the finding: Congress attached
       caps, clocks and reporting to every mobilization authority, then walled them off
       from domestic use — leaving the domestic instrument, §12406, the only one with
       no cap, no clock and no reporting requirement at all.

◆ ONCE TROOPS MOVE  —  the courthouse, and what it can actually reach
│
├─ ◇ Is the President's §12406 finding reviewable at all?
│     Government ......... Martin v. Mott (1827): the exigency determination "belongs
│                          exclusively to the President, and ... his decision is
│                          conclusive upon all other persons."
│     Every court to
│     reach it, 2025-26 ... NO. Sterling v. Constantin (1932) supplies the standard —
│                          the executive gets a "permitted range of honest judgment,"
│                          which is deference, not immunity.
│     9th Cir. 6/19/2025 .. reviewable, but "highly deferential"      → stay GRANTED
│     7th Cir. 10/16/2025 . reviewable; great deference on the FACTS, but NO deference
│                          and de novo review on what the STATUTORY WORDS MEAN
│
├─ ★ SUPREME COURT — Trump v. Illinois, 12/23/2025  (stay DENIED · unsigned · 3 dissents)
│     "Regular forces" in §12406(3) LIKELY MEANS THE REGULAR U.S. MILITARY — not ICE,
│     not the Federal Protective Service, not any federal civilian officer.
│       ⟹ the President must be unable WITH THE MILITARY to execute the laws
│       ⟹ which presupposes the military COULD lawfully execute them
│       ⟹ which §1385 makes exceptional
│       ⟹ and the government "failed to identify a source of authority" that would.
│     ⚑ Formally an interim stay order at the "likely" stage, not a merits holding.
│       REVIEWABILITY EXPRESSLY RESERVED. The Insurrection Act is untouched by it.
│       One concurrence reached the judgment on the narrower ground that the required
│       determination had simply never been made — so the broad statutory reading
│       commanded fewer votes than the 6-3 outcome suggests.
│
├─ WHO CAN SUE ......... STATES and CITIES, on sovereign and proprietary injury.
│                        Individual protesters generally cannot: Laird v. Tatum (1972)
│                        holds that subjective chill is not an Article III injury.
│
└─ ◇ WHAT A COURT CAN ORDER — and the gap that ended every single one of these cases
      ✓ Enjoin the DEPLOYMENT — the operational use of the troops ....... done, repeatedly
      ✓ Declare a PCA violation and enjoin law-enforcement use ........... 9/2/2025
      ✓ Enter a PERMANENT INJUNCTION after trial on the predicates ....... 11/7/2025
      ~ Order DEFEDERALIZATION — the unit back to the governor ........... ordered
                                 6/12/2025, stayed within hours, never sustained
      ✕ Reach the merits after the executive has already withdrawn ....... ⚑ THE GAP
        A case ends when the troops go home. Withdrawal moots the appeal, and the
        ruling below survives unreviewed — or, if it was the government's own appeal,
        is dismissed on the government's own motion.

◆ FIVE THINGS THIS MAP IS FOR
│
│  1. THE CONTESTED VARIABLE IS STATUS, NOT DEPLOYMENT. Title 32 already gives the
│     federal government everything it wants except the ability to act over a governor's
│     objection. Every fight of 2025-26 was about buying that one thing.
│
│  2. THE POSSE COMITATUS ACT IS THE HINGE, AND IT IS A CRIMINAL STATUTE NOBODY IS EVER
│     PROSECUTED UNDER. Its entire practical force is as the premise of a civil
│     injunction — which is why the argument is always about whether it applies, never
│     about whether it was broken.
│
│  3. §12406 FEDERALIZES; IT DOES NOT AUTHORIZE POLICING. The government's litigating
│     position collapsed those two repeatedly, and every court that reached it pulled
│     them apart. A district court put it plainly: reading §12406 as its own exception
│     to §1385 would nullify the Act.
│
│  4. THE ONE DOOR THAT WOULD ACTUALLY WORK WAS NEVER OPENED. The Insurrection Act
│     switches §1385 off by its terms. It was threatened for eighteen months and never
│     invoked — because §254 requires a signed, dated, published proclamation ordering
│     the insurgents to disperse, and that document is a political cost the informal
│     route avoids entirely.
│
│  5. NOT ONE OF THESE RULINGS WAS AFFIRMED ON THE MERITS. Four district courts ruled
│     against the government; every case ended in withdrawal, voluntary dismissal, or
│     mootness before an appellate court could decide it. The law now governing the
│     next federalization is a three-page interim stay order, a permanent injunction
│     nobody reviewed, and a circuit stay in the one jurisdiction with no governor.
