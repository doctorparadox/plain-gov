HOW A FEDERAL PROSECUTION BEGINS AND ENDS                       (as of 16 September 2026)
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path  └─▶ branch/exit  ◇ decision  ✕ case ends  ⚑ load-bearing stage
│            ▣ supervision layer   [ ] dated note or caveat
│   Scale:   66,662 individuals sentenced in federal court in FY2025. 98% of all cases
│            resolved by GUILTY PLEA; more than 99% of immigration cases did. Roughly
│            2-3% of federal convictions follow a trial.
│            [ U.S. Sentencing Commission, 2025 Sourcebook / 2025 Individual Datafile,
│              covering 1 Oct 2024 - 30 Sep 2025. ]
│
■ 0. PREDICATION   (before a case exists, someone decides to look)
│     An agency opens a matter: FBI, DEA, ATF, HSI, IRS-CI, an Inspector General, or a
│     referral from a regulator, a grand jury in another district, or a member of Congress.
│     └─▶ no judicial officer is involved. Nothing here is public. Nothing here is reviewable.
│     [ The opening threshold is set by agency guidelines (for the FBI, the AG Guidelines
│       for Domestic FBI Operations and the DIOG), not by statute. Changing that threshold
│       changes what is investigated without changing a single criminal law. ]
│
├─◆ 1. INVESTIGATION   (agents build it; the prosecutor supplies the compulsory process)
│     │  AGENT TOOLS ─────────────── interviews · surveillance · informants · consensual
│     │                              recordings · undercover work · administrative subpoenas
│     │  COURT-AUTHORIZED TOOLS ──── search warrants (Rule 41, probable cause, a magistrate
│     │                              judge signs) · Title III wiretaps (AG-level approval,
│     │                              plus necessity) · pen registers · § 2703(d) orders
│     │  GRAND JURY TOOLS ────────── subpoenas duces tecum and ad testificandum · immunity
│     │                              under 18 U.S.C. § 6002-6003 · secrecy under Rule 6(e)
│     │
│     ◇ is the target notified?  ─── generally NO. A target letter is discretionary and in
│     │                              most districts uncommon outside white-collar practice.
│     └─▶ statute of limitations runs throughout: 5 years by default (18 U.S.C. § 3282),
│          longer for specific offenses, none for capital offenses or most terrorism.
│
├─◆ 2. THE CHARGING DECISION   ⚑⚑  (the most consequential stage, and it has no docket)
│     │
│     │  ▣ THE SUPERVISION CHAIN
│     │    Line AUSA ──▶ Section/Unit Chief ──▶ Criminal Chief ──▶ First Assistant ──▶
│     │    U.S. ATTORNEY (93 of them, across 94 judicial districts)
│     │      └─▶ for sensitive categories, further up: the litigating Division at Main
│     │           Justice ──▶ DEPUTY ATTORNEY GENERAL ──▶ ATTORNEY GENERAL
│     │
│     │  ▣ JUSTICE MANUAL 9-27.000 - PRINCIPLES OF FEDERAL PROSECUTION
│     │    9-27.220  substantial federal interest + admissible evidence sufficient to
│     │              obtain and sustain a conviction
│     │    9-27.230  adequacy of an alternative (state prosecution, civil, regulatory)
│     │    9-27.260  IMPERMISSIBLE considerations: political association, activities or
│     │              beliefs; race; religion; sex; national origin
│     │    [ By its own terms the Manual confers NO enforceable rights. A defendant cannot
│     │      move to dismiss because it was ignored. It binds only if supervisors enforce it. ]
│     │
│     │  ▣ PRIOR-APPROVAL CATEGORIES (a partial list; each routes above the district)
│     │    public corruption and election offenses ...... Public Integrity Section
│     │    RICO ......................................... Organized Crime and Gangs Section
│     │    FCPA ......................................... Fraud Section
│     │    national security and FARA ................... National Security Division
│     │    subpoenas to members of the news media ....... Attorney General personally
│     │    [ These are internal routing rules, revisable by memorandum, with no notice to
│     │      Congress and no publication requirement when narrowed. ]
│     │
│     ◇ charge, or decline?
│     ├─▶ CHARGE ───────────────────▶ continue to stage 3
│     └─▶ DECLINE ✕ ── THE EXIT WITH NO RECORD. No filing, no docket number, no notice to
│          the subject, no statistic published identifying the matter. Presumptively
│          unreviewable under Heckler v. Chaney (1985). Selective- and vindictive-
│          prosecution claims exist but United States v. Armstrong (1996) requires a
│          threshold showing before discovery is even allowed, which is close to
│          unobtainable when the comparator cases are the ones that were never filed.
│
├─◆ 3. THE GRAND JURY   ⚑  (the only citizen check, and the one that almost never fires)
│     16-23 citizens · 12 votes to indict · no judge in the room · no rules of evidence ·
│     no defense counsel inside · target has no right to appear · Rule 6(e) secrecy
│     ├─▶ TRUE BILL ──────────────▶ indictment returned, continue to stage 4
│     ├─▶ NO BILL ✕ ─── the matter ends unless re-presented, and the Justice Manual
│     │                  requires the responsible U.S. Attorney's approval to go again.
│     └─▶ BYPASS ROUTES that skip this stage entirely:
│          · INFORMATION — the defendant waives indictment, almost always as part of a
│            negotiated plea. This is how a large share of white-collar cases are charged.
│          · COMPLAINT + Rule 5 — an arrest first on a magistrate-signed complaint, with
│            the indictment following within 30 days under the Speedy Trial Act.
│          · MISDEMEANOR — no indictment required at all.
│     [ FY2013 baseline: 5 no bills in 196,969 matters (BJS). 2025-26 saw an unusual
│       cluster of federal no bills in politically sensitive matters. Both figures are
│       carried here as reported; no rate is asserted for 2026, because none is published. ]
│
├─◆ 4. ARREST OR SUMMONS ──▶ INITIAL APPEARANCE ──▶ ARRAIGNMENT
│     Rule 5 initial appearance before a magistrate judge, usually within 24-48 hours ·
│     appointment of counsel under the Criminal Justice Act · the detention decision
│     ◇ detention or release?  (Bail Reform Act, 18 U.S.C. § 3142)
│     ├─▶ released on conditions ── the majority of defendants, but not of drug or
│     │                             immigration defendants
│     └─▶ DETAINED PENDING TRIAL ── a rebuttable presumption of detention attaches to
│          whole statutory categories. Detention is the single strongest predictor of
│          pleading guilty, which is why this stage belongs on a map of charging outcomes
│          and not merely on a map of custody.
│     Arraignment: the indictment is read, a plea is entered (virtually always NOT GUILTY
│     at this stage), and the Speedy Trial Act clocks start: 30 days arrest-to-indictment,
│     70 days indictment-to-trial.
│
├─◆ 5. DISCOVERY AND MOTIONS   (asymmetric by design, and the clock stops here)
│     GOVERNMENT MUST DISCLOSE ── Rule 16 materials · Brady exculpatory evidence · Giglio
│                                 impeachment of its own witnesses · Jencks statements,
│                                 which by statute need not come until after direct exam
│     DEFENSE MUST DISCLOSE ────── far less: alibi, insanity, expert reciprocity
│     NO DEPOSITIONS of witnesses, unlike every civil case in the same courthouse
│     ◇ dispositive motions: to dismiss the indictment · to suppress · to sever · to
│       compel · venue · vindictive or selective prosecution
│     └─▶ every motion filed EXCLUDES time from the 70-day clock (18 U.S.C. § 3161(h)),
│          as does any "ends of justice" continuance. A complex case can run years with
│          the statutory clock never expiring.
│
├─◆ 6. THE PLEA   ⚑⚑  (where 98 of every 100 cases actually end)
│     │  Rule 11(c)(1)(A) charge bargain — counts dismissed or not filed
│     │  Rule 11(c)(1)(B) recommendation  — non-binding on the judge
│     │  Rule 11(c)(1)(C) agreed sentence — binding if the judge accepts the agreement
│     │  Plus: cooperation agreements, proffer ("queen for a day") letters, deferred and
│     │  non-prosecution agreements (mostly corporate), and pretrial diversion.
│     │
│     │  THE PRICE OF REFUSING ── not a rule, but the sum of ordinary mechanisms:
│     │    · acceptance of responsibility (-2, or -3) is available only on a plea
│     │    · a mandatory minimum count can be dropped, or never filed
│     │    · the § 851 prior-conviction enhancement can be withheld, or filed
│     │    · a § 5K1.1 cooperation departure requires the prosecutor's motion
│     │  [ NACDL's 2018 study found the average post-trial sentence exceeded the average
│     │    post-plea sentence by more than 3x across most primary offense categories. ]
│     ├─▶ PLEA ACCEPTED ✕ ────────▶ straight to stage 8, sentencing
│     └─▶ PLEA REJECTED / REFUSED ▶ stage 7, trial
│
├─◆ 7. TRIAL   (rare enough to be a statistical outlier rather than the default)
│     Jury of 12, unanimous · government bears proof beyond a reasonable doubt · Rule 29
│     motion for judgment of acquittal at the close of the government's case and again
│     after the verdict
│     ├─▶ ACQUITTAL ✕ ── final. The Double Jeopardy Clause bars any government appeal,
│     │                   which makes this the only truly unreviewable ending on the map.
│     └─▶ CONVICTION ──▶ stage 8
│     [ Acquitted conduct may still raise the sentence at stage 8 under a preponderance
│       standard. The Sentencing Commission narrowed this by amendment in 2024; it did
│       not eliminate it. ]
│
├─◆ 8. SENTENCING   (the judge decides; the Guidelines decide what the judge starts from)
│     Presentence report by U.S. Probation ──▶ objections ──▶ Guidelines calculation:
│       offense level (base + specific offense characteristics + adjustments) crossed with
│       criminal history category, on the 43 x 6 table
│     ──▶ departures and variances ──▶ the 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a) factors ──▶ sentence imposed
│     Advisory since United States v. Booker (2005) — but the range must still be
│     calculated correctly, treated as the starting point, and any variance explained well
│     enough to survive reasonableness review.
│     └─▶ MANDATORY MINIMUMS override the whole calculation where they apply. The only two
│          exits are the § 3553(f) safety valve and a § 5K1.1 / Rule 35(b) cooperation
│          motion, which only the prosecutor can file. The charging decision at stage 2
│          therefore sets the sentencing floor here, six stages earlier.
│
├─◆ 9. APPEAL AND COLLATERAL REVIEW
│     DIRECT APPEAL ─── to the circuit (14 days to notice). Preserved issues reviewed for
│                       error; unpreserved ones for plain error. Most plea agreements
│                       contain an APPEAL WAIVER, which is generally enforced.
│     ──▶ petition for certiorari ── discretionary, and granted in a vanishingly small
│          fraction of federal criminal cases
│     COLLATERAL ────── 28 U.S.C. § 2255 motion (1-year limit, ineffective assistance and
│                       jurisdictional claims) · a second one requires circuit authorization
│     COMPASSIONATE RELEASE ── 18 U.S.C. § 3582(c)(1)(A), which the First Step Act let
│                       defendants file directly rather than only through the BOP
│     └─▶ EXECUTIVE EXIT — the pardon or commutation, which is outside this pipeline
│          entirely and reviewable by nothing in it. Mapped separately.
│
