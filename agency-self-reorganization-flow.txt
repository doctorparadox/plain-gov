HOW AN AGENCY REORGANIZES ITSELF WITHOUT CONGRESS                    (as of 25 October 2026)
│  ──▶ main path  └─▶ branch  ■ stage  ◆ instrument  ◇ decision  ⊘ foreclosed by statute
│  ◎ the hinge: statute stops, an internal document decides   ☐ advance notice to Congress
│  ✕ stopped, or a check that is not one   ⚑ a duty with no due date   ★ used in 2025-26
│  ◈ a reading or count made here, published nowhere        Offices here are plain text.
│
■ STAGE 0 · THE PROBLEM THIS ROUTE EXISTS TO SOLVE
│
│   ⊘ An agency created by statute can be abolished only by statute.
│   The President's standing power to send Congress a reorganization plan that took
│   effect unless Congress objected EXPIRED 31 DECEMBER 1984 and was never renewed.
│   In the years between the legislative veto's fall and that expiry it went unused.
│
│   ──▶ So the operative question is never "may this agency be abolished."  It is:
│       HOW MUCH OF THIS AGENCY DID CONGRESS ACTUALLY WRITE DOWN?
│
■ STAGE A · READ THE ORGANIC STATUTE                 (the step that cannot be skipped)
│
│   ◆ Count the officers the statute creates.  Anything it does not create was made
│     by someone inside the department, and can be unmade the same way.
│
│   WORKED EXAMPLE — THE CENSUS BUREAU, 13 U.S.C. § 21
│   ├─ officers the section creates .......... ONE — the Director, and no other
│   ├─ appointment .......................... the President, with Senate consent,
│   │                                         "without regard to political affiliation"
│   ├─ qualification ........................ "a demonstrated ability in managing
│   │                                         large organizations"
│   ├─ term ................................. five years; no more than two full terms
│   ├─ holdover ............................. allowed, but no more than one year
│   ├─ ☐ removal ............................ the President must give Congress written
│   │                                         reasons NOT LATER THAN 60 DAYS BEFORE
│   └─ the Director's duties ................ those "imposed by law, regulations, or
│                                             orders of the Secretary"
│
│   ◎ THE HINGE IS THE LAST LINE.  A statute that defines an office's work by
│     reference to the Secretary's orders has handed that office's content to the
│     Secretary.  Hold Stage A beside Stage C: the one protected seat in the statute
│     is the only seat the statute makes, and its own duties are assignable.
│
■ STAGE B · THE INSTRUMENT                                 (what does the reorganizing)
│
│   ◆ 5 U.S.C. § 301, the housekeeping statute — the head of an executive department
│     "may prescribe regulations for the government of his department," including
│     "the distribution and performance of its business."  No notice, no comment
│     period, no Federal Register publication, no outside review.
│   ◆ 5 U.S.C. § 302(b) — the head may DELEGATE final action on matters of "the
│     employment, direction, and general administration of personnel" downward.
│   ◆ The vehicle: a department organization order, an administrative order, a
│     delegation of authority, or a secretarial memorandum.  It is numbered rather
│     than published, and it is the entire paperwork of a reorganization.
│
│   ──▶ WHAT ONE SUCH ORDER CAN DO
│       ├─ create an office, directorate, division or post
│       ├─ abolish one — the 2023 Census order abolished a division in one sentence
│       │                and cited no statute at all for the power to do it
│       ├─ merge, split or rename one
│       ├─ move a reporting line
│       └─ shift a function from one component to another
│
│   TWO DEPARTMENTS WHERE CONGRESS WROTE THE RULE DOWN INSTEAD
│   ├─ ☐ Homeland Security · 6 U.S.C. § 452 — the Secretary may reallocate functions
│   │     and establish, consolidate, alter or discontinue units, but only after
│   │     60 DAYS' NOTICE to the committees with the reasons for it;  ⊘ and never to
│   │     abolish an agency, unit, program or function that a statute requires.
│   └─ Energy · 42 U.S.C. § 7253(a) — the Secretary may "establish, alter,
│         consolidate or discontinue such organizational units," with the nuclear
│         security administration carved out of the authority by a later statute.
│
│   ◈ These two are the exception that proves the rule.  Where Congress wanted
│     advance notice of an internal reorganization, it legislated for it, department
│     by department.  No general statute supplies one, so outside those grants and
│     outside the money gate at Stage F the notice owed to Congress is none.
│
■ STAGE C · THE POSTS                              (creating and ending jobs nobody voted on)
│
│   ──▶ A non-statutory post is created, split, renamed or abolished by the same kind
│       of order, and the only public trace is whatever the agency chooses to announce.
│
│   ★ WORKED EXAMPLE — THE TOP OF THE CENSUS BUREAU, 2025-26
│   ├─ Feb 2025 ..... the confirmed Director departs.  The career deputy director and
│   │                 chief operating officer becomes acting director
│   ├─ 19 Sep 2025 .. A MEMO designates a chief of staff from the office of the
│   │                 department's under secretary for economic affairs as performing
│   │                 the duties of Director; the career deputy returns to number two
│   ├─ 12 Jun 2026 .. A PRESS RELEASE announces two deputy directors — one for data,
│   │                 policy and science, one for administration and operations.  The
│   │                 first is described elsewhere as a newly created political post
│   ├─ late Jul 2026  the charter of the bureau's internal data-stewardship policy
│   │                 committee is rewritten to seat that new deputy as a co-chair
│   └─ 7 May 2026 ... ◈ the bureau's own leadership page, as revised that day, lists
│                     the acting head and NO DEPUTY DIRECTOR OF ANY KIND
│
│   ⚑ WHETHER THE CAREER NUMBER-TWO POST STILL EXISTS CANNOT BE SETTLED FROM ANY
│     PUBLIC DOCUMENT, AND IS NOT ASSERTED HERE.  [unconfirmed]  The department's
│     organization order for the bureau is still the one signed 22 June 2023, which
│     describes a single deputy director and none of the 2026 announcements.
│
│   ◆ THE TITLE THAT STARTS NO CLOCK
│     The acting head's title is "performing the NON-EXCLUSIVE functions and duties"
│     of the Director.  The Vacancies Act's 210-day limit and its provision voiding
│     the acts of an improperly serving official attach to a "function or duty" that
│     statute or regulation requires to be performed by "the applicable officer (and
│     only that officer)."  Everything else is delegable under ordinary law.
│     ◎ So the thinner the organic statute, the less there is that can be exclusive,
│       and the less of the office the Act reaches — and § 21 makes the Director's
│       duties the Secretary's to assign.  ◈ On the reading set out below, a
│       delegation of all that is delegable is not an acting appointment, and no
│       clock in the Act begins to run.  [No court has tested this designation.]
│
■ STAGE D · THE REPORTING LINE AND THE CHARTER          (control without moving anyone)
│
│   ◆ REALIGNMENT — a component keeps its name, its staff and its budget line, and
│     reports to a different official.  Nothing is created or abolished.  What moves
│     is who signs off, and there is no document a reader can check for it.
│   ◆ CHARTER REWRITE — an internal board or policy committee is not a federal
│     advisory committee and owes the public nothing: no charter filed with anyone,
│     no notice, no balance requirement, no minutes, no renewal clock.  Its chair and
│     its membership are set by whoever signs the charter, and reset the same way.
│   ◇ is the body created by statute? ─no──▶ ⊘ its composition is an internal paper
│                                    └yes─▶ composition is fixed — but who staffs
│                                           it, and who chairs it, may not be
│
■ STAGE E · THE PEOPLE                           (the two authorities that move humans)
│
│   ◆ DETAIL · 5 U.S.C. § 3341 — the department head may detail employees "among the
│     bureaus and offices" of the department by WRITTEN ORDER, for up to 120 days,
│     ◈ renewable in further 120-day periods with NO LIMIT ON THE NUMBER OF RENEWALS.
│     The only place the section mentions reorganization is the defence exception,
│     and there reorganization REMOVES the time limit rather than imposing one.
│   ◆ REASSIGNMENT · 5 U.S.C. § 3395 — a career senior executive may be moved to any
│     senior executive position in the same agency for which they are qualified:
│     ├─ within the commuting area ..... 15 days' written notice
│     ├─ outside the commuting area .... consultation about the reasons and the
│     │                                  appointee's preferences, then 60 days'
│     │                                  written notice with reasons; waivable in
│     │                                  writing by the appointee
│     └─ ✕ NOT INVOLUNTARILY within 120 days of the appointment of a new agency head,
│           or of a new noncareer immediate supervisor who writes the appraisal; and
│           up to 60 days spent on a detail do not count toward that 120, so parking
│           the executive elsewhere does not run the protection down.
│
│   ◈ That 120-day bar is the only hard timing limit anywhere on this map.  It
│     protects the senior executive rather than the structure, and it lapses by
│     itself four months after the new head arrives.
│
■ STAGE F · WHERE CONGRESS SEES IT                        (one gate, and it is about money)
│
│   ◇ DOES THE REORGANIZATION MOVE MONEY BETWEEN PROGRAMS?
│   │
│   ├─no──▶ ⊘ NOBODY OUTSIDE THE DEPARTMENT HAS TO BE TOLD ANYTHING AT ALL.
│   │          An order that renames an office, splits a post and moves a reporting
│   │          line inside a single appropriation triggers no notice anywhere.
│   │
│   └─yes─▶ ☐ THE REPROGRAMMING PROVISION.  In the Commerce, Justice, Science act
│              that funds the Census Bureau, section 505 bars obligating or spending
│              funds through a reprogramming that
│              ├─ (1) creates or initiates a new program, project or activity
│              ├─ (2) eliminates a program, project or activity
│              ├─ (3) increases funds or personnel for anything denied or restricted
│              ├─ (4) RELOCATES AN OFFICE OR EMPLOYEES
│              ├─ (5) REORGANIZES OR RENAMES OFFICES, PROGRAMS OR ACTIVITIES
│              └─ (6) contracts out or privatizes work done by federal employees
│              ──▶ items (2) onward require written notice to both Appropriations
│                  Committees 15 days in advance; after 1 August the period is 30
│                  days and the reprogramming must be an extraordinary circumstance.
│                  Item (1) is a flat bar, not a notice requirement.
│              [the numbered list is read from the FY2026 act itself; the day counts
│               and the augmentation threshold of $500,000 or 10 per cent, whichever
│               is less, are quoted from an earlier year of the same recurring
│               provision and are unconfirmed for FY2026]
│
│   ◎ THE GATE IS HUNG ON THE WRONG HINGE.  Clause (5) names reorganization in terms —
│     and it is a condition on a REPROGRAMMING OF FUNDS.  What is regulated is the
│     movement of money; the reorganization is only the reason for it.  So the
│     reorganizations that cost nothing are the ones nobody has to be told about.
│
■ STAGE G · WHAT CAN STOP IT                        (three candidates, two of them weak)
│
│   ◆ THE RIDER — the one instrument with a record of working.  Beginning in 2007,
│     appropriations acts barred the use of funds for reorganizations carried out
│     under the Homeland Security Act's own reorganization section, year after year,
│     until the FY2017 act left the bar out.  A rider can also require the
│     Appropriations Committees' prior approval before an account is reorganized, and
│     consultation before "any significant reorganization or restructuring of
│     offices."  It expires with the fiscal year and has to be won again every year.
│   ◆ THE COURT — tested in 2025-26, and tested narrowly.  ★ An executive order of
│     February 2025 directed agencies to prepare large-scale reductions in force,
│     and budget- and personnel-office guidance then required every agency to submit
│     a two-phase reduction-and-reorganization plan for approval.  The Supreme Court
│     stayed the injunction against the order and the guidance, finding the
│     government likely to succeed on their lawfulness, and EXPRESSLY LEFT OPEN the
│     legality of the individual agency plans.  Eleven months later the appellate
│     fight was still over whether a court may read those plans at all.
│   ✕ THE PUBLISHED ORDER — not a check, though it looks like one.  The department's
│     organization order for the bureau is dated 22 June 2023.  Its order for the
│     department's own mission and organization is dated 2012.  Of the forty-eight
│     organization orders listed, several were signed in the 1980s or 1990s.
│     ⚑ Nothing sets a deadline for reissuing one, so the published structure of a
│     federal agency is a record of some earlier arrangement, of unknown vintage.
│

║  THE PATTERN                                                                            ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  Every protection on this map is attached to a PERSON.  The officer who may not be      ║
║  removed without sixty days' written reasons to Congress.  The senior executive who     ║
║  may not be reassigned for one hundred and twenty days after a new head arrives.  The   ║
║  employee owed fifteen days before a move across town, sixty before a move out of it.   ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  Not one of them is attached to the STRUCTURE.  No office has to be announced before    ║
║  it ends.  No document has to be reissued when it changes.  No number of days is owed   ║
║  to anyone at all before a post is renamed, split, merged, or left in place and         ║
║  emptied of the authority that made it worth holding.                                   ║
║                                                                                         ║
║  Which is why the sixty-day removal clause on the Census Bureau's one statutory seat    ║
║  is not the protection it reads as.  It guards the chair.  Everything the chair         ║
║  presides over is a paragraph in an unpublished order, and an order can be rewritten    ║
║  in an afternoon by someone whose name the reader will never learn.                     ║
