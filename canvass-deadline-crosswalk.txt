■ PART A — WHAT A CANVASS IS, AND WHY THERE ARE TWO OF THEM
│
├─ A canvass is not a count. The count happens on election night and after; the canvass
│  is the ACCOUNTING for that count — precinct returns reconciled, provisional and cured
│  ballots resolved, the totals aggregated and declared official.
│
├─◆ STAGE 1 · THE COUNTY (or municipal) CANVASS
│   ├─ Who: a county board of canvassers — in many states the county commission or board
│   │       of supervisors sitting in a different capacity, with the clerk as secretary
│   ├─ Output: the ABSTRACT OF VOTES, the county's official return
│   └─ ✱ The board is almost always a body of general-purpose elected officials doing a
│        MINISTERIAL task. That mismatch is the whole of Part E.
│
└─◆ STAGE 2 · THE STATE CANVASS
    ├─ Who: a board of state canvassers, a state board of elections, or the chief election
    │       officer — composition varies more than any other element here
    ├─ Input: every county abstract. The state cannot finish until the last county reports,
    │         which is why most extension instruments in Part D are triggered by a MISSING
    │         RETURN rather than by a difficulty at the state level
    └─ Output: the official statewide result, and the certificate downstream law runs on

■ PART C — THE STATE CANVASS DEADLINE, AND THE FINDING THAT NINETEEN AREN'T DEADLINES
│
├─◆ LANE 1 · UNCONDITIONAL COMPLETION DATE — 31 states ◈
│   │  A date by which the state-level canvass must be DONE.
│   ├─ Day 14 Florida · Day 15/Dec Missouri · Day 16 Minnesota · Day 17–18 Georgia
│   ├─ Day 20 Michigan · Day 21 Idaho · Day 22 Alabama · Day 27 Colorado, Montana, Iowa
│   ├─ Day 30 Mississippi, New Jersey, Oregon, Washington · Day 31 Illinois · Day 33 Texas
│   ├─ Day 38 California · Within 20 Maine · Within 30 Maryland, Arkansas
│   ├─ 3rd Monday Kentucky · 3rd Tuesday New Mexico · 3rd Monday+ Arizona (postponable)
│   ├─ 4th Monday Nebraska, Utah · 4th Tuesday Nevada · Last Tuesday Nov. Indiana
│   ├─ Dec. 1 Wisconsin · 1st Mon. after 1st Wed. Dec. New York · 3rd Mon. Dec.+ Virginia
│   └─ Ohio: 10 days after the county abstracts arrive · North Carolina: 10 days after the
│      Tuesday three weeks out — ✱ both measured from an EVENT, not from election day
│
├─◆ LANE 2 · CONVENE-ONLY — a date to START and none to FINISH — 4 states
│   ├─ Alaska ......... begins no later than day 16 and "continues until completed"
│   ├─ Kansas ......... "must begin by Dec. 1" (§ 25-3206(b)) — a start, not a finish
│   ├─ Vermont ........ board convenes 10 a.m. one week after; no completion deadline
│   └─ Wyoming ........ convenes no later than the 2nd Wednesday; no completion deadline
│      ⊘ The duty to canvass is stated. The date it must end is not.
│
├─◆ LANE 3 · CONVENE + A STATUTORY ADJOURNMENT CAP — 4 states
│   │  An outer bound exists, but by arithmetic rather than by a stated deadline.
│   ├─ North Dakota ... meets by day 17, may adjourn up to 3 days (§§ 16.1-15-35, -38)
│   ├─ South Carolina . convenes within 10 days, may adjourn 5 days presidential /
│   │                   15 days other offices (§§ 7-17-220, 7-17-230)
│   ├─ South Dakota ... convenes within 7 days, may adjourn up to 10 (§ 12-20-47)
│   └─ Virginia ....... meets 3rd Monday in December, may adjourn up to 3 (§ 24.2-679)
│
├─◆ LANE 4 · CONTINGENT ON NO CONTEST OR RECOUNT BEING FILED — 5 states
│   │  ✱ The deadline exists only in the uncontested case — so the closer the race, the
│   │    less of a deadline there is, which inverts what a deadline is for.
│   ├─ Connecticut .... day 30 if no contest; after the 3rd Monday in Dec. if contested
│   ├─ Hawaii ......... after the time to bring a contest expires (§§ 11-155, 11-174.5)
│   ├─ Louisiana ...... on or before day 14 IF no contest is filed (§ 18:574)
│   ├─ New Hampshire .. after the recount filing period passes (§§ 659:84, 660:1)
│   └─ Oklahoma ....... 5 p.m. Friday after, IF no contest is filed (26 Okla. Stat. 8-108)
│      [Iowa sits in lane 1 but carries the same feature: § 50.48(6) delays the state
│       canvass for any office subject to a recount.]
│
└─◆ LANE 5 · NO STATE COMPLETION DEADLINE SPECIFIED AT ALL — 6 states
    ├─ Delaware ....... not specified — and its county stage names only a start time
    ├─ Massachusetts .. presidential only (declared day 10 after returns, certified 14
    │                   days later if no contest); OTHER OFFICES not specified
    ├─ Pennsylvania ... not specified
    ├─ Rhode Island ... not specified; completed once all returns reach the Secretary
    ├─ Tennessee ...... not specified
    └─ West Virginia .. not specified — the state with the LATEST county deadline (day 30)
       ✱ Pennsylvania is in this lane. The 2026-10-11 build turned on a Pennsylvania
         recount-waiver window that expires at noon on the 2nd Wednesday (25 P.S.
         § 3154(h)) — a deadline measured to the hour, sitting in a state with no stated
         deadline for finishing the canvass it governs.

■ PART D — WHO MAY MOVE THE DATE: FIVE INSTRUMENTS, AND ONE THAT ISN'T A STATUTE
│
├─◆ 1 · BOARD ADJOURNMENT WITH A CAP — the board moves its own date, up to a limit
│   └─ North Dakota 3 days · South Carolina 5/15 · South Dakota 10 · Virginia 3
│      ↗ Held by the canvassing board itself. No outside approval.
│
├─◆ 2 · AUTOMATIC DAY-TO-DAY POSTPONEMENT UNTIL THE RETURNS ARRIVE
│   ├─ Montana ........ if returns are incomplete the board postpones "day to day" until
│   │                   they are received (§ 13-15-401)
│   └─ Maryland ....... second Friday after, or within 48 hours of completion if later
│      ↗ Held by nobody — it operates by law. ✱ The cleanest design on this map: the
│        deadline cannot be missed, because a missing return moves it automatically.
│
├─◆ 3 · CHIEF ELECTION OFFICER DISCRETION
│   ├─ New Hampshire .. the Secretary of State may order a different county canvass date
│   │                   (§ 659:75)
│   └─ Arizona ........ the state canvass may be POSTPONED UP TO 30 DAYS until all county
│                       results are received (§ 16-648)
│      ✱ Arizona's 30-day postponement is the instrument that made the 2022 Cochise
│        County standoff survivable — one county's refusal could not run the state out
│        of time. See Part E.
│
├─◆ 4 · CONTEST OR RECOUNT CONTINGENCY — the filing moves the date
│   └─ Connecticut · Hawaii · Iowa · Louisiana · New Hampshire · Oklahoma · Massachusetts
│      ↗ Held by whichever party files. ✱ The only lane where a PRIVATE actor moves a
│        public deadline, which is the same structure the recount-waiver statutes use —
│        see How a Conceded Election Is Finalized.
│
└─◆ 5 · AN AUTHORIZATION GATE — Colorado, and it is a RULE rather than a statute
    ├─ ⏱ Statute: the county canvass is due no later than day 22 (§ 1-10-102); the state
    │      canvass no later than day 27 (§ 1-10-103)
    ├─ ↗ Election Rule 10.4 (8 CCR 1505-1): "The Secretary of State may extend the canvass
    │      deadline for one or more counties" "in order to complete the risk-limiting
    │      audit in accordance with Rule 25.2"
    └─ ✱ AND THE SENTENCE THAT INVERTS THE WHOLE COLUMN: "No canvass board may certify
         official results until authorized to do so by the Secretary of State." In
         Colorado the day-22 date is not a deadline a county races toward — it is the
         far edge of a window the STATE opens. The county cannot be late without
         permission and cannot be early without permission either. The instrument that
         does this is an administrative rule, not the Election Code, so it is changeable
         without a legislature.

■ PART E — WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE DEADLINE PASSES UNMET: THE COLUMN NO TABLE CARRIES
│
├─◆ THE BASELINE · NOTHING STATED — the modal case ⊘
│   └─ NCSL's canvass-deadline page describes NO penalty and NO consequence for a missed
│      deadline in any of the fifty states, beyond the extension and adjournment
│      provisions in Part D. ✱ A canvass deadline is, in most states, a duty with a date
│      and no sanction — which is why the question has to be answered from case law and
│      from chapters of the code that are not about elections.
│
├─◆ REMEDY 1 · THE WRIT OF MANDAMUS — the universal answer, and it is not election law ⚖
│   ├─ An extraordinary order compelling an official to perform a non-discretionary duty.
│   │  It works here precisely BECAUSE the canvass is ministerial: there is no judgment
│   │  for a court to defer to.
│   ├─ New Mexico, June 2022 — Otero County's commission refused to certify the primary.
│   │  The Secretary of State went to court to compel it; the commission then certified
│   │  2–1 at an emergency meeting. ✱ The statute relied on dates to at least 1953 and,
│   │  by contemporaneous reporting, had never been used before that month.
│   └─ ✱ The enforcement mechanism of American canvass law is a writ older than the
│        statutes it enforces, invented for an unrelated purpose, and essentially unused
│        until 2022.
│
├─◆ REMEDY 2 · CRIMINAL LIABILITY — Arizona, and it sits in the criminal chapter ⚖
│   ├─ 2022: two of the three Cochise County supervisors voted against canvassing the
│   │  midterm results by the November 28 deadline, after the County Attorney's Office
│   │  advised that the canvass was a non-discretionary duty.
│   ├─ The Secretary of State sued; the court ordered the board to reconvene; it certified
│   │  2–0, one supervisor absent.
│   ├─ A state grand jury indicted both on 27 November 2023 on two class 5 felonies —
│   │  CONSPIRACY and INTERFERENCE WITH AN ELECTION OFFICER.
│   ├─ One pleaded guilty on 21 October 2024 and received probation and a fine.
│   └─ ✱ Note what the charges are NOT: there is no offense of "missing the canvass
│        deadline." The conduct was reached by general conspiracy and interference
│        statutes, which is the same borrowing pattern as remedy 1.
│
├─◆ REMEDY 3 · THE COST OF THE COURIER — Nebraska, the one express statutory consequence
│   │  ⚑ and the only column-4 legislation passed anywhere in 2026
│   ├─ If a county's abstract has not reached the Secretary of State by the third Monday
│   │  after the election (§ 32-1034), the Secretary MAY, under § 32-1035:
│   │    ├─ require the abstract sent by overnight delivery with a tracking number, or by
│   │    │  next-day hand delivery, AT THE COUNTY'S EXPENSE; or
│   │    └─ send a messenger to the county clerk, AT THE COUNTY'S EXPENSE
│   ├─ And if the delay was the official's own fault or neglect, "he or she shall be
│   │  responsible to the county for the cost of the messenger."
│   └─ ✱ This is the complete set of express consequences found in the canvass chapters
│        examined: a shipping invoice, and in the worst case the clerk pays for it
│        personally. Set beside remedy 2, the fifty-state spread on what a missed canvass
│        deadline costs runs from TWO CLASS 5 FELONIES to ONE OVERNIGHT ENVELOPE.
│
└─◆ WHAT NO STATE APPEARS TO HAVE — the holes, named rather than filled
    ├─ No self-executing fallback: nowhere examined does a missed county deadline cause
    │  the state to canvass the county's returns ITSELF, or cause the unofficial return to
    │  become official by operation of law. The duty does not transfer.
    ├─ No removal trigger tied to the deadline as such.
    └─ ⊘ So in the modal state the answer to "what if they just don't" is: somebody must
         go to court, and a court must be willing to act inside the remaining calendar.

■ PART F — THE FEDERAL CLOCK OVER THE TOP, AND WHY IT DOES NOT REACH NOVEMBER 2026
│
├─◆ IN A PRESIDENTIAL YEAR — the Electoral Count Reform Act supplies the hard outer bound
│   ├─ ⏱ The state executive must issue the CERTIFICATE OF ASCERTAINMENT six days before
│   │     the meeting of electors.
│   ├─ ✱ That date works BACKWARD through every lane in Part C: a state's canvass, its
│   │     recounts and its contests must all finish inside it, whatever its own code says
│   │     — including the states in lanes 2, 4 and 5 that set no completion date at all.
│   ├─ ⚖ If the certificate is not issued or names the wrong winner: an action for an
│   │     order compelling issuance, heard by a THREE-JUDGE DISTRICT COURT (two circuit
│   │     judges and one district judge), with Supreme Court review on expedited
│   │     certiorari. The court has until the day before the electors meet. A certificate
│   │     issued under that order binds Congress in the count.
│   └─ ✱ So the one place American law DOES answer the missed-deadline question in advance
│        is the only place where the answer could not be left to the states.
│
└─◆ IN A MIDTERM — there is no federal backstop at all
    ├─ No certificate of ascertainment · no electors · no ECRA clock.
    ├─ U.S. Const. art. I § 5 cl. 1 makes each chamber the judge of its own members'
    │  elections, and the Federal Contested Elections Act's 30-day notice clock runs from
    │  the state's DECLARATION of the result — whenever that comes. A state that never
    │  finishes its canvass does not trip a federal deadline; it postpones the start of
    │  the federal one.
    └─ ✱ THE MIDTERM PAYOFF: on 3 November 2026 the canvass runs on state deadlines alone.
         In 19 states (Part C lanes 2–5) there is no unconditional date by which the state
         canvass must be done, and in the modal state no stated consequence if it is not.
         The downstream map is How a Contested House Seat Is Decided (queued).

■ PART G — WHAT CHANGED FOR NOVEMBER 2026 ⚑
│
├─◆ IDAHO · county canvass 10 days → NINE
│   ├─ § 34-1205 now reads: the board "shall meet within nine (9) days after an election."
│   ├─ Attributed by Ballotpedia to H 873, enacted as Session Law ch. 286, signed
│   │  2 April 2026, effective 1 July 2026; passed House 69–0–1 and Senate 34–0–1.
│   └─ [unconfirmed] WHICH session law made it nine. The statute page's amendment history
│      runs 1970–2025 (most recently 2025 ch. 113 § 18) and carries no 2026 citation, and
│      the site notes statutes are updated 1 July following each session. The OPERATIVE
│      TEXT is nine days; the attribution is not independently verified here.
│
├─◆ NEBRASKA · LB 1075, approved by the Governor 15 April 2026 — and NOT what it looked like
│   ├─ Reported as creating a submission deadline where none existed. What the bill text
│   │  shows is narrower and more interesting:
│   ├─ § 32-1034 — the abstract must reach the Secretary by the third Monday after the
│   │  election; if sent electronically or by fax, the original by the fourth Wednesday.
│   ├─ § 32-1035 — the late-abstract remedies are re-pinned FROM "the deadlines specified
│   │  in section 32-1034" TO the third Monday itself.
│   └─ ✱ So the 2026 change is not a new deadline. It is the ENFORCEMENT TRIGGER being
│        fastened to a specific date — the only state legislating in column 4 this year.
│
└─◆ WISCONSIN · 2025 Wisconsin Act 214 — real, and it does not apply to this election
    ├─ Replaces the flat 1 December state canvass for PRESIDENTIAL elections: county
    │  canvass before noon on the Saturday after, state canvass within 21 days.
    ├─ Purpose: conformity with the federal Electoral Count Reform Act clock in Part F.
    ├─ Applicability: the 2028 presidential election.
    └─ ✱ The only 2026 canvass legislation aimed at the federal deadline is the only one
         that does nothing for 3 November 2026. Wisconsin's midterm deadlines are
         unchanged: county day 14 (§ 7.60), state 1 December (§ 7.70).
