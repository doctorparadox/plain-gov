STATE EMERGENCY POWERS CROSSWALK — what a governor's declaration unlocks, and who
can switch it off                                            (as of September 2026)
│  Legend:  ▸ lane / check   ⚡ trigger   → what it unlocks   ⏱ clock
│           ⚖ what a court will review   ⊘ limit with no remedy attached
│           ◈ STATE-ONLY — no federal analogue   ⚑ contested in 2025–26
│
│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: the federal system has the grants without the checks.
│  The states have the checks. Whether any of them bind is Part B.
│
■ PART A — THE SEVEN LANES A DECLARATION OPENS
│
├─▸ 1. SUSPENSION OF STATUTES AND REGULATIONS ......... the broadest lane, and the
│      ⚡ the governor's own finding that a disaster or emergency exists   [oldest]
│      → suspend "regulatory statutes prescribing procedures for conducting state
│        business" and the rules of any state agency, where compliance would
│        "prevent, hinder, or delay necessary action"
│      ✱ THAT PHRASE IS NOT FIFTY DRAFTING CHOICES. It is one sentence, written
│        into the 1950s civil-defense model act and copied forward, which is why
│        state emergency codes read like each other and unlike their own statutes.
│      ⊘ almost every version limits the suspension to PROCEDURAL statutes and
│        names no forum to decide which ones those are
│      ⚖ courts review WHETHER THE SUSPENDED STATUTE WAS PROCEDURAL — not whether
│        the emergency was real. That is where the litigation actually lands.
│
├─▸ 2. PROCUREMENT AND CONTRACTING WAIVER ............. the lane with the money
│      ⚡ the same declaration; no separate finding required in most states
│      → buy without competitive bidding · sole-source award · waive the bid
│        protest track · in some states seize or condemn goods outright
│      → North Carolina's version reaches "purchase, condemnation, seizure, or by
│        other means" without regard to existing law    [G.S. 166A-19.30]
│      ⊘ the audit that would catch abuse runs AFTER the emergency ends, in a
│        report to a legislature that has no power to unwind a signed contract
│
├─▸ 3. NATIONAL GUARD — STATE ACTIVE DUTY ............. the force lane, and the one
│      ⚡ the declaration, or in most states the governor's word alone   most
│      → Guard members serve under STATE law, paid by the STATE, commanded by the
│        governor through the adjutant general                          misread
│      ✱ STATE ACTIVE DUTY IS THE ONLY STATUS IN WHICH THE POSSE COMITATUS ACT
│        DOES NOT APPLY, because the Guard is not then a federal force. Troops
│        doing law enforcement in an American street are usually in this status.
│      ⚑ → the governor's control is NOT exclusive. 10 U.S.C. § 12406 lets the
│        President federalize the same units, and the state declaration does not
│        block it — see the federal companion map for the four doors in.
│
├─▸ 4. MOVEMENT, ASSEMBLY, AND QUARANTINE ............. the lane with the least
│      ⚡ the declaration, and in about half the states a SEPARATE public-health
│        emergency declared by a health officer on a different clock
│      → evacuation orders · curfews · closure of roads and public places
│      → limits on the congregation of persons · isolation and quarantine
│      ✱ TWO SWITCHES, NOT ONE. The public-health track descends from the 2001
│        Model State Emergency Health Powers Act, drafted after the anthrax
│        letters and adopted in pieces by most states. It has its OWN trigger,
│        its OWN clock and its OWN termination rule — which is why a legislature
│        that ends the governor's emergency has often not ended anything.
│      ⚖ this is the most litigated lane in the country and the courts almost
│        never reach the declaration. They reach the DELEGATION (Michigan, 2020)
│        or the FORM OF THE ORDER (Wisconsin, 2020).
│
├─▸ 5. PRICE-GOUGING ENFORCEMENT ...................... the only lane that creates
│      ⚡ the declaration — in most states the statute is DORMANT until one exists
│      → a majority of states plus DC, Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands make
│        excessive pricing unlawful once an emergency is declared  [CRS R47072,
│        updated 5 May 2026]
│      → the enforcer is the ATTORNEY GENERAL, who in 43 states is elected
│        separately and is not the governor's subordinate
│      ✱ THE DECLARATION IS A PRIVATE-LAW EVENT. Signing it changes what a
│        merchant may charge a customer. Letting it lapse changes it back.
│      ⊘ Maine and Michigan are the exceptions worth knowing: their prohibitions
│        do not need an emergency to switch on   [CRS R47072]
│
├─▸ 6. INTERSTATE MUTUAL AID — EMAC ................... the lane nobody counts
│      ⚡ A GOVERNOR-DECLARED EMERGENCY. There is no other key.
│      → the Emergency Management Assistance Compact, ratified by Congress as
│        Pub. L. 104-321 (1996); 50 states, DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S.
│        Virgin Islands and the Northern Mariana Islands are members
│      → out-of-state responders arrive as AGENTS OF THE REQUESTING STATE
│      → their licenses, credentials and certifications are honored across the
│        state line · tort liability and workers' compensation transfer with them
│      ✱ THE DECLARATION IS AN INTERSTATE INSTRUMENT, not a domestic one. A
│        governor who lets it lapse to satisfy the legislature also switches off
│        every other state's ability to send help on recognized credentials.
│      ✱ This is the single strongest argument against a hard clock, and it is
│        almost never the argument that gets made.
│
└─▸ 7. THE KEY TO THE FEDERAL DOOR .................... the lane that is not a
       ⚡ the state declaration, plus a finding that the disaster exceeds state   power
         and local capability                                                  at all
       → the governor REQUESTS a federal major disaster or emergency declaration
         under the Stafford Act; the President grants or denies it
       ⊘ the governor cannot compel the answer, appeal a denial to any court, or
         obtain the money any other way
       ✱ A STATE DECLARATION IS A PRECONDITION, NOT A CLAIM. This is the cleanest
         federalism hinge in the whole map: the state's act is complete, lawful
         and entirely insufficient on its own.
│
■ PART B — THE THREE VARIABLES THAT DECIDE WHETHER IT EVER ENDS
│
├─▸ VARIABLE 1 — CAN THE LEGISLATURE TERMINATE IT?
│      ~42 states say yes on paper, by concurrent or joint resolution     [NCSL]
│      ✱ THE PAPER ANSWER IS NOT THE ANSWER. One sub-question decides everything:
│
│      ├─ IS THE RESOLUTION PRESENTED TO THE GOVERNOR?
│      │    NO  → a simple majority of both chambers ends the emergency and the
│      │          governor cannot stop it. This is a LEGISLATIVE VETO, and it is
│      │  ◈       lawful in states because INS v. Chadha construed the federal
│      │          Presentment Clause and binds nobody else.
│      │    YES → the legislature needs the same two-thirds it would need to
│      │          override a veto. The check is decorative, exactly as federally.
│      │
│      └─ WHICH ONE A STATE HAS IS OFTEN NOT IN THE EMERGENCY STATUTE AT ALL.
│           It is in the state constitution's presentment clause, and it is
│           usually decided by the state supreme court in the middle of the
│           emergency it is about to resolve.
│           ✱ Pennsylvania, July 2020: resolution must be presented. Check dead.
│           ✱ Pennsylvania, 18 May 2021: voters amend the constitution, ~52%.
│         ◈   Check alive, un-vetoable, and a 21-day cap added on top.
│           ✱ ELEVEN MONTHS FROM COURT DEFEAT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REPAIR.
│             Congress has had since 1983.
│
├─▸ VARIABLE 2 — DOES A CLOCK RUN ON ITS OWN?
│      ~30 states put an automatic expiry on the declaration              [NCSL]
│      typical terms: 15 days (KS, SC) · 21 (PA) · 28 (MI) · 30 (AK, ME, RI, UT,
│      and the most common single figure) · 45 (TN, cut from 60) · 60 (FL, WI) ·
│      90 (OH)                                                           [NCSL]
│      ⏱ compare the federal default: ANNUAL, and self-renewing by signature
│      ✱ SO THE STATES HAVE THE CLOCK THE FEDERAL SYSTEM NEVER BUILT. And then:
│
│      └─ ⊘ IS RE-DECLARING ON THE SAME FACTS BARRED?
│           Mostly no. A cap with no re-declaration bar is not a limit on the
│           emergency — it is a RENEWAL SCHEDULE, and the governor keeps the pen.
│           ✱ This is the provision that separates a reform that binds from a
│             reform that photographs well. It is also the provision most often
│             dropped between the model bill and the enacted text.
│           ✱ ~7 states also capped the NUMBER of renewals             [NCSL]
│
└─▸ VARIABLE 3 — WILL A COURT REVIEW THE DECLARATION ITSELF?
       Almost never — and this is the one place the state answer matches the
       federal one exactly. Courts treat the existence of the emergency as
       committed to the executive. What they will review is one layer down:
       ⚖ THE DELEGATION.  Michigan, October 2020: the state supreme court struck
         the 1945 Emergency Powers of the Governor Act on state NONDELEGATION
         grounds — the statute, not the declaration, not the virus.
       ⚖ THE FORM OF THE ACT.  Wisconsin, May 2020: the health secretary's
         stay-home order fell because it was a RULE issued without rulemaking.
         The court did not reach whether the emergency was real.
       ✱ The winning arguments are structural and procedural, not factual. A
         challenge that argues the emergency is exaggerated loses. A challenge
         that argues the legislature never delegated this, or that the order is
         a rule in disguise, wins — and the state nondelegation doctrine is in
         much better health than the federal one.
│
■ ◈ THE FOURTH VARIABLE, WHICH HAS NO FEDERAL ANALOGUE AT ALL
│
├─ THE PLURAL EXECUTIVE.  The governor is not the executive branch. In 43 states
│    the attorney general is separately elected; most states also elect a
│    secretary of state and a treasurer. North Carolina goes furthest: the
│    largest emergency powers — compelled evacuation, economic controls over
│    food, fuel, rents and wages, control of assembly, procurement by seizure —
│    require the CONCURRENCE OF THE COUNCIL OF STATE, ten separately elected
│    statewide officials.        [G.S. 166A-19.30]
│  ◈ A CHECK INSIDE THE EXECUTIVE BRANCH, EXERCISED BY PEOPLE THE GOVERNOR
│    CANNOT FIRE. Nothing in the federal system resembles this. The President's
│    Cabinet serves at pleasure; there is no one in the executive branch with
│    an independent mandate and a vote.
│
└─ THE BALLOT.  In 26 states the people can reach the statute books directly,
     and every state can amend its own constitution far more easily than the
     federal one can be amended. This is the repair mechanism. It is why the
     state version of the Chadha problem got fixed and the federal one did not.
   ◈ THE FEDERAL SYSTEM HAS NO EQUIVALENT REPAIR PATH. Not a slower one — none.
│
■ PART C — WHAT NO STATE DECLARATION DOES
│
├─ SUSPEND THE STATE CONSTITUTION  The suspension power reaches statutes and
│    regulations. State constitutional rights — speech, assembly, due process,
│    the right to bear arms in the many states that name it — are outside it,
│    and several states wrote explicit carve-outs after 2020 barring emergency
│    orders that close churches or firearms dealers.
│
├─ MOVE OR ALTER A FEDERAL ELECTION  ⚑  Federal election day is fixed by federal
│    statute. A governor's emergency power over STATE election administration is
│    real and varies enormously — polling-place relocation, deadline extension,
│    absentee rules — but it is a power to administer the federal date, never to
│    change it. With the midterms roughly five weeks out, this is the distinction
│    most likely to be blurred in both directions.
│
├─ BLOCK FEDERALIZATION OF THE STATE'S OWN GUARD  The declaration puts the Guard
│    in state active duty. It does not keep it there. 10 U.S.C. § 12406 runs
│    over the governor's objection, and the 2025–26 litigation turned on the
│    statute's text, not on the state's consent.
│
├─ CREATE POWER THE LEGISLATURE NEVER DELEGATED  Everything in Part A is
│    statutory. A governor who reaches past the grant is not exercising
│    emergency power; he is exercising none, which is what Michigan held.
│
└─ SURVIVE ITS OWN LAPSE  This is the asymmetry the reform debate keeps missing.
     When the declaration ends, the price-gouging prohibition switches off, the
     EMAC credential and liability transfer switches off, the procurement
     waivers switch off, and the Stafford request loses its predicate. THE
     CLOCK DOES NOT DISTINGUISH BETWEEN AN EMERGENCY THAT IS OVER AND AN
     EMERGENCY A LEGISLATURE HAS DECIDED TO END. That is the honest cost of
     the strongest check on this map, and it is a real one.
