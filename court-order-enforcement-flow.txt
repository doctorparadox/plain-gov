HOW A FEDERAL COURT ORDER IS ENFORCED                             (as of 15 October 2026)

│
■ 0. THERE HAS TO BE AN ORDER, AND IT HAS TO BE CLEAR AND SPECIFIC            ⚑
│     Nothing downstream survives a defective stage 0, and this is where the
│     government now wins. The order must be definite enough that a reasonable
│     person knows what it forbids; ambiguity is resolved in the alleged
│     contemnor's favour, and in CRIMINAL contempt that rule is near-absolute.
│     ◇ is the order still in force?
│     ├─ VACATED or REVERSED ──▶ civil contempt dies with it. A court cannot coerce
│     │     compliance with an order that no longer exists.                         ✕
│     ├─ VACATED but violated while in force ──▶ criminal contempt survives in theory,
│     │     because it punishes a past act rather than compelling a future one
│     └─ IN FORCE ──▶ continue
│     ◇ is it clear and specific?
│     ├─ AMBIGUOUS ──▶ no criminal contempt, full stop                              ✕
│     └─ CLEAR ──▶ continue
│     [ J.G.G.: the 15 Mar 2025 TRO barring "removing" detainees was held ambiguous,
│       and that single word ended the criminal-contempt route twice — D.C. Cir.
│       8 Aug 2025 (2-1) and again 14 Apr 2026 (2-1). Both were MANDAMUS, which means
│       the government did not have to wait to be charged to appeal. ]
│
├─◆ ROUTE A. THE STRICTER ORDER        — the route judges actually use           ⚑ ◈
│     Before contempt, the judge restates the order in narrower language and adds
│     the machinery that makes non-compliance legible:
│     ├─ a finding that the prior order was "clear and unambiguous" (building stage 0
│     │     for whatever comes next)
│     ├─ itemised relief — the specific funds, files, people or documents
│     ├─ a hard deadline with a time of day
│     ├─ a certification of compliance, signed, filed on the docket
│     └─ a named declarant: an official who must swear personally to the facts
│     └─▶ then daily or weekly status reports, and sometimes a show-cause order held
│          in reserve rather than issued
│     ◇ complied?  ──yes──▶ ★  (this is the ordinary ending, and it is not reported)
│                  ──no───▶ Route B
│     [ The 2026 pattern is to EXTEND rather than punish. Asked for contempt in the
│       diversity-visa litigation on 23 Sep 2026, the court instead gave a 10-day
│       extension on 30 Sep — because the government had spent the first 10 days not
│       instructing its posts to comply. The remedy for not using the time was
│       more time. ]
│
├─◆ ROUTE B. CIVIL CONTEMPT            — coercive, remedial, PURGEABLE           ⚑
│     Purpose is future compliance, not punishment, which is why the contemnor
│     always holds the keys. Burden: clear and convincing evidence of a clear order,
│     notice, and non-compliance.
│     ├─ WILLFULNESS IS NOT AN ELEMENT. Good faith, understaffing and caseload are
│     │     mitigation, not defences.
│     ├─ B1 the conditional fine ──▶ a per-day amount that begins on a stated date
│     │     UNLESS the act is done. Paid to the court (coercive) or to the injured
│     │     party (compensatory) — a distinction that decides who may waive it.
│     ├─ B2 the conditional confinement ──▶ available against a natural person; the
│     │     contemnor is released on compliance. Rarely reached; has been ordered.
│     ├─ B3 the compensatory award ──▶ the movant's actual losses from the violation
│     └─ B4 against a government DEFENDANT ──▶ the fine is largely uncollectable.
│           Sovereign immunity is not waived for contempt sanctions against the
│           United States; the workable target is the individual officer or the
│           individual attorney, in their personal capacity.
│     ◇ purged?  ──yes──▶ ★ fine lifted, nothing collected, no appealable sanction
│                ──no───▶ Route C or Route D
│     [ WORKED EXAMPLE, and the whole route in miniature: D. Minn., 18 Feb 2026 —
│       a $500/day conditional fine on a Special Assistant U.S. Attorney personally,
│       for a release order ignored in all three of its requirements. Documents
│       returned 19 Feb; fine lifted by written order 20 Feb. No money changed hands.
│       The first civil contempt of a federal attorney in the second term WORKED,
│       in one day, and cost nothing — which is the argument for the route and the
│       argument against it at the same time. ]
│
├─◆ ROUTE C. CRIMINAL CONTEMPT         — punitive, NOT purgeable, and blocked    ⚑
│     18 U.S.C. § 401: disobedience of a lawful writ, process, order, rule, decree
│     or command. Compliance after the fact is no defence, because the point is
│     punishment for a completed act.
│     │
│     ├─ C1 ◇ WAS IT IN THE JUDGE'S PRESENCE?
│     │     ├─ YES ──▶ SUMMARY DISPOSITION, Rule 42(b). The judge who saw or heard it
│     │     │     certifies the facts, signs the order, files it. No prosecutor, no
│     │     │     jury, no notice. This is the one fast route, and it is unavailable
│     │     │     for anything an agency does outside the courtroom.
│     │     └─ NO  ──▶ PROSECUTION ON NOTICE, Rule 42(a) ──▶ C2
│     │
│     ├─ C2 THE NOTICE — Rule 42(a)(1). In open court, or by order to show cause, or
│     │     by arrest order. Must state the time and place of trial, allow reasonable
│     │     time to prepare, state the essential facts, and SAY IT IS CRIMINAL
│     │     CONTEMPT. A probable-cause finding is the usual predicate.
│     │
│     ├─ C3 THE PROSECUTOR — Rule 42(a)(2)                              ⚑ THE CHOKEPOINT
│     │     ├─ the court must FIRST request an attorney for the government, unless
│     │     │     the interest of justice requires another attorney
│     │     ├─ that attorney works for the Department of Justice
│     │     ├─ ◇ where the contemnor is a federal official, the prosecuting office
│     │     │     reports up to the same administration being prosecuted
│     │     └─ ◇ GOVERNMENT DECLINES ──▶ the court MUST appoint another attorney.
│     │           A private prosecutor, paid or pro bono, exercising federal
│     │           prosecutorial power by judicial appointment. Challenged on
│     │           Appointments Clause grounds; cert denied 2023. The provision is
│     │           what keeps the route from being a pure veto — and no court has
│     │           reached it against a federal official in the second term.
│     │
│     ├─ C4 THE TRIAL — Rule 42(a)(3)
│     │     ├─ the referring judge must RECUSE if the contempt involved disrespect
│     │     │     toward or criticism of that judge, unless the defendant consents
│     │     ├─ ◇ JURY?  constitutional floor: a jury is required where the penalty
│     │     │     imposed exceeds the petty-offence line (over six months), because
│     │     │     contempt carries no statutory maximum to measure in advance
│     │     └─ ◇ JURY ON DEMAND, 18 U.S.C. § 3691: yes, where the act is ALSO a crime
│     │           under federal or state law — EXCEPT for contempt of an order in a
│     │           suit "brought by or on behalf of the United States," and except
│     │           contempt in the court's presence.
│     │           ⚑ So the jury right turns on WHO SUED. A private plaintiff's
│     │             injunction is enforced with a jury on demand; the government's
│     │             own injunction is not.
│     │
│     ├─ C5 THE CLOCK — 18 U.S.C. § 3285: a § 402 contempt proceeding must be begun
│     │     within ONE YEAR of the act. A contempt proceeding is also no bar to a
│     │     later ordinary prosecution for the same conduct.
│     │
│     └─ C6 THE EXIT — conviction is a federal criminal conviction, and criminal
│           contempt is a federal offence against the United States, so it is
│           PARDONABLE. The route that punishes rather than coerces therefore ends
│           in the discretion of the officer most likely to have directed the
│           defiance.                                                               ✕
│     [ No criminal-contempt prosecution of a federal official has reached C3 in the
│       second term. J.G.G. got to C2 on 17 Apr 2025 (probable cause, with a stated
│       intention to appoint a Rule 42(a)(2) prosecutor) and was vacated 8 Aug 2025;
│       a 8 Dec 2025 order compelling testimony from two DOJ lawyers was vacated
│       14 Apr 2026 with a direction to TERMINATE the proceedings outright. ]
│
├─◆ ROUTE D. STOP ASKING — PERFORMANCE BY SOMEONE ELSE                           ⚑
│     The only route that does not depend on the defendant's cooperation, because it
│     transfers the duty instead of punishing its breach.
│     ├─ D1 Rule 70(a) ──▶ the court directs the act be done BY ANOTHER PERSON
│     │     appointed by the court, at the disobedient party's expense
│     ├─ D2 Rule 70(b) ──▶ where the act is conveying title, the CLERK's deed or the
│     │     judgment itself does the legal work the party refused to do
│     ├─ D3 Rule 70(d) ──▶ writ of attachment or sequestration against the party's
│     │     property, and a writ of assistance to put the prevailing party in
│     │     possession
│     ├─ D4 THE MONITOR ──▶ a court-appointed outsider with reporting and sometimes
│     │     decision authority inside the institution, reporting to the judge
│     ├─ D5 THE RECEIVER ──▶ operational control removed from the defendant
│     │     altogether. The strongest remedy in the civil toolkit.
│     └─ ◇ APPLIED TO A FEDERAL AGENCY?  ──▶ not yet. Every modern example is a
│           state or municipal institution, where the Eleventh Amendment problem is
│           different and there is no coequal-branch objection.
│     [ WORKED EXAMPLE: the New York City jails. Receivership ordered 13 May 2025;
│       a "remediation manager" named 27 Jan 2026 with authority to act independently
│       of the mayor, reporting to the district judge rather than the city, budgeted
│       at roughly $10m for the following year. Eight months from order to a named
│       person with a budget — which is why this route is a remedy for institutions
│       and not for a single defied deadline. ]
│
├─◆ ROUTE E. MANDAMUS — compel the ministerial act
│     28 U.S.C. § 1361 (action in the nature of mandamus) and § 1651 (All Writs Act).
│     ├─ reaches only duties that are NONDISCRETIONARY and purely ministerial —
│     │     disbursing money owed, issuing a document, performing a step the statute
│     │     leaves no judgment about
│     ├─ does NOT reach a discretionary judgment, however badly exercised
│     └─ ◇ its real use in 2025-26 has been the OTHER WAY: the government using
│           mandamus in the court of appeals to stop a district judge's contempt
│           inquiry before any charge existed. Both J.G.G. terminations were
│           mandamus.                                                                ⚑
│
└─◆ ROUTE F. THE LAWYERS AND THE BILL — no contempt required
      ├─ F1 Rule 11 (FRCP) ──▶ sanctions for filings made for an improper purpose,
      │     including unnecessary delay. Reaches the attorney, not the policy.
      ├─ F2 Rule 37 ──▶ discovery sanctions, up to adverse-inference and default
      ├─ F3 FRAP 46 ──▶ appellate discipline: fine, suspension, disbarment, for
      │     conduct unbecoming a member of the bar or failure to comply with a rule
      ├─ F4 referral to the state bar or to the court's own disciplinary panel
      ├─ F5 FEE-SHIFTING ──▶ 28 U.S.C. § 2412, the Equal Access to Justice Act: fees
      │     against the United States where the government's position was not
      │     substantially justified. Congress HAS waived immunity here, which is why
      │     fees are the one money route that actually collects.
      └─ F6 ADVERSE PROCEDURAL CONSEQUENCE ──▶ waiver of unfiled arguments, striking
            a pleading, deeming facts admitted. Costs the government the case rather
            than money, and needs no sanction finding at all.

══ THE ENFORCEMENT ARM ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
   28 U.S.C. § 561 ──▶ the United States Marshals Service is a BUREAU OF THE
   DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE. The Director is appointed by the President with Senate
   confirmation and reports to the Attorney General. The Marshals serve the courts'
   process, execute their orders, and make the arrests — and a marshal asked to
   arrest a federal official for contempt of an order against the executive is an
   employee of the executive.
   └─▶ There is no judicial police force. The courts' bailiff is the defendant's
        subordinate, by statute, and that is the structural fact every route above
        eventually runs into.

══ WHAT CONGRESS TRIED, AND HOW ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
   The 2025 reconciliation bill reported by the House Judiciary Committee on
   29 Apr 2025 contained a provision barring any court of the United States from
   using APPROPRIATED FUNDS to enforce a contempt citation for failure to comply with
   an injunction or temporary restraining order "if no security was given when the
   injunction or order was issued pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 65(c)" —
   and it applied to orders issued before, on, or after enactment.
   ├─ Rule 65(c) security is almost never required, and essentially never required of
   │     a plaintiff suing the government, so the provision would have switched off
   │     contempt enforcement for nearly every injunction against the United States
   │     then in existence.
   ├─ ⚑ THE METHOD IS THE POINT: it repealed nothing. It left § 401, Rule 42 and the
   │     contempt power intact and defunded the enforcement of the result — an
   │     appropriations rider doing the work of a jurisdictional statute.
   ├─ a court could have defeated it prospectively by setting a $1 bond
   └─ ✕ ruled out of the reconciliation vehicle by the Senate parliamentarian under
         the Byrd rule in June 2025; NOT in the enacted Act.

══ WHAT NONE OF THESE ROUTES REACHES ═════════════════════════════════════════════════
   ├─ THE PRESIDENT PERSONALLY. No federal court has held a sitting president in
   │     contempt. The routes above run against agencies, departments, officials and
   │     attorneys.
   ├─ COMPLIANCE THAT IS SLOW RATHER THAN REFUSED. Every route needs a violation to
   │     point at; "processing, but not yet" is the hardest posture to charge, and it
   │     is the posture that produced an extension instead of a sanction in 2026.
   ├─ THE OFFICIAL WHO SET THE POLICY. Civil contempt reaches whoever is in the
   │     courtroom — usually a line attorney. The policy-maker is reachable only by
   │     criminal contempt, which is the route that does not work.
   ├─ MONEY FROM THE TREASURY, absent a waiver. Fee-shifting collects; contempt fines
   │     against the United States do not.
   └─ THE ORDER THAT WAS NEVER CLEAR ENOUGH. After stage 0, the drafting of the
         original injunction is itself an enforcement decision, made months before
         anyone defies anything.
