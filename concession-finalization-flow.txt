HOW A CONCEDED ELECTION IS FINALIZED — the law has no concept of a concession,
and the one lever it does have is a recount waiver        (as of 11 October 2026)

│           ⊘ no legal effect, or a provision that does not exist
│           ◇ who decides   ✱ the structural point   ⚑ live in 2026

│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: THE ONLY FEDERAL ANSWER ON RECORD TO "WHAT IS THE LEGAL
│  EFFECT OF A CONCESSION SPEECH?" IS THE WORD "NONE." Everything below is that
│  word elaborated across six machines — and the ONE place where something does
│  move, which is not a concession at all but a separate written instrument with
│  its own deadline. Read Part C before trusting any account of a "waived"
│  recount, including the 2024 Pennsylvania one.
│
■ PART A — WHAT A CONCESSION IS
│
├─▸ A PRESS EVENT. A candidate says, in public or by telephone, that he lost.
│     That is the whole of it.
│     ⊘ NOT a withdrawal of candidacy — the name stays on the certified return
│     ⊘ NOT a waiver of any remedy — recount, contest and appeal all survive it
│     ⊘ NOT a filing, a trigger, or a deadline of any kind
│     ⊘ NOT required — no state or federal law obliges any candidate to concede
│     ◇ decided by: the candidate alone, at a time of his own choosing
│
├─▸ THE ONLY FEDERAL STATEMENT ON RECORD
│     NATIONAL ARCHIVES, Electoral College FAQ:
│       Q. "What is the legal effect of a concession speech?"
│       A. "None. A candidate's concession speech does not impact the States'
│           duties and responsibilities related to the Electoral College
│           process."
│     ✱ THE QUESTION IS ASKED OFTEN ENOUGH THAT THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT PUT IT IN
│       AN FAQ, AND THE ANSWER IS ONE WORD.
│
└─▸ HOW OFTEN IT HAPPENS  ◈ measured, not statutory — see Note 1
      Every defeated major-party House and Senate runner-up, 2020 and 2022 —
      884 races, 31 non-major-party runners-up excluded:
        CONCEDED .................... 65%    (Senate 77% · House 62%)
        EXPLICITLY REFUSED .......... 8%
        NO PUBLIC STATEMENT ......... 31%
      by year:   conceded  63% (2020) → 68% (2022)
                 refused   11% / 47 candidates (2020) → 4% / 18 (2022)
      by party, both cycles:   14% of defeated Republicans refused
                                1% of defeated Democrats refused
      ✱ A THIRD OF DEFEATED CANDIDATES SAY NOTHING AT ALL. The non-concession
        that gets written about is the 8%; the silent 31% is four times larger
        and has exactly the same legal effect, which is none.

■ PART B — THE SIX MACHINES, AND WHAT A CONCESSION DOES TO EACH
│
├─▸ 1. THE CANVASS AND CERTIFICATION .................. ⊘ NOTHING
│     The board counts what the returns say and certifies. Courts call the duty
│     MINISTERIAL — an obligation "involving merely the execution of a set task"
│     where the law "prescribes and defines the time, mode an[d] occasion for its
│     performance with such certainty that nothing remains for judgment or
│     discretion."  [Meyers v. Schultz]
│     MICHIGAN wrote it into its constitution after the 2020 Wayne County
│     refusals: it is "the ministerial, clerical, nondiscretionary duty of a
│     board of canvassers, and of each individual member thereof, to certify
│     election results based solely on" the certified statements of votes.
│     [Mich. Const. art. II § 7(3) — adopted 8 Nov 2022, 59.99% to 40.01%]
│     ✱ A BOARD MAY NOT CERTIFY EARLY, DIFFERENTLY, OR NOT AT ALL BECAUSE A
│       CANDIDATE CONCEDED. The concession is not on the face of the returns,
│       and the face of the returns is all the board may look at.
│     → if it stalls: mandamus, or a statutory petition — New Mexico lets ANY
│       VOTER petition for an order compelling certification
│
├─▸ 2. THE AUTOMATIC RECOUNT ..................... ⚡ THE ONE LIVE LANE
│     Fires on a margin, by operation of law, in 26 STATES AND D.C.
│     ⊘ a concession does not stop it, delay it, or narrow it
│     ⚡ BUT a separate written instrument — a RECOUNT WAIVER — does, in the
│       states that provide one. Part C is that provision. Part D is what
│       happened in the one state whose window had already shut.
│
├─▸ 3. THE ELECTION CONTEST ........................... ⊘ NOTHING, DIRECTLY
│     A lawsuit with its own clock, running from certification in Florida,
│     Colorado, North Carolina and Virginia § 24.2-805.
│     ⊘ no state's contest statute mentions concession
│     ✱ BUT SEE PART E. In a state where only the losing CANDIDATE may file, a
│       concession is functionally decisive — the one person with standing has
│       publicly abandoned the claim and nobody may take it up.
│
├─▸ 4. THE PARTY'S OWN LITIGATION ..................... ⊘ NOTHING
│     A party committee's suits over ballot rules, undated envelopes and
│     provisional counts are its own. The candidate's concession neither moots
│     them nor withdraws them; they end when the party dismisses them or the
│     relief sought becomes unavailable.
│
├─▸ 5. SEATING IN CONGRESS ............................ ⊘ NOTHING
│     U.S. Const. art. I § 5 cl. 1 makes each chamber the judge of its own
│     members' elections. A conceded race still produces a certificate, and the
│     chamber still seats — or declines to seat — on its own motion.
│
└─▸ 6. THE ELECTORAL COLLEGE  (presidential years) .... ⊘ NOTHING
      The states appoint electors on the certified result and the electors meet
      on the statutory date. Per the National Archives, a concession "does not
      impact the States' duties and responsibilities."
│
■ PART C — THE RECOUNT WAIVER: THE ONLY LEGALLY OPERATIVE SHADOW
│
│  ✱ THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION OF THIS MAP. There is no legal act called
│    "conceding." There IS a legal act called requesting in writing that the
│    recount not be made, and it is the only thing a losing candidate can do
│    that changes the machinery. It is not a concession, it does not require
│    one, and a concession does not accomplish it.
│
├─▸ PENNSYLVANIA .... "A recount and recanvass shall not be ordered under
│                     subsection (g)(1)(i) if the [defeated candidate] requests
│                     in writing that a recount and recanvass not be made."
│                     ⚠ DEADLINE: "by 12 o'clock noon on the second Wednesday
│                     following the election."   [25 P.S. § 3154(h)]
│                     ✱ THE ONLY HARD WAIVER DEADLINE FOUND. See Part D.
│                     ⊘ no waiver for a BALLOT QUESTION under (g)(1)(ii) — the
│                       provision reaches candidate races only
│
├─▸ FLORIDA ......... "A recount need not be ordered with respect to the returns
│                     for any office, however, if the candidate or candidates
│                     defeated or eliminated from contention for such office by
│                     one-half of a percent or less of the votes cast for such
│                     office request in writing that a recount not be made."
│                     [Fla. Stat. § 102.141(7)]
│                     ⊘ NO DEADLINE stated in the subsection
│
├─▸ NEBRASKA ........ "Any losing candidate may waive his or her right to a
│                     recount by filing a written statement" with the Secretary
│                     of State, election commissioner or county clerk with whom
│                     the candidate filed.   [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-1119(2)]
│                     ⊘ NO DEADLINE stated
│
├─▸ ARIZONA ......... ⊘ NO WAIVER PROVISION FOUND. § 16-661 says a recount "is
│                     required" where the margin "is less than or equal to
│                     one-half of one percent" and says nothing about declining
│                     it; in practice the Secretary of State petitions a superior
│                     court and a judge orders the recount.
│                     [A.R.S. § 16-661; 2024 AZ-03 Democratic primary]
│
└─▸ ✱ THE SHAPE: WHERE THE WAIVER EXISTS IT IS A WRITING, NOT A SPEECH — AND ITS
      DEADLINE IS THE VARIABLE NOBODY TRACKS. Of the four states worked above,
      two set no deadline at all, one sets noon on a named day, and one offers no
      waiver. ◈ NO FIFTY-STATE TABLE OF RECOUNT-WAIVER DEADLINES WAS LOCATED.
      Every published recount survey counts triggers, thresholds and completion
      dates; the waiver is one sentence inside the section and goes unsurveyed.
      See Note 2.
│
■ PART D — ⚑ PENNSYLVANIA 2024: A MANDATORY RECOUNT ENDED AFTER ITS OWN WAIVER
│           WINDOW HAD CLOSED
│
│  THE CLOCK, as the statute sets it:
│    TUE  5 NOV 2024 ... election day
│    WED 13 NOV, NOON .. ⚠ STATUTORY WAIVER DEADLINE — the last moment a defeated
│                        candidate may request that the recount not be made
│                        [25 P.S. § 3154(h)]
│    WED 13 NOV ........ the Secretary of the Commonwealth ORDERS the statewide
│                        recount; the margin is inside 0.5%
│                        [outer limit: 5 p.m., the second Thursday — 14 Nov]
│    …the recount runs in all 67 counties; several finish…
│    THU 21 NOV ........ the defeated candidate CONCEDES, and his campaign asks
│                        the Department of State to halt the recount
│    FRI 22 NOV ........ the Department emails the counties that offices which
│                        had not finished the Senate recount were "not required
│                        to do so"
│    MON 25 NOV ........ county certification deadline
│
├─▸ ✱ THE FINDING: THE CONCESSION CAME EIGHT DAYS AFTER THE ONLY DOOR THE
│     STATUTE LEAVES OPEN HAD CLOSED. The § 3154(h) waiver expired at noon on
│     13 November — the same day the recount was ordered. The request to stop
│     arrived on 21–22 November. By then the recount was not waivable; it was a
│     mandatory proceeding already under way.
│
├─▸ ⊘ NO STATUTE WAS CITED. The Department's public statement names no section
│     of the Election Code. The stated reason was that continuing "would not be
│     in the best interest of taxpayers." † The press coverage uniformly
│     describes a candidate waiving or calling off the recount; the statutory
│     waiver had lapsed, so whatever this was, it was not that.
│
└─▸ ✱ WHAT THE EPISODE ACTUALLY SHOWS. A concession has no legal effect — and it
      ended a statutorily mandated statewide recount anyway, through an
      administrative decision the Election Code does not provide for, which
      nobody challenged, because the one person with the standing and the motive
      to challenge it had just conceded. The distance between what a concession
      does in law and what it does in fact is this entire map.
│
■ PART E — STANDING: WHO IS LEFT AFTER THE CANDIDATE CONCEDES
│
├─▸ VIRGINIA — THE CLOSED DOOR
│     § 24.2-807 is titled "Contest only on complaint of unsuccessful party."
│     "The contest shall be initiated only by a written complaint of one or more
│     of the unsuccessful candidates."
│     ✱ WHEN A VIRGINIA CANDIDATE CONCEDES, THE ONLY PEOPLE IN THE COMMONWEALTH
│       WITH STANDING TO CONTEST HAVE PUBLICLY ABANDONED THE CLAIM, AND NO
│       VOTER, PARTY OR TAXPAYER MAY REVIVE IT. The statute never mentions
│       concession and does not need to.
│
├─▸ FLORIDA — THE OPEN DOOR
│     § 102.168(1): the certification may be contested "by any unsuccessful
│     candidate for such office or nomination thereto, or by any elector
│     qualified to vote in the election related to such candidacy, or by any
│     taxpayer, respectively."
│     deadline — within 10 DAYS after midnight of the date the LAST board
│                responsible for certifying the results does so  [§ 102.168(2)]
│     grounds  — misconduct, fraud or corruption by an election official ·
│                ineligibility of the winner · illegal votes received or legal
│                votes rejected · bribery of an elector or official
│                [§ 102.168(3)]
│     ✱ A FLORIDA CANDIDATE'S CONCESSION EXTINGUISHES NOTHING. A qualified
│       elector who has never met him may file the contest he declined to bring.
│
└─▸ ✱ THE SAME PRESS EVENT ENDS THE REMEDY IN ONE STATE AND NOT IN THE OTHER,
      AND NEITHER STATUTE SAYS A WORD ABOUT CONCESSIONS. This is the mechanism
      by which a legally weightless act becomes legally decisive: not through any
      rule about concessions, but through a rule about who may sue.
      ◈ The full forum-and-standing picture is the contest crosswalk, linked
        below. This part is only the half of it that a concession touches.

■ PART F — THE TWO WITHDRAWN CONCESSIONS, AND WHY BOTH WERE FREE
│
├─▸ 2000 — THE PRESIDENTIAL RACE
│     On election night, 7 November, the losing nominee telephones his opponent
│     and concedes. Before any public speech he is told Florida is inside the
│     automatic-recount margin — a lead of 1,784 votes, under 0.5% — and
│     TELEPHONES AGAIN TO RETRACT. The machine recount begins the next day.
│     The final concession comes 13 DECEMBER 2000, the day after the Supreme
│     Court's ruling ended the recount.
│     ✱ THE RETRACTION COST NOTHING, BECAUSE NOTHING HAD ATTACHED TO THE FIRST
│       CALL. There was no instrument to rescind.
│
├─▸ 2018 — THE FLORIDA GOVERNORSHIP
│     Conceded on election night, Tuesday 6 November. By Saturday 10 November
│     the margin — about 0.41% — had ordered a statewide machine recount, and
│     the concession was withdrawn in terms worth quoting: "I am replacing my
│     words of concession with an uncompromised and unapologetic call that we
│     count every single vote." Conceded again 17 November, after the recount.
│     ✱ AGAIN FREE. The recount had been ordered by operation of law on the
│       margin; the concession neither triggered it nor could have stopped it.
│
└─▸ ✱ THE PATTERN: A CONCESSION IS COSTLESS TO WITHDRAW PRECISELY BECAUSE IT IS
      LEGALLY WEIGHTLESS, and both known withdrawals fall in the same window —
      between election night and a margin-triggered recount, when the machinery
      is running on its own and the candidate's words are not an input to it.
      ⊘ NO REPORTED CASE WAS FOUND holding a candidate estopped by a concession,
      in any state. [unconfirmed whether one exists — see Note 5]

■ PART G — ⚑ WHAT IS LIVE FOR 3 NOVEMBER 2026
│
├─▸ THE PLEDGE THAT EXISTS BECAUSE THE LAW DOES NOT. Reported 8 October 2026: a
│     bipartisan House pledge to accept this November's results and to oppose
│     efforts to block fellow members from being seated in the next Congress.
│     ⊘ VOLUNTARY AND UNENFORCEABLE — no resolution, no rule, no sanction. It is
│       the concession norm written down, which is the strongest form the norm
│       has ever taken and is still not law.
│     ◇ the risk it addresses: objections to seating members on 3 JANUARY 2027
│       under art. I § 5, which is the one machine in Part B that a chamber
│       controls outright. One prominent election-law scholar has called the
│       scenario "very far-fetched."

└─▸ ⊘ WHAT THIS MAP DOES NOT PREDICT. Whether the 8% refusal rate rises or falls

      mandatory recount after its waiver window has closed. The structure above
      is what each of those would run into.
