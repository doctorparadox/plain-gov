■ 0. THE TWO THINGS EVERYONE CALLS "A CLEARANCE"
│
│     ┌─────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│     │  ELIGIBILITY                │  ACCESS                                       │
│     ├─────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│     │  Confidential / Secret /    │  Read-in to a specific compartment or program │
│     │  Top Secret                 │  SCI (by compartment) · SAP (by program)      │
│     │  An ADJUDICATED STATUS      │  A PERMISSION, granted by the owning agency   │
│     │  Investigated, decided,     │  Requires eligibility + need-to-know + an     │
│     │  recorded, PORTABLE between │  NdA; the agency may also impose a polygraph  │
│     │  agencies by reciprocity    │  NOT a higher clearance level                 │
│     │  (SEAD 7)                   │                                               │
│     │  ★ carries the § 5.2 due-   │  ✕ carries NO procedure. Withdrawn at will,   │
│     │    process package below    │    no cause stated, no appeal, same day       │
│     └─────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
│
│     THIS SPLIT IS THE WHOLE MAP. Everything procedural in stages 3-5 protects the
│     left column. The right column is where an agency acts when it wants a result.

■ 1. ⚙ THE POLICY STACK — WHO WRITES THE RULES
│     ├─ EO 13526 (2009) ..... classifies the INFORMATION: three levels, original vs.
│     │                        derivative authority, declassification tracks
│     ├─ EO 12968 (1995) ..... governs ACCESS by persons; § 5.2 = the review
│     │                        procedures on denial/revocation
│     │     └─▶ § 5.2(e): agency head or principal deputy may personally certify that
│     │          a procedure cannot be provided without damaging national security.
│     │          "This certification shall be conclusive."          [the inside switch]
│     ├─ EO 10865 (1960) ..... the INDUSTRY track — cleared contractor personnel
│     ├─ EO 13467 (2008) ..... names the two executive agents:
│     │     ├─ SECURITY EXECUTIVE AGENT ......... the DNI  (government-wide, not just IC)
│     │     │     staffed by NCSC's Special Security Directorate
│     │     │     Jay Clayton, sworn in Aug. 3, 2026 — 9th DNI   [ODNI PR 12-26]
│     │     └─ SUITABILITY & CREDENTIALING EXEC AGENT ... the Director of OPM
│     └─ SEAD SERIES ......... the SecEA's binding directives:
│           SEAD 3 reporting requirements · SEAD 4 the 13 adjudicative guidelines
│           SEAD 6 continuous evaluation · SEAD 7 reciprocity · SEAD 8 temporary
│           eligibility
│
│     NOTE THE SHAPE: not one statute. 50 U.S.C. § 3341 touches the process at the
│     edges; the substance is executive orders and directives the executive writes,
│     interprets and may certify around.
│
├──▶ 2. THE GRANT TRACK
│     │
│     ├─ 2a. SPONSORSHIP — no one applies for a clearance. A federal agency or a
│     │        cleared contractor with a classified contract must sponsor a named
│     │        person against a specific position.       ◇ no sponsor ─▶ no process ✕
│     ├─ 2b. SF-86 — the Questionnaire for National Security Positions, filed in
│     │        e-QIP or NBIS. Omissions here become Guideline E problems later.
│     ├─ 2c. INVESTIGATION — conducted for ~95% of the government by DCSA
│     │        ├─ Tier 3 ... Secret / Confidential, non-critical-sensitive
│     │        └─ Tier 5 ... Top Secret and SCI-eligible, critical-sensitive
│     │        [DCSA industry data, FY2026 Q1, fastest 90% of cases, end to end:
│     │         about 156 days Secret, about 227 days Top Secret]
│     │        [NBIS, the system meant to replace the legacy stack, is years late:
│     │         DoD projects full deployment FY2027, legacy retirement by FY2028]
│     ├─ 2d. ADJUDICATION — a federal adjudicator, never the investigator, applies
│     │        SEAD 4's THIRTEEN GUIDELINES under the whole-person concept:
│     │          A allegiance ....... B foreign influence .... C foreign preference
│     │          D sexual behavior .. E personal conduct ..... F financial
│     │          G alcohol .......... H drugs ................ I psychological
│     │          J criminal conduct . K handling protected info L outside activities
│     │          M use of information technology
│     │        Each guideline lists DISQUALIFYING conditions and MITIGATING ones.
│     │        There is no automatic bar. There is also no burden on the government:
│     │        doubt is resolved in favor of national security, not the applicant.
│     │        ├─yes─▶ ★ ELIGIBILITY GRANTED, recorded in the system of record
│     │        ├─?───▶ SEAD 8 TEMPORARY / INTERIM eligibility — granted on partial
│     │        │        results, revocable with no process at all  [see Route Out 2]
│     │        └─no──▶ go to ROUTE OUT 1 (the SOR track), below
│     └─ 2e. RECIPROCITY (SEAD 7) — a current, in-scope eligibility is supposed to
│              transfer between agencies without re-investigation.  [in practice the
│              receiving agency's ACCESS decision is separate and is not reciprocal —
│              which is how a "portable" clearance stops at a door]
│
├──▶ 3. THE ACCESS LAYER — where the job actually happens
│     ├─ need-to-know determined by the information owner, not by the clearance
│     ├─ SCI read-in, compartment by compartment (ODNI-controlled caveats)
│     ├─ SAP read-in, program by program; access rosters may themselves be classified
│     ├─ Nondisclosure Agreement signed (SF-312 or program-specific)
│     └─ agency-imposed conditions: CI-scope or full-scope polygraph, foreign-travel
│           reporting, foreign-contact reporting (SEAD 3)
│
├──▶ 4. THE STANDING LOOP — CONTINUOUS VETTING (SEAD 6)
│     │   Replaced the 5- and 10-year periodic reinvestigation under Trusted
│     │   Workforce 2.0. The file is no longer reopened on a calendar; it is never
│     │   closed.
│     ├─ automated record checks run against the enrolled population: criminal,
│     │    financial, terrorism, foreign travel, public records
│     ├─ SEAD 3 self-reporting obligations run alongside — and FAILING TO REPORT is
│     │    itself a Guideline E concern, which has revoked clearances the underlying
│     │    event alone would not have
│     ├─ [74% of the vetted federal workforce enrolled at FY2026 Q2; about 1.13 new
│     │    actionable alerts per quarter per 100 enrollees at FY2026 Q1]
│     ├─ [19 May 2026: DCSA replaced the NISP contractor periodic reinvestigation
│     │    with a five-year questionnaire]
│     └─▶ an alert routes to an adjudicator ──▶ resolved and closed
│            └─▶ or ──▶ ROUTE OUT 1
│
■ 5. THE THREE DOORS OUT — each with less process than the last
│
├──▶ ROUTE OUT 1 — ADJUDICATIVE REVOCATION      the one with a procedure
│     ├─ 1. suspension of access pending resolution — immediate, and not itself
│     │       appealable; the person is out of the building on day one
│     ├─ 2. STATEMENT OF REASONS (SOR) — written, guideline by guideline
│     ├─ 3. WRITTEN REPLY [20 days in the DoD industrial track]
│     ├─ 4. ◇ FORUM SPLIT — same statute, two different machines:
│     │       ├─ CONTRACTOR (EO 10865 / DoD Dir. 5220.6) ─▶ DOHA administrative
│     │       │     judge, an adversarial HEARING on the record, with a transcript
│     │       │     └─▶ appeal to the three-judge DOHA APPEAL BOARD [15 days],
│     │       │          which reviews for procedure and defers on credibility
│     │       └─ FEDERAL EMPLOYEE / MILITARY (EO 12968 § 5.2) ─▶ a PERSONAL
│     │             APPEARANCE before an adjudicative authority, NOT a hearing:
│     │             no subpoena, no cross-examination, no neutral record
│     │             └─▶ appeal to the agency's own high-level panel
│     │                  (in DoD, the Personnel Security Appeal Board)
│     ├─ 5. ◇ § 5.2(e) CERTIFICATION — at any point, the agency head or principal
│     │       deputy may certify a procedure away. Conclusive by its own terms. ✕
│     └─ 6. FINAL AGENCY DECISION ──▶ into the ceiling at stage 6
│
├──▶ ROUTE OUT 2 — ACCESS WITHDRAWAL              no cause, no procedure, same day
│     ├─ read-off from the SCI compartment or the SAP; eligibility untouched
│     ├─ need-to-know simply declared absent
│     ├─ interim/temporary eligibility withdrawn (SEAD 8 carries no review right)
│     └─ RESULT: the person cannot do the job, holds a clearance on paper, and has
│           nothing to appeal — there is no adverse eligibility action to contest.
│           [This is the door that does the work. It generates no SOR and therefore
│            no record, which is one reason the population of people removed this
│            way cannot be counted from outside.]
│
└──▶ ROUTE OUT 3 — PRESIDENTIAL DIRECTIVE                bypasses 1 and 2 entirely
      ├─ EO or presidential memorandum names individuals and directs agency heads
      │     to revoke and rescind access
      ├─ the § 5.2 package does not attach: EO 12968 binds AGENCY HEADS, and the
      │     President's authority over access is constitutional, not conferred by
      │     the order that describes it — so he is not bound by its procedures
      ├─ [20 Jan 2025 — EO revoking the clearances of the 51 signatories of the
      │     October 2020 "laptop letter"]
      ├─ [Mar. 2025 — presidential memorandum naming 15 individuals incl. attorney
      │     Mark Zaid]
      ├─ [Aug. 2025 — announced revocation for 37 current and former national
      │     security officials]
      └─▶ the only live test: CHALLENGE THE MOTIVE, NOT THE MERITS
            ├─ Zaid v. Executive Office of the President (D.D.C.) — 23 Dec. 2025,
            │     motion to dismiss DENIED, preliminary injunction GRANTED on First
            │     Amendment, due process and right-to-counsel grounds
            │     └─▶ D.C. Circuit No. 26-5009, argued 14 May 2026
            │          [no decision as of this build — 18 Sept. 2026]
            └─ other district courts have gone the other way, holding a revocation
                  unreviewable even when pleaded as a pretextual ideological purge

■ 6. THE CEILING — DEPARTMENT OF THE NAVY v. EGAN, 484 U.S. 518 (1988)
      ├─ HOLDING: the grant of a clearance is a sensitive, discretionary judgment
      │     committed by law to the executive; absent a specific grant by Congress,
      │     no outside non-expert body may review the MERITS
      ├─ WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY: that the procedures are unreviewable, that the
      │     First Amendment stops at the door, or that Congress could not legislate
      ├─ CONSEQUENCE: revocation is an instrument that needs no cause a court will
      │     examine. The only questions left justiciable are procedural and
      │     motivational — and § 5.2(e) is aimed squarely at the procedural half.
      └─ [50 U.S.C. § 3341 is the closest Congress has come to the "specific grant"
            Egan left open. It sets timeliness goals and reporting duties. It does
            not create a right of review.]
