ELECTION BALLOT CHAIN OF CUSTODY FLOW                             (as of 3 October 2026)
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path   └─▶ branch/consequence   ◇ decision   ⚑ load-bearing
│            ⊞ control living only in a procedure manual, not in statute
│            ✂ a LAWFUL break: outside legal authority takes custody and the
│              custody rules provide no answer
│            ⊘ obligation stated with no remedy attached   ◈ undiagrammed stage
│   Authority: one federal rule, and it is not an election statute. 52 U.S.C.
│            § 20701 (Civil Rights Act of 1960 § 301) commands 22-month retention
│            of federal election records on penalty of $1,000 and one year.
│            Everything else on this map is state statute, state administrative
│            rule, or a county procedure manual.
│   The four artifacts, repeated at every single transfer below:
│            SEAL (numbered) · SEAL LOG · TRANSFER FORM (two signatures)
│            · ACCESS LOG
│
■ 1. MANUFACTURE AND PRE-ELECTION STORAGE   ⚑ the chain starts before any voter
│     Ballots are printed on controlled stock and stored before anyone votes.
│     ├─ Security paper: watermark or barcode stock required by statute in some
│     │   states (e.g. Ga. Code § 21-2-372).
│     ├─ Video surveillance of the print facility (e.g. Ohio Admin. Code
│     │   111:3-10-10) · continuous on-site monitoring by a designated official
│     │   (e.g. Va. Code § 24.2-617).
│     ├─ ⊞ WAREHOUSE ACCESS LOG — every entry and exit, every time unfinished or
│     │   finished ballot stock is on the premises (e.g. Cal. Code Regs. tit. 2
│     │   § 20253). This is administrative rule, not statute, almost everywhere.
│     └─ INVENTORY LOG: how many were printed. Stage 7 cannot reconcile against
│         anything if this number was never written down.
│
├─▶ 2. LOGIC AND ACCURACY TESTING, AND THE SEAL THAT FOLLOWS IT   ⚑
│     Before the equipment can be used, a test deck with a known result is run
│     through each tabulator and the output compared to what it should be.
│     ├─ Hardware diagnostic, input/output devices, communications ports, and
│     │   display of the firmware or software hash (MD5 or SHA-1) in the more
│     │   prescriptive states (e.g. Colo. Code Regs. § 1505-1-11.3.1).
│     ├─ ──▶ THE MACHINE IS THEN SEALED, the seal number recorded, and the
│     │      documentation retained. The seal is the whole point of the test: it
│     │      converts "this machine counted correctly on Tuesday" into "and
│     │      nothing has been opened since."
│     └─ ⊞ No federal statute requires the test, specifies the test deck, or says
│         who may watch. State codes generally provide for at least one public
│         test; the deck design and the observer rules are manual-level.
│
├─▶ 3. DISTRIBUTION TO THE POLLING PLACE   ⚑ first signed hand-off
│     Sealed packages go out, marked on the outside with the precinct and the
│     count, and the receiving judges SIGN A RECEIPT that comes back.
│     ├─ e.g. Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 16-509 (sealed packages, receipts returned);
│     │   25 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 3044 (county board keeps a record of the time and
│     │   manner of each delivery).
│     └─ ◈ The round trip is the control, not the delivery: the number sent must
│         equal the number returned plus the number voted plus the number spoiled.
│         Almost nobody draws this as a loop, and it is one.
│
├─▶ 4. THE VOTING PERIOD   ⚑ three populations of ballot in three custody regimes
│     │   The single most misunderstood stage. "The ballots" are never one pile.
│     │
│     ├─ (a) IN-PERSON, at the precinct.
│     │       ──▶ equipment secured with access limited to authorized personnel
│     │            (e.g. Kan. Admin. Regs. § 7-21-1); a board member periodically
│     │            examines the device face for damage or tampering (e.g. Fla.
│     │            Stat. § 101.5613).
│     │
│     ├─ (b) THE DROP BOX — custody with no election officer present at the
│     │       moment of deposit, which is why the retrieval rule carries it.
│     │       ──▶ emptied by two people of different party affiliation, at a
│     │            stated minimum frequency, with a record of the date, the time
│     │            and the NAMES of the persons removing them (e.g. Ky. Rev.
│     │            Stat. § 117.086(f)).
│     │       ──▶ hardware where the statute reaches it: bolted to a foundation,
│     │            24-hour video surveillance, constructed so ballots cannot be
│     │            removed without tampering being evident (e.g. Vt. Stat. tit.
│     │            17 § 2543A).
│     │
│     └─ (c) MAIL AND CENTRAL COUNT — the envelope is in the postal system, which
│             is a custody regime belonging to a federal agency and appearing on
│             no election-office seal log at all.
│             └─▶ ◈ Custody of a mail ballot begins, for charting purposes, at
│                  receipt. What happened before receipt is not in the chain and
│                  cannot be put there.
│
├─▶ 5. PRECINCT CLOSE AND THE SEAL   ⚑ the last moment paper and number coexist
│     Polls close, the tabulator prints the results tape, the voted ballots go
│     into a container, and the container is SEALED with a numbered seal written
│     onto a chain-of-custody form and signed.
│     ├─ e.g. Tex. Elec. Code § 127.066: the presiding judge, an election clerk
│     │   and up to two watchers SIGN THE SEAL, and the box is then delivered to
│     │   the central counting station BY TWO ELECTION OFFICERS.
│     └─▶ From here two artifacts travel on different rules: the PAPER, which is
│          evidence and is retained 22 months under § 20701, and the ELECTRONIC
│          TOTALS, which are a report. They are not required to travel together,
│          and usually do not — see How Election Results Are Transmitted and
│          Reported, which follows the second one.
│
├─▶ 6. ◇ TRANSPORT TO THE COUNTY   ⚑ the stage with the sharpest state variation
│     │
│     ├─ (a) TWO-PERSON BIPARTISAN TRANSPORT — the common rule: two officials of
│     │       different major parties, BY THE MOST DIRECT ROUTE (e.g. Wis. Stat.
│     │       § 5.85; S.D. Codified Laws § 12-17B-9).
│     │
│     ├─ (b) LAW-ENFORCEMENT ESCORT — a minority rule, and a notable one given
│     │       stage 11: at least one member of the county sheriff's department
│     │       accompanies the ballot cards, which are at all times under at least
│     │       two persons, one from each of two parties (Ind. Code
│     │       § 3-12-3-2.5(d)).
│     │
│     └─ (c) SPLIT-KEY CONTROL — neither custodian can open the receptacle alone:
│             the clerk retains one key and gives the other to a board member not
│             of the clerk's party (e.g. Ind. Code § 3-11.5-6-25).
│
├─▶ 7. RECEIPT AND RECONCILIATION   ⚑ the only arithmetic check in the chain
│     The seal number on the container is checked against the form that travelled
│     with it, custody transfers by signature, and the counts are reconciled.
│     ├─ Unused ballots + spoiled before delivery + spoiled and returned by voters
│     │   + affidavit ballots cast, added to ballots cast, recorded on a BALLOT
│     │   RECONCILIATION FORM (e.g. N.Y. Elec. Law § 9-106).
│     ├─ Delivered-to-precinct against returned-from-precinct, and a discrepancy
│     │   that cannot be reconciled MUST BE NOTED (e.g. Minn. Stat. § 204C.28).
│     └─ ⊘ "Must be noted" is the entire remedy. The note goes in the file. No
│         statute surveyed here gives any person outside the office a procedure
│         to compel an explanation of a noted discrepancy. The canvass may catch
│         it; nothing makes anyone look.
│
├─▶ 8. THE TABULATION ROOM   ⚑ the access list is the control
│     ├─ Proceedings open to the public, but NO PERSON except those employed and
│     │   authorized may TOUCH a ballot (e.g. Wis. Stat. § 5.87). Observation and
│     │   custody are deliberately separated: you may watch, never handle.
│     ├─ ⊞ Physical and electronic access to the election management system
│     │   limited to persons the chief elections authority authorizes, on
│     │   individual password-protected accounts — and in the strictest states a
│     │   hard cap: no more than FOUR administrative user accounts per election
│     │   (Colo. Code Regs. § 1505-1-20.5.2(c)(7)).
│     └─ REAL-TIME AUDIT LOG on the central accumulator: logins and logouts,
│         precincts zeroed, ballots read per precinct, reports generated, every
│         error and warning, each date- and time-stamped (1 Tex. Admin. Code
│         § 81.62). The machine keeps its own access log; see Election Management
│         System.
│
├─▶ 9. ◇ A SEAL NUMBER DOES NOT MATCH   ⚑ what the chain is for
│     │   Every control above exists to make this branch reachable. A chain of
│     │   custody does not prevent tampering. It makes tampering VISIBLE.
│     │
│     ├─ ──▶ INCIDENT REPORT, describing the incident and the rule that may have
│     │       been violated, filed on a clock: Colorado, not later than two
│     │       business days after discovery (Colo. Code Regs. 1505-1-20.12.2).
│     ├─ ──▶ NOTIFICATION UP: the chief elections official immediately, the
│     │       Secretary of State within 24 hours (Cal. Code Regs. tit. 2
│     │       § 20690).
│     ├─ ──▶ EQUIPMENT OUT OF SERVICE, replaced if possible, and the votes cast
│     │       on it before removal subjected to a manual tally or a risk-limiting
│     │       audit as part of the official canvass (same provision).
│     └─▶ ◇ AND IF IT REACHES A COURT, three remedies have been applied: count
│          the votes despite the break · void the election and order a new one ·
│          invalidate only the subset of ballots the breach touched. Which one
│          depends on the state and on the judge.
│
├─▶ 10. POST-ELECTION STORAGE   ⚑ the only federal rule on the whole map
│     Voted ballots stay in a sealed container under a named legal custodian.
│     ├─ 52 U.S.C. § 20701: every officer of election shall RETAIN AND PRESERVE,
│     │   for twenty-two months, all records and papers coming into his
│     │   possession relating to any "act requisite to voting" in a federal
│     │   election. Willful failure: fined not more than $1,000 or imprisoned not
│     │   more than one year, or both.
│     ├─ § 20702: willfully stealing, destroying, concealing, mutilating or
│     │   altering those records carries the same penalty, and reaches ANY
│     │   PERSON, not only an officer.
│     ├─ DOJ's reading: the duty follows the paper. If custody is handed to
│     │   another individual or organization, "the duty to retain and preserve
│     │   any record or paper so deposited shall devolve upon such custodian" —
│     │   and the office must keep ultimate management authority over retention
│     │   and security, INCLUDING THE RIGHT TO PHYSICALLY ACCESS the records.
│     │   Handing the ballots away can itself be the violation.
│     └─ ⊘ AND THE ASYMMETRY. § 20703: the demand to inspect may be made by the
│         Attorney General or his representative, and must state its basis and
│         purpose. § 20704: what is obtained may be disclosed only to Congress,
│         committees, government agencies, and in court or grand jury
│         proceedings. § 20705: a federal district court may compel production.
│         Nowhere in Chapter 207 is there a counterpart for anyone else. See
│         closing block A.
│
├─▶ 11. ✂ SEIZURE — THE LAWFUL BREAK   ⚑ and the live question of 2026
│     An outside legal authority — a sheriff, a prosecutor, a legislative
│     committee — presents a warrant or a subpoena for the ballots themselves.
│     ├─ Every control from stage 1 to stage 10 is built against an unauthorized
│     │   hand. None of them is built against an authorized one.
│     ├─ ──▶ The paper leaves the custodian's control while the retention duty
│     │       under § 20701 stays on the custodian, who no longer has the right of
│     │       physical access DOJ says the duty requires.
│     ├─ [Mar 2026: more than 650,000 voted ballots taken from a county registrar
│     │   under a judge's warrant. 24 Sep 2026: the state supreme court held the
│     │   seizure unlawful because the plain text mandates that voted ballots
│     │   remain in the custody of elections officials, and ordered them
│     │   returned. See Note 5.]
│     └─▶ ⊘ The gap the case exposes is at the front end, not the back. A judge
│          deciding the warrant application has the election calendar, the
│          custody rules and a fraud allegation in front of them and no standard
│          for weighing them. [9 Sep 2026: a judicial guide proposes six — see
│          Note 6.] None of it is law.
│
└─▶ 12. DESTRUCTION   ◈ the end of the chain, and the least documented stage
      After the federal 22 months and whatever longer period state law sets, the
      ballots are destroyed under the custodian's supervision, catalogued and
      returned in proper order as they are handled (e.g. N.M. Stat. § 1-14-9).
      └─ ◈ This is the one stage with no public-facing artifact anywhere in the
          chain. The seal log ends. Nothing replaces it.

A. THE INSPECTION ASYMMETRY — THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE MAP IN FOUR SECTIONS
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  § 20701  the paper must be kept 22 months ............ duty on the custodian
  § 20702  nobody may steal or alter it ............... prohibition on everyone
  § 20703  the demand to see it may be made by ........ THE ATTORNEY GENERAL
  § 20705  and a district court may compel it ......... for THAT demand
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A voter, candidate, journalist, county supervisor or state legislator .. ∅
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The only national rule in American ballot custody is a preservation duty with
  exactly one key, and it is not held by the public. Every route by which a
  non-federal actor actually gets to look at a ballot — a recount, a contest, an
  audit, a public-records request — comes from STATE law and exists at the
  state's pleasure. This is why the vault's custody questions keep resolving to
  "the office that kept the log."

B. WHERE THE CONTROLS ACTUALLY LIVE
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  TYPICALLY IN STATUTE                 TYPICALLY IN A PROCEDURE MANUAL  ⊞
  ───────────────────────              ──────────────────────────────────
  bipartisan oversight                 which seal to buy, and from whom
  that seals be numbered               the steps of the L&A test
  that reconciliation happen           the format of the custody log
  retention minimums                   storage environment conditions
  criminal penalties for access        the incident-report form itself
  that pre-election testing occur      who may observe, and from where
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The left column says a control must exist. The right column is the control.
  This is the same hollow-command shape the 10-01 build found in HAVA — a
  federal or state requirement that a standard EXIST, with the content left
  entirely downstream — and it now appears in five consecutive builds.
  Load-bearing caveat, in the blueprint's own words: "Legislative silence on a
  specific procedure is not a prohibition; adopting sound custody practices is
  an exercise of administrative discretion," and "variation is normal. It is not
  evidence that any jurisdiction is falling short."
