HOW A U.S. ATTORNEY IS APPOINTED AND REMOVED        (as of September 17, 2026)
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path   └─▶ branch   ◇ decision   ✕ ends/rejected   ★ takes office
│            §  = 28 U.S.C. unless another title is named
│            [] = dated 2025-26 practice note or caveat
│
■ 0. THE OFFICE — 93 OFFICERS FOR 94 JUDICIAL DISTRICTS
│     ├─ § 541(a) .. one U.S. attorney for each judicial district, appointed by the
│     │              President by and with the advice and consent of the Senate
│     │              [Guam and the N. Mariana Islands share one officer -> 93 for 94]
│     ├─ § 541(b) .. FOUR-YEAR TERM — it expires, but the officer HOLDS OVER until a
│     │              successor is appointed and qualifies. A term, not a tenure.
│     ├─ § 541(c) .. "is subject to removal by the President" — at will, no cause
│     └─ § 547 .... the duties: prosecute all offenses against the United States in
│                   the district, defend the U.S. in civil actions, collect its debts
│
│   ONE OFFICE, FOUR WAYS IN. Only Route A carries a term. The other three are
│   temporary fills, and the entire 2025-26 fight is over how long they may last.
│
├──▶ ROUTE A — PRESIDENT + SENATE (§ 541)          the only permanent route
│     │
│     ├─ 1. Home-state senators recommend · White House Counsel vets · FBI background
│     │       └─▶ [custom, not law — it collapses whenever the home-state delegation
│     │            belongs to the other party, which is when Routes B-D get used]
│     ├─ 2. PRESIDENT NOMINATES ──▶ referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee
│     ├─ 3. ◇ BLUE SLIP — both home-state senators must return one
│     │       ├─yes─▶ hearing · questionnaire · committee vote
│     │       └─no──▶ the nominee is never scheduled ✕
│     │                [a COMMITTEE CUSTOM, revocable by the chair at any time. Still
│     │                 honored for U.S. attorneys and district judges; abandoned for
│     │                 circuit judges in 2017-18]
│     ├─ 4. Committee reports ──▶ Senate floor
│     │       └─▶ confirmed EN BLOC, in packages, by voice vote or one roll call
│     │            [Senate-confirmed U.S. attorneys in the 119th Congress have
│     │             arrived in batches: 16 in Oct 2025, 14 in Dec 2025, 13 in
│     │             May 2026, 4 in Aug 2026]
│     ├─ 5. ★ APPOINTED, COMMISSIONED, SWORN — the four-year term begins
│     └─ 6. Serves at the pleasure of the President ──────────▶ see 5. REMOVAL
│
├──▶ ROUTE B — INTERIM APPOINTMENT BY THE ATTORNEY GENERAL (§ 546(a)-(c))
│     │     The fast fill. No Senate, no court, no hearing — a signature.
│     │
│     ├─ § 546(a) .. the AG "may appoint a United States attorney" for a district in
│     │              which the office is vacant
│     ├─ § 546(b) .. THE BAR: the AG shall NOT appoint a person to whose appointment
│     │              by the President the Senate REFUSED to give advice and consent
│     ├─ § 546(c) .. the appointment runs until the EARLIER of
│     │                (1) a § 541 presidential appointee qualifying, or
│     │                (2) 120 DAYS after the AG's appointment
│     │              [added by Pub. L. 110-34 in June 2007, restoring a cap Congress
│     │               had deleted in the 2006 PATRIOT Act reauthorization]
│     └─ ◇ DAY 120 ARRIVES WITH NO CONFIRMED SUCCESSOR
│           └─▶ the appointment EXPIRES BY OPERATION OF LAW ────────▶ ROUTE C
│                [courts have read § 546 to authorize ONE 120-day appointment per
│                 vacancy — the power does not reset and does not return to the AG]
│
├──▶ ROUTE C — THE DISTRICT COURT APPOINTS (§ 546(d))          THE BACKSTOP
│     │     The unusual provision, and the hinge of the whole design: the
│     │     appointment power moves to the JUDICIARY.
│     │
│     ├─ Who acts ..... the district court for that district — in practice the board
│     │                 or council of active district judges, acting by order
│     ├─ What it does . appoints a U.S. attorney "to serve until the vacancy is
│     │                 filled"; the order is filed with the clerk of the court
│     ├─ How long ..... NO 120-day cap. A court appointee can outlast an entire
│     │                 nomination fight, and serves on the court's authority
│     └─ ◇ WHAT THE COURT ACTUALLY DOES WITH THE POWER
│           ├─ appoints the sitting interim ──▶ continuity, but the officer now
│           │                                   holds by judicial order, not the AG's
│           ├─ appoints someone else ────────▶ commonly a career official or the
│           │                                   standing first assistant
│           └─ DECLINES TO ACT ─────────────▶ the office runs on an acting or
│                                              delegated official ──────▶ ROUTE D
│                [N.D.N.Y.'s board of judges expressly declined to exercise § 546(d),
│                 July 2025 — a public refusal, stated in one sentence, no reasons]
│
├──▶ ROUTE D — THE WORKAROUNDS (2025-26: defeating the § 546(d) reversion)
│     │     Three instruments, none of them § 546, all aimed at one result — keeping
│     │     an unconfirmed appointee in the chair past day 120.
│     │
│     ├─◆ D1. FIRST-ASSISTANT ELEVATION — Federal Vacancies Reform Act
│     │      ├─ 5 U.S.C. § 3345(a)(1) . the FIRST ASSISTANT to a vacant Senate-
│     │      │                          confirmed office performs its functions and
│     │      │                          duties temporarily, in an ACTING capacity
│     │      ├─ 5 U.S.C. § 3346 ....... 210 days from the date the vacancy OCCURRED,
│     │      │                          with tolling while a nomination is pending
│     │      ├─ THE MOVE ............. name the expiring interim "first assistant,"
│     │      │                          then let the FVRA elevate her to "acting"
│     │      └─ ✕ REJECTED. The FVRA elevates a first assistant who ALREADY HELD
│     │           THAT JOB WHEN THE VACANCY OPENED — not one installed months later
│     │           [3d Cir. Dec 1, 2025 · 9th Cir. Aug 18, 2026 · 2d Cir. Aug 21, 2026]
│     │
│     ├─◆ D2. "SPECIAL ATTORNEY" DESIGNATION — 28 U.S.C. § 515
│     │      ├─ lets the AG specially retain an attorney to conduct any kind of legal
│     │      │  proceeding, "including grand jury proceedings"
│     │      └─ but it confers LITIGATING AUTHORITY, NOT THE OFFICE. A person can do
│     │         every piece of the work and still not BE the U.S. attorney
│     │         [used in tandem with D1 in the 2025-26 designations]
│     │
│     └─◆ D3. WHOLESALE DELEGATION — 28 U.S.C. §§ 509, 510
│            ├─ the AG may delegate "any function" to any other DOJ officer or
│            │  employee, and may authorize successive redelegation
│            └─ ✕ REJECTED as a route to the office: the AG cannot create a DE FACTO
│               acting U.S. attorney by delegating ALL of the office's functions and
│               duties to one person [9th Cir., Aug 2026]
│
├──▶ 5. REMOVAL — WHAT ENDS EACH ROUTE, AND WHO DECIDES
│     ├─ § 541 appointee ......... the PRESIDENT, at will, no cause, no notice, no
│     │                            finding — § 541(c) is unqualified
│     │                            [in practice the demand is conveyed through the
│     │                             Deputy AG's office; refusal to resign is simply
│     │                             followed by removal]
│     ├─ § 546 interim ........... the AG, by rescinding — or the 120-day clock,
│     │                            which requires nobody's signature at all
│     ├─ § 546(d) court appointee  the President's § 541(c) removal power reaches
│     │                            this officer too. But the COURT cannot be ordered
│     │                            to appoint a replacement, and may decline
│     ├─ FVRA acting official .... the 210-day clock, or withdrawal of the
│     │                            designation that created the acting status
│     └─ EVERY ROUTE .............. resignation, or a § 541 successor qualifying
│
└──▶ 6. THE CHALLENGE TRACK — WHERE AN APPOINTMENT ACTUALLY GETS TESTED
      │     Not in the Senate. By a DEFENDANT or a SUBPOENA RECIPIENT, after the
      │     fact, in a courtroom, as a collateral attack on the officer's authority.
      │
      ├─ WHO RAISES IT ... a charged defendant, by motion to dismiss the indictment,
      │                    or a grand-jury target, by motion to quash the subpoena
      ├─ ON WHAT GROUND .. § 546 read as a one-appointment statute · the FVRA's
      │                    first-assistant limit · the Appointments Clause
      ├─ ◇ WHAT THE COURT CAN DO ABOUT IT
      │    ├─ DISQUALIFY the official from this matter ──▶ office continues, the
      │    │                                               case is reassigned
      │    ├─ QUASH the grand jury subpoenas issued on the bad authority
      │    ├─ DISMISS THE INDICTMENT WITHOUT PREJUDICE ──▶ the government may
      │    │    re-indict IF the limitations period has not run   ◀── THE HINGE.
      │    │    "Without prejudice" is only a reprieve while the clock still has
      │    │    time on it; on a stale charge it is a dismissal with prejudice
      │    │    wearing a milder name.
      │    └─ ACCEPT RATIFICATION ──▶ an officer who IS validly serving adopts the
      │         earlier acts as her own. Courts have allowed this to cure a late or
      │         defective FILING — not to retroactively validate an invalid officer.
      └─ ──▶ appeal ──▶ court of appeals ──▶ petition for certiorari ──▶ SCOTUS
              [United States v. Jackson, No. 26-304, filed Sep 8, 2026: the
               Solicitor General asks the Court to read the FVRA more broadly,
               warning of disruption across U.S. attorney's offices]
