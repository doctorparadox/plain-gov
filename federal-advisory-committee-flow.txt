THE FEDERAL ADVISORY COMMITTEE SYSTEM — ONE GATE IN, FIVE WAYS OUT (October 2026)
│   Federal Advisory Committee Act of 1972 · 5 U.S.C. ch. 10 · 41 C.F.R. part 102-3
│   ~1,000 chartered committees across ~50 agencies · 1,021 reported for FY2023
│   ☐ a published notice is required    ⊘ foreclosed by statutory text
│   ◎ the Act stops here    ✕ a requirement that does not bind    ⚑ no due date
│   ★ used or decided in 2025–26    ◈ a count or reading made on this page
│
■ TIER 0 — THE COVERAGE TEST (two conditions; fail either and none of this applies)
│
│   ┌────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┐
│   │  CONDITION 1 — MEMBERSHIP          │  CONDITION 2 — CONTROL             │
│   │  At least one member who is NOT a  │  "Established or utilized" by the  │
│   │  full-time or permanent part-time  │  President or an agency. § 1001(2) │
│   │  federal officer or employee.      │  "Utilized" = the government       │
│   │  § 1001(2)                         │  "exercises actual management or   │
│   │                                    │  control of its operation."        │
│   │  ⊘ A group of federal employees    │  § 102-3.25                        │
│   │    only is excluded outright by    │  ◎ A group genuinely run by        │
│   │    § 1001(2)(B)(i).                │    someone else is outside the Act │
│   └────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
│   ★ THE LIVE TEST: the Energy Department convened a Climate Working Group in
│     April 2025 outside the chartering process. On 30 January 2026 the District
│     of Massachusetts held it had been a federal advisory committee all along —
│     established and used, with non-federal members — and that its violations of
│     the Act were established as a matter of law. The court did not strike the
│     group's report from the public record; it made it procedurally unusable as
│     a basis for federal action. The Department continues to publish it.
│   ◈ The holding matters structurally rather than politically: the way to fail
│     the coverage test is to give up control, and an agency that wants a
│     particular answer is the least likely party to do that.
│
├─◆ TIER 1 — THE THREE WAYS A COMMITTEE COMES INTO EXISTENCE
│   │
│   │  ┌──────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
│   │  │ STATUTORY        │ PRESIDENTIAL         │ DISCRETIONARY            │
│   │  │ (non-discretion) │ (non-discretionary)  │                          │
│   │  ├──────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
│   │  │ Congress         │ The President, by    │ The agency head, on its  │
│   │  │ requires it by   │ directive, usually   │ own authority or under a │
│   │  │ statute, usually │ an executive order   │ general congressional    │
│   │  │ naming it        │                      │ authorization            │
│   │  ├──────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
│   │  │ ENDS: only by    │ ENDS: when the       │ ENDS: when the agency    │
│   │  │ legislation      │ directive is         │ head decides it has      │
│   │  │ ⊘ An executive   │ rescinded            │ served its purpose       │
│   │  │   order cannot   │                      │ ◎ No vote, no comment    │
│   │  │   terminate it   │                      │   period, no cause       │
│   │  ├──────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
│   │  │ 58% of FY2023    │ 3% of FY2023         │ ◈ the balance, ~39%      │
│   │  └──────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┘
│   │  ◈ The percentages are the published FY2023 shares; the discretionary
│   │    share is the remainder and is where almost all 2025–26 activity landed.
│   │
│   │  ◇ WHICH BOX A COMMITTEE SITS IN IS A DRAFTING QUESTION, NOT A POLICY ONE.
│   │    Authorizing language that does not name a specific committee, and
│   │    committee-report language in the legislative history, both leave the
│   │    body DISCRETIONARY — so Congress can ask for an advisory body and still
│   │    leave the agency free to end it.
│
├─◆ TIER 2 — THE CHAIN OF OFFICIALS (nobody in this chain is Senate-confirmed
│   │        for this purpose, and the top of it is a staff office)
│   │
│   │  GSA COMMITTEE MANAGEMENT SECRETARIAT — § 1006
│   │    ├─ government-wide oversight of the whole FACA program
│   │    ├─ must be CONSULTED before a discretionary committee is established,
│   │    │    re-established, renewed or merged (§ 102-3.60), and may give an
│   │    │    assessment of whether it serves the public interest (§ 102-3.65)
│   │    ├─ writes and maintains 41 C.F.R. part 102-3
│   │    ├─ runs the public FACA database and the annual comprehensive review
│   │    └─ ✕ CONSULTATION IS NOT APPROVAL. Nothing in the Act gives this office
│   │         power to refuse an establishment or stop a termination.
│   │           ◎ Functions moved here from the budget office in 1977 by
│   │             Executive Order 12024. The referee is an administrative
│   │             services agency, by executive order, not by design.
│   │    │
│   │    ▼
│   │  AGENCY HEAD — § 1007(a)
│   │    ├─ designates the Committee Management Officer and the Designated
│   │    │    Federal Officers; may delegate both
│   │    ├─ issues the agency's administrative guidelines
│   │    └─ signs the written determination to close a meeting (§ 102-3.105(e))
│   │    │
│   │    ▼
│   │  COMMITTEE MANAGEMENT OFFICER (CMO) — § 1007(b)
│   │    ├─ one per agency · supervises establishment, procedures and reporting
│   │    └─ custodian of the records: charters, membership lists, closed-meeting
│   │         determinations (§ 102-3.115)
│   │    │
│   │    ▼
│   │  DESIGNATED FEDERAL OFFICER (DFO) — § 1009(e)–(f), § 102-3.120
│   │    ├─ CALLS every meeting · APPROVES every agenda · ADJOURNS the meeting
│   │    ├─ must chair or attend · ⊘ NO MEETING MAY OCCUR WITHOUT THE DFO
│   │    └─ ◎ THIS IS THE QUIETEST EXIT IN THE WHOLE STRUCTURE. A committee
│   │         whose designated officer never calls a meeting has not been
│   │         terminated, has not lost its charter and has not lost its members.
│   │         It simply does not meet, and no notice of that is published
│   │         anywhere. ⚑ The Act sets no minimum meeting frequency; the charter
│   │         states an ESTIMATE of number and frequency (§ 1008(c)(2)(H)).
│   │    │
│   │    ▼
│   │  THE COMMITTEE — advisory only. § 1002(b)(6): its role is advisory, and
│   │    determinations of action remain with the federal officials.
│
├─◆ TIER 3 — THE CHARTERING GATE AND THE TWO-YEAR CLOCK
│   │
│   │  A committee may not meet or take any action until a charter is FILED.
│   │  Filed with, in parallel (§ 1008(c), § 102-3.70):
│   │    ├─ the agency head
│   │    ├─ the standing committees of jurisdiction in BOTH chambers
│   │    ├─ the GSA Committee Management Secretariat
│   │    ├─ the Office of Management and Budget
│   │    └─ the Library of Congress
│   │
│   │  The charter must state (§ 102-3.75): the mission or charge · the specific duties ·
│   │  the general operating characteristics · ⚑ an ESTIMATE of the number and
│   │  frequency of meetings.
│   │
│   │  ADDITIONALLY, FOR A DISCRETIONARY COMMITTEE (§ 102-3.60(a), § 102-3.65),
│   │  the agency head must give the Secretariat a written public interest
│   │  determination, copied to the budget office, which should address:
│   │    1. why the committee is essential to agency business
│   │    2. its costs, its member-selection criteria and the agency's other
│   │       committees
│   │    3. why the advice is not available from another committee, another
│   │       federal source or a cheaper, less burdensome one
│   │  plus ☐ a Federal Register notice at least 7 CALENDAR DAYS before the
│   │  charter is filed, carrying that determination.
│   │
│   │  ◇ THE TWO-YEAR CLOCK — § 1008(c)(1), § 1013(b)(2)
│   │    Every charter expires two years after filing. Renewal is affirmative:
│   │    somebody has to sign. ☐ Renewal and re-establishment require the same
│   │    Secretariat consultation as establishment.
│   │      ◎ EXIT 2. A committee can be ended by nobody signing. The charter
│   │        lapses on its own terms, the committee becomes unable to conduct
│   │        business, and no termination decision is ever made or announced.
│   │    ★ A statutory committee whose termination requirement has been waived
│   │      but which holds no approved charter is administratively inactive —
│   │      it exists in law and cannot act.
│
├─◆ TIER 4 — THE RULES THAT APPLY ONCE THE CHARTER IS FILED
│   │
│   │  FAIR BALANCE — § 1004(b)(2)
│   │    "fairly balanced in terms of the points of view represented and the
│   │    functions to be performed"
│   │    ├─ for non-discretionary committees, balance is written into the
│   │    │    statute or directive that created the body
│   │    ├─ for discretionary committees, selection criteria in the written
│   │    │    determination (§ 102-3.60(a)(3))
│   │    └─ ★ the § 102-3.60(b) considerations were converted from mandatory to
│   │         advisory phrasing by the 16 December 2025 final rule
│   │           ✕ The statutory "shall" is unchanged. The regulation that told
│   │             agencies how to satisfy it now says "should."
│   │
│   │  OPEN MEETINGS AND NOTICE — § 1009, § 102-3.150
│   │    ├─ ☐ notice in the Federal Register at least 7 CALENDAR DAYS ahead,
│   │    │    carrying the committee name, time, date, place, agenda summary,
│   │    │    open-or-closed status and the DFO's contact details
│   │    │    ★ cut from 15 days by the 16 December 2025 final rule
│   │    ├─ shorter notice only if the President finds national security
│   │    │    requires it or the agency head finds exceptional circumstances,
│   │    │    with the reasons stated in the notice
│   │    ├─ ★ the § 102-3.150(c) website and announcement practices were
│   │    │    likewise softened in December 2025
│   │    └─ ◇ CLOSING A MEETING runs on the Sunshine Act's ten exemptions
│   │         (5 U.S.C. § 552b(c)). The DFO asks the agency head, who issues a
│   │         written determination, available to the public on request
│   │         (§ 102-3.155). A closed meeting is still noticed, with the
│   │         exemption cited.
│   │           ◈ NOT ONE OF THE FIVE EXITS, BUT THE SAME EFFECT. A closed meeting
│   │             is still a meeting, and the committee is still chartered and
│   │             still staffed — but the
│   │             public channel the Act was written to create is shut. Open
│   │             meetings at the Department of Health and Human Services
│   │             averaged 255 a year in the decade before 2025 and numbered 91
│   │             in 2025 [Nature, April 2026].
│   │
│   │  RECORDS AND MINUTES — § 1009(c), § 1012, § 102-3.165
│   │    ├─ detailed minutes, certified by the chair, the DFO responsible for
│   │    │    securing certification within 90 days
│   │    ├─ committee reports deposited with the Library of Congress for public
│   │    │    inspection (§ 1012)
│   │    └─ ⚑ Agencies report committee data to the GSA FACA database. For the
│   │         public, the regulation says only that agencies SHOULD post minutes
│   │         on an agency committee website "if one exists" (§ 102-3.165(c)).
│   │
│   │  PUBLIC PARTICIPATION — § 102-3.140
│   │    ├─ any member of the public may file a written statement
│   │    └─ ✕ speaking to the committee is allowed only "if the agency's
│   │         guidelines so permit" — the right to be heard aloud is set by the
│   │         agency being advised.
│
├─◆ TIER 5 — THE EXCLUSIONS (where a group sits outside all of Tier 4)
│   │
│   │  ├─ WHOLLY FEDERAL — composed only of full-time or permanent part-time
│   │  │    federal officers or employees. § 1001(2)(B)(i), § 102-3.40(g).
│   │  │    ⊘ Excluded by the statute itself, not only by regulation.
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ NOT ACTUALLY MANAGED OR CONTROLLED by the executive branch.
│   │  │    § 102-3.40(c). ◎ The condition tested and failed in the January
│   │  │    2026 Energy Department case. ★
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ INDIVIDUAL ADVICE, NOT COLLECTIVE — a group assembled so that each
│   │  │    person gives their own view, or purely to exchange facts and
│   │  │    information. § 102-3.40(d)–(e). ◈ The most elastic exclusion on the
│   │  │    list, because it turns on how the meeting is characterised rather
│   │  │    than on who is in the room.
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ INTERGOVERNMENTAL — federal officials with elected state, local and
│   │  │    tribal officers acting in their official capacities, exclusively
│   │  │    on federal programs with shared intergovernmental responsibilities.
│   │  │    § 102-3.40(f).
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ ALSO LISTED IN § 102-3.40 — roundtables, workshops, townhalls, listening
│   │  │    sessions and small expert meetings seeking individual advice (k);
│   │  │    outside groups sharing views already formed (m); agencies briefing
│   │  │    the private sector (n); public engagement required by statute (l);
│   │  │    local civic groups (h); groups advising state or local officials (i);
│   │  │    committees with primarily operational functions (j).
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ SUBCOMMITTEES — § 102-3.35
│   │  │    ◇ The test is who receives the advice:
│   │  │      ├─ reports ONLY to its parent committee ──▶ the Act and part
│   │  │      │    102-3 in general do not apply
│   │  │      └─ advises a federal officer or the agency DIRECTLY, or its advice
│   │  │           is adopted by the parent without further deliberation ──▶ the
│   │  │           meeting rules apply (§ 102-3.145)
│   │  │    ◎ Under a discretionary committee, a subcommittee not made up entirely
│   │  │      of parent-committee members needs the same consultation and written
│   │  │      determination as a new committee (§ 102-3.60(a)). The charter states
│   │  │      whether subcommittees may be created and by whom (§ 102-3.75(l)).
│   │  │
│   │  ├─ PREPARATORY AND ADMINISTRATIVE WORK — a gathering solely to collect
│   │  │    information or handle administrative matters is not a "meeting."
│   │  │    § 102-3.160.
│   │  │
│   │  └─ STATUTORY CARVE-OUTS — Congress exempts particular committees by name
│   │       in substantive legislation (for example 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7c(a)(6)(I)
│   │       and 16 U.S.C. § 7702(d)(C)(iv)).
│
└─◆ TIER 6 — THE FIVE EXITS, ORDERED BY HOW MUCH ANYONE IS TOLD
    │
    │  EXIT 1 — TERMINATION BY THE AGENCY HEAD ★
    │    The agency head determines the purpose has been fulfilled or is no
    │    longer being carried out, and notifies the Secretariat (§ 102-3.55).
    │    Available for discretionary committees only.
    │    ◈ The only exit on this list that can generate a public document, and
    │      part 102-3 does not require one: § 102-3.65 lists Federal Register
    │      notices for establishing, renewing, reestablishing and merging.
    │    ★ WORKED EXAMPLE: one notice at 90 FR 24383 (10 June 2025) terminated
    │      fourteen Commerce committees together, among them all three of the
    │      Census Bureau's advisory committees.
    │
    │  EXIT 2 — THE CHARTER LAPSES
    │    Two years pass and nobody renews. No decision, no notice, no record of
    │    a choice having been made. The committee cannot conduct business.
    │
    │  EXIT 3 — DEPOPULATION ★
    │    Members are removed, or terms are allowed to expire unfilled. The
    │    committee continues to exist with nobody on it.
    │    ⊘ This route, like Exits 2 and 4, can reach a STATUTORY committee, which
    │      no executive action can repeal.
    │    ★ PARALLEL: all twenty-two members of the National Science Board, the
    │      governing and oversight body created by the National Science
    │      Foundation Act of 1950, were removed on 24 April 2026. The Board
    │      remains in law. ◈ Its open sessions run under the Sunshine Act, so
    │      read it as the method at work, not as a FACA committee case.
    │
    │  EXIT 4 — NO MEETING IS CALLED
    │    The Designated Federal Officer holds the exclusive power to call a
    │    meeting (§ 1009(f)) and is under no duty to exercise it. ⚑
    │    ◈ Indistinguishable from the outside from an active committee between
    │      meetings. Nothing is filed and nothing expires.
    │
    │  EXIT 5 — RECONSTITUTION OUTSIDE THE ACT ★
    │    The function is moved to a body that fails Tier 0: an all-federal
    │    working group, a contractor-convened panel, a consultation framed as
    │    individual rather than collective advice, or a subcommittee reporting
    │    inward. The advice keeps arriving; the public channel closes.
    │    ★ WORKED EXAMPLE AND ITS LIMIT: the Energy Department's 2025 Climate
    │      Working Group took this route and a court held in January 2026 that
    │      the route had not worked, because the Department had kept control.
    │

        THE READING, SET OUT PLAINLY
        ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
        Eleven requirements.  Two of them bind without an exit: the charter
        must be filed before the committee acts, and the committee's advice
        remains advice.  Both limit what the COMMITTEE can do.  Of the nine
        aimed at the AGENCY, four are satisfied by publishing or filing a
        document, three can be shortened, closed or withheld on the agency's
        own say-so, one was moved from "must" to "should" in December 2025,
        and one - the duty to meet at all - was never written.
        ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
        The Act was built to make outside advice public.  It does that well
        while a committee is meeting, and it says almost nothing about
        whether a committee meets.
