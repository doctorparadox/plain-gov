HOW A CONGRESSIONAL REDISTRICTING MAP IS DRAWN AND CHALLENGED  (doctrine as of Aug 31, 2026)
│   Federal congressional districts · state-legislative maps follow a parallel but looser track
│   ◇ = decision point   ⛔ = hard stop   ⏱ = clock that can decide the case
│
■ STAGE 0 — THE CONSTITUTIONAL FLOOR: WHO GETS TO DRAW AT ALL
│
├─◆ Art. I §2 + 14th Am. §2 ...... seats apportioned among the states by population, every 10 years
├─◆ Art. I §4 (Elections Clause) . "Times, Places and Manner" prescribed by each STATE legislature,
│                                   BUT "Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such
│                                   Regulations" — a federal power that exists and is mostly unused
├─◆ 2 U.S.C. § 2c ................ federal statute: single-member districts, one Rep per district
├─◆ 14th Am. Equal Protection .... districts must be substantially equal in population
└─◆ Voting Rights Act § 2 ........ no practice that "results in" denial or abridgement on account
                                    of race  [narrowed to near-inoperability by CALLAIS — Stage 5c]
│
│   ⭐ NOTHING HERE SAYS "ONCE PER DECADE." The Constitution requires apportionment every ten
│      years; no federal law caps how often a state may REDRAW within the decade. LULAC v. Perry
│      (2006) confirmed it. The once-a-decade pattern was a norm, and norms do not need repealing.
│
──▶ STAGE 1 — APPORTIONMENT (the input, fixed for the decade)
│
│   Census enumeration ──▶ method of equal proportions divides 435 seats ──▶ Commerce Secretary
│   reports to the President ──▶ President transmits to Congress ──▶ Clerk notifies each governor
│
│   Output: each state's SEAT COUNT. States gaining or losing seats must redraw; every state
│   redraws anyway to re-equalize population.        [the apportionment stage is mapped separately]
│
──▶ STAGE 2 — WHO HOLDS THE PEN  ◇ varies by state
│
├─◇ LEGISLATURE DRAWS (majority of states) ──▶ ordinary bill ──▶ governor's veto applies
│   │                                            └─▶ ⚠ in NC, MD, CT and a few others the
│   │                                                congressional map is NOT subject to veto
│   └─▶ the drawing body is the body with the most direct stake in the outcome
│
├─◇ INDEPENDENT COMMISSION (AZ, CA, MI, CO, WA...) ── legislators excluded; map takes effect
│   │                                                  without legislative adoption
│   └─▶ upheld against an Elections Clause challenge in Arizona State Legislature (2015)
│
├─◇ POLITICAL / BACKUP COMMISSION (NJ, HI; OH, IN as backup) ── party-appointed, or acts only
│                                                                on legislative deadlock
├─◇ ADVISORY COMMISSION ──▶ recommends only; legislature still adopts  ⛔ not a real constraint
│
└─◇ COURT-DRAWN (default on failure) ──▶ deadlock, veto, or a struck map with no timely
                                          replacement ──▶ special master ──▶ remedial map
│
│   ⭐ THE COMMISSION IS ONLY AS STRONG AS THE STATE CONSTITUTION BEHIND IT. Where a legislature
│      can amend or bypass the commission it created, the commission is a speed bump.
│
──▶ STAGE 3 — THE DRAWING CONSTRAINTS  (one is objective; the rest are where discretion lives)
│
├─◆ EQUAL POPULATION ....... congressional: near-mathematical equality (Wesberry / Karcher)
│                             state legislative: ~10% total deviation generally tolerated
│                             ⭐ the ONLY criterion with a number attached to it
├─◆ RACE ................... § 2 may REQUIRE a majority-minority district (Gingles, 1986)
│                             …while the 14th Am. FORBIDS race as the predominant motive (Shaw)
│                             ⭐ states have been caught between these two for 30 years
├─◆ CONTIGUITY ............. required nearly everywhere; the weakest real limit (point contiguity,
│                             water contiguity, and "land bridges" all commonly accepted)
├─◆ COMPACTNESS ............ required in ~40 states — but the metric is almost never specified,
│                             and the dozen standard measures disagree with each other
├─◆ POLITICAL SUBDIVISIONS . keep counties/cities whole "where practicable" — always yielding
├─◆ COMMUNITIES OF INTEREST  the least defined criterion, and the main public-testimony channel
├─◆ INCUMBENT PROTECTION ... expressly permitted in some states, expressly banned in others
└─◆ PARTISAN ADVANTAGE ..... ⭐ NOT prohibited by federal law at all after Rucho (Stage 5d)
│
──▶ STAGE 4 — ENACTMENT
│
│   Draft ──▶ committee / commission hearings ──▶ floor or commission vote ──▶ ◇ governor's
│   signature (where the veto applies) ──▶ MAP ENACTED ──▶ candidate filing opens under new lines
│                                    └─▶ veto ──▶ override, or deadlock ──▶ back to Stage 2 court
│
└─◇ CITIZEN VETO TRACK (referendum states only)
    signatures gathered ──▶ ◇ does state law allow a referendum on THIS subject?
    ├─ yes ──▶ measure certified to the ballot ──▶ map suspended or not, per state law
    └─ no  ──▶ ⛔ petition rejected — and that threshold question is itself litigated
       [MO 2026: ~300,000 signatures gathered; Sec. of State rejected the petition Aug 4; a Cole
        County judge held Aug 19 that the state constitution does not permit a referendum on
        congressional redistricting; on appeal, with a ⏱ Sept 8 deadline for ballot placement]
│
──▶ STAGE 5 — THE FOUR CHALLENGE DOORS  (a map can be attacked on any or all)
│
├─◆ (a) EQUAL POPULATION / ONE PERSON ONE VOTE .......... 14th Am. · Baker, Reynolds, Wesberry
│       Status: LIVE and rarely contested — the arithmetic is checkable, so states comply.
│
├─◆ (b) RACIAL GERRYMANDER .............................. 14th Am. · Shaw v. Reno (1993)
│       Claim: race was the PREDOMINANT factor ──▶ strict scrutiny ──▶ state must show a
│       compelling interest, narrowly tailored. Status: LIVE — and now the ascendant theory.
│
├─◆ (c) VOTE DILUTION under VRA § 2 ..................... statutory · Gingles (1986)
│   │   Old test: 3 preconditions (compact minority group · political cohesion · white bloc
│   │   voting usually defeats it) + totality of circumstances. EFFECTS, not intent.
│   └─▶ ⛔ LOUISIANA v. CALLAIS, Apr 29 2026, 6–3 (Alito): § 2 is violated "only when the
│           evidence supports a strong inference that the State INTENTIONALLY drew its districts
│           to afford minority voters less opportunity because of their race." Past discrimination
│           and the ongoing "effects of societal discrimination" get "much less weight."
│           ⭐ § 2 WAS ENACTED IN 1982 PRECISELY TO REMOVE AN INTENT REQUIREMENT. Restoring it
│              collapses the statute into the constitutional claim it was written to supplement.
│
└─◆ (d) PARTISAN GERRYMANDER ............................ ⛔ federal door CLOSED
    │   Rucho v. Common Cause (2019, 5–4): nonjusticiable political question in FEDERAL court —
    │   "beyond the reach of the federal courts," while conceding the maps were "highly partisan."
    └─▶ SURVIVING ROUTE: STATE constitutional claims in STATE court (free-elections clauses,
        equal-protection and free-speech analogues) — PA 2018, NC 2022, NY 2022, UT 2025
        └─▶ ◇ but a state constitution can be amended, a state supreme court is often ELECTED,
            and Rucho expressly blessed this route while leaving it entirely to the states
            [VA 2026: amendment passed a referendum by ~3 pts on Apr 21 — and the Supreme Court
             of Virginia struck it on May 8 for defects in HOW the legislature advanced it;
             2022/2024 lines ordered used. The counter-map died on procedure, not on merits.]
│
──▶ STAGE 6 — THE CLOCKS  ⏱  (where most challenges are actually decided)
│
├─⏱ THE PURCELL PRINCIPLE ..... courts grow reluctant to change election rules as an election
│                                nears. No fixed deadline exists — which is the point.
├─⏱ CANDIDATE FILING .......... once filing opens under a map, disruption arguments compound
├─⏱ PRIMARY DATE .............. a map used in a primary is very hard to dislodge for the general
└─⏱ APPELLATE / SHADOW DOCKET . a stay can install a map for a full cycle with no merits ruling
│
│   ⭐ TIMING IS A MERITS-INDEPENDENT DEFENSE. A plaintiff can be right and still lose, because
│      the calendar ran — and the election then proceeds under the very map being challenged.
│
──▶ STAGE 7 — REMEDY  (what a court can actually do)
│
├─▶ enjoin the map ──▶ ◇ remand to the legislature with a deadline
│                      ├─ legislature complies ──▶ new map (often materially similar)
│                      └─ misses the deadline ──▶ special master draws a remedial map
├─▶ declaratory relief only ──▶ ⛔ map stays in use
└─▶ deny relief on Purcell ──▶ ⛔ election proceeds under the challenged map
│
│   ⛔⭐ THERE IS NO RE-RUN. No court has ever ordered a congressional election re-held because
│         the districts were later held unlawful. The seats won under an unlawful map are seated,
│         vote for two years, and count toward control of the chamber. THE REMEDY IS ALWAYS
│         PROSPECTIVE, WHICH MEANS DELAY IS NOT A COST OF LITIGATION — IT IS A STRATEGY IN IT.
│

  FIVE STRUCTURAL FACTS
  1. The pen is held by the interested party in most states, and that is the default design,
     not a failure of it. Every reform on this map is an exception carved out of that default.
  2. Federal law bans map-drawing on race and permits map-drawing on party — so the safest
     defense to a racial-gerrymander claim is to say the motive was partisan.
  3. Rucho closed the federal door and pointed to the states; Callais narrowed the statute to
     an intent test. Both moves shifted redistricting from federal-judicial to state-political
     control — and state courts and state constitutions are themselves electable and amendable.
  4. Every criterion except equal population is either undefined or optional. The one number in
     the whole system is the one nobody fights about.
  5. The clock outranks the merits. Purcell, filing deadlines and the shadow docket dispose of
     more redistricting challenges than any doctrine on this map — and losing on the clock
     produces a real election under the disputed lines, with no mechanism to undo it.
