│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: IN SEVEN STATES AN AUTOMATIC RECOUNT IS THE ONLY SECOND
│  COUNT A LOSING CANDIDATE CAN GET, AND IN FOUR OF THEM THAT CANDIDATE CAN ALSO
│  CANCEL IT. The power runs one way: nowhere on this map can the loser ask for a
│  recount, and in six states the loser can stop one. Read Part D before trusting
│  any deadline here — on the one recent occasion the deadline was tested, it
│  governed the filing and not the result.
│
■ PART A — THE SIX STATES WHERE THE DEFEATED CANDIDATE CAN SWITCH IT OFF
│
├─▸ PENNSYLVANIA ................................ 25 P.S. § 3154(h)
│     ☐ "A recount and recanvass shall not be ordered ... if the defeated
│       candidate requests in writing that a recount and recanvass not be made."
│     ▸ who may file: the defeated candidate, singular
│     ⏳ "by 12 o'clock noon on the second Wednesday following the election" —
│       and the order itself is due by 5 p.m. on the second THURSDAY, so the
│       window shuts about twenty-nine hours before the duty attaches
│     ⊘ ballot questions: the waiver reaches subsection (g)(1)(i) only, which is
│       the candidate trigger. A question approved or rejected by the same
│       half-point margin recounts and nobody can stop it.
│     $ the Commonwealth. A completed statewide recount was estimated above
│       $1 million in 2024.
│     ✱ THE ONLY STATE ON THIS MAP WHOSE WAIVER CLOCK IS SET BY THE CALENDAR
│       RATHER THAN BY THE CANVASS, AND THE ONLY ONE THAT HAS BEEN TESTED.
│
├─▸ MICHIGAN .................................... Mich. Comp. Laws § 168.880a(6)
│     ☐ the recount "must not be conducted if, within 48 hours after the
│       election is certified, the losing candidate files a written statement
│       with the secretary of state requesting that the recount ... not be
│       conducted"
│     ▸ who may file: the losing candidate — ★ OR A LOSING BALLOT QUESTION
│       COMMITTEE, which has the same option in the same subsection
│     ⏳ 48 hours after certification. Keyed to the canvass, not to election
│       day, so the date moves when the canvass moves.
│     ★ THE ONLY STATE FOUND WHERE A BALLOT-QUESTION RECOUNT CAN BE SWITCHED
│       OFF AT ALL, AND THE ONLY ONE WHERE THE FILER NEED NOT BE A PERSON.
│     ▸ triggers: 0.1% statewide · 75 votes or fewer for state senate · 25 or
│       fewer for state representative
│
├─▸ FLORIDA ..................................... Fla. Stat. § 102.141(7)
│     ☐ "A recount need not be ordered with respect to the returns for any
│       office ... if the candidate or candidates defeated or eliminated from
│       contention ... request in writing that a recount not be made."
│     ▸ who may file: EVERY candidate inside the half-point margin. The plural
│       is the operative word — one trailing candidate cannot switch it off
│       over another trailing candidate's objection.
│     ⊘ deadline: none stated anywhere in the subsection
│     ⊘ ballot questions: the waiver clause names offices only. The same
│       subsection's recount duty for measures and judicial-retention
│       questions has no comparable exit.
│
├─▸ SOUTH CAROLINA .............................. S.C. Code § 7-17-280
│     ☐ no recount is required if "such other candidate or candidates shall
│       waive a recount in writing"
│     ▸ who may file: again every other candidate inside the one-point margin
│     ⊘ deadline: none. The recount is otherwise ordered "forthwith", which is
│       a speed instruction and not a date, so the waiver races an unscheduled
│       duty.
│     ⊘ ballot questions: amendments, questions and other issues are recounted
│       on order of the Board of State Canvassers, with no waiver clause.
│
├─▸ CONNECTICUT ................................. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 9-311a
│     ☐ the defeated candidate files a written statement "waiving this right to
│       such canvass"
│     ▸ where it is filed: ★ TWO DIFFERENT OFFICES BY RACE — the municipal
│       clerk for a municipal office, the Secretary of the State for a state or
│       district office
│     ⊘ deadline: none stated in the section
│     ✱ Connecticut calls the recanvass the candidate's RIGHT. See Part C.
│
└─▸ NEBRASKA .................................... Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-1119(2)
      ☐ "Any losing candidate may waive his or her right to a recount by filing
        a written statement with the Secretary of State, election commissioner,
        or county clerk with whom he or she made his or her filing."
      ▸ where it is filed: whichever office the candidate filed to run with —
        the statute routes the waiver by the candidacy rather than by the race
      ⊘ deadline: none stated
      $ "all expenses of a recount under this section shall be paid by those
        political subdivisions involved" — so the thing being waived is a
        publicly funded count, and the statute still calls it the loser's right
│
■ PART B — THE FIVE WHERE NOBODY CAN
│
├─▸ ARIZONA ..................................... A.R.S. § 16-661
│     a recount "is required" at a margin within one-half of one per cent of
│     the votes cast for both candidates. ⊘ NO WAIVER APPEARS ANYWHERE IN THE
│     SECTION. What the statute does instead is remove whole offices from the
│     duty: precinct committeemen, school and community-college boards, fire
│     districts and other special districts are exempt by subsection (B).
│     ✱ Arizona switches recounts off by OFFICE, in advance, by statute —
│       never by a candidate, and never after the votes are in.
│
├─▸ NEW YORK .................................... N.Y. Elec. Law § 9-208(4)
│     the board "shall conduct a full manual recount" on any one of three
│     triggers: a margin of twenty votes or fewer · a margin of 0.5% or less ·
│     or a contest of a million ballots or more decided by under 5,000 votes.
│     ⊘ no waiver, and ⊘ no concession exception. A conceded race inside any of
│     the three triggers is recounted by hand regardless.
│
├─▸ HAWAII ...................................... HRS § 11-158
│     the chief election officer "shall conduct a recount", completed and
│     announced by the fifth business day after the election, and "no candidate
│     shall be charged for the cost of a mandatory recount". ⊘ no waiver.
│     ✱ The one state that both pays for the count and refuses to let the
│       beneficiary decline it, which is the clearest statement on the map that
│       the recount is owed to the public rather than to the loser.
│
├─▸ WASHINGTON .................................. RCW 29A.64.021
│     mandatory below 2,000 votes AND below 0.5%. ⊘ no waiver — but ★ THE ONLY
│     STATE HERE WHERE THE WINNER HAS ANY SAY AT ALL: under subsection (3) "the
│     apparent winning candidate and the next leading candidate may jointly
│     choose to request an alternative method of conducting the recount."
│     ✱ The candidates together may change HOW the ballots are recounted and
│       neither may stop the recount. Method is negotiable; existence is not.
│
└─▸ COLORADO .................................... C.R.S. § 1-10.5-101(1)(b)
      a recount "shall be held" where the gap is at or under one-half of one
      percent of the HIGHEST vote cast in the contest. ⊘ nothing in subsection
      (1) or (2) lets anyone decline it. The interested-party route at
      § 1-10.5-106 runs the other way: it is how someone GETS a recount, at
      their own expense. [§ 1-10.5-106 was not read in full for this map.]
│
■ PART C — ONE NAME, THREE DIFFERENT LEGAL ACTS
│
├─▸ (a) A CONDITION ON THE STATE'S DUTY ......... Pa. · Fla. · Mich.
│     The statute speaks to the officer: the recount "shall not be ordered",
│     "need not be ordered", "must not be conducted". The candidate's filing is
│     a fact that switches off somebody else's obligation.
│
├─▸ (b) A WAIVER OF THE CANDIDATE'S OWN RIGHT ... Conn. · Neb. · S.C.
│     The statute speaks to the candidate: the losing candidate "may waive his
│     or her right". This presupposes the recount exists for the candidate's
│     benefit, and a right is the holder's to give away.
│
└─▸ (c) ✱ THE DIFFERENCE IS NOT COSMETIC, AND IT IS THE FINDING OF THIS PART.
      A duty owed to the public is not a losing candidate's to release; a
      personal right is. Two of these states write a recount PAID FOR BY THE
      PUBLIC as the loser's own property — Nebraska says so in the same section
      that charges the political subdivisions for it. Nothing in either design
      asks what the voters in the contest are owed, and no statute on this map
      gives them anyone to ask.
│
■ PART D — THE DEADLINE, AND THE ONE TIME IT WAS TESTED
│
├─  ONLY TWO OF THE SIX STATE A DEADLINE AT ALL. Pennsylvania sets a calendar
│   date and hour; Michigan sets a 48-hour clock off certification. Florida,
│   South Carolina, Connecticut and Nebraska ⊘ state none, which means the
│   practical limit in those four is whenever the count is finished.
│
├─  THE 2024 PENNSYLVANIA SENATE SEQUENCE, which is the whole argument for why
│   this column is the one that matters:
│
│      5 Nov   election. Margin under 0.25%, about 16,500 votes, inside the
│              half-point trigger
│     13 Nov   ⏳ NOON — the § 3154(h) window closes. No waiver is filed.
│     13 Nov   evening — the recount is ordered statewide. Estimated cost above
│              $1 million.
│     20 Nov   the recount begins in the counties
│     21 Nov   evening — the trailing candidate concedes and asks the state to
│              end the recount. ◎ THIS IS EIGHT DAYS AFTER THE STATUTORY WINDOW
│              CLOSED, SO THE INSTRUMENT IN PART A WAS NO LONGER AVAILABLE.
│     21 Nov   counties are told that night that they need not finish. About
│              ten had already completed their recounts.
│     22 Nov   the state announces the recount is over.
│
├─  ◎ THE HINGE. The statute's deadline governed the FILING and did not govern
│   the OUTCOME. What stopped the recount was not the waiver instrument but an
│   administrative decision to honour a request the statute no longer
│   authorised — announced in the language of saving taxpayers money, which is
│   the reasoning the deadline exists to take out of anyone's hands.
│
└─  ✱ SO THE REAL COLUMN IS NOT "WHAT IS THE DEADLINE" BUT "IS ANYONE BOUND BY
    IT." On the only recent occasion when the two answers differed, the second
    one decided. A deadline no court was asked to enforce is a deadline that
    held for eight days and then did not.
│

■ PART F — WHAT NO WAIVER ON THIS MAP REACHES
│
├─  A BALLOT QUESTION, IN EVERY STATE BUT ONE. Pennsylvania, Florida and South
│   Carolina each write the waiver for offices and leave the question recount
│   running; Michigan alone hands the same switch to a ballot question
│   committee. ✱ The reason is structural rather than principled: a question
│   has no defeated candidate to do the asking, and only Michigan went looking
│   for a substitute.
│
├─  ⊘ THE RECOUNT THE LOSER WANTS. The power on this map runs one direction
│   only. In Arizona, Connecticut, Florida, Hawaii, New York, Pennsylvania and
│   South Carolina there is no candidate-initiated recount at all, so a losing
│   candidate's entire statutory authority over the second count is the
│   authority to cancel one. ✱ Four of those seven states give the loser a
│   switch that only turns off.
│
├─  A RECOUNT ALREADY UNDER WAY. ⊘ No statute read here says what becomes of
│   one. Pennsylvania's window shuts before the order issues, so on the
│   statute's own timetable the question cannot arise; it arose anyway in 2024
│   and was answered by an instruction to the counties.
│
├─  THE ELECTION CONTEST. Waiving a recount is not conceding and does not
│   forfeit a contest in court, which is a separate instrument with its own
│   standing rules and its own clock.
│
├─  ⊘ THE PUBLIC. No statute on this map requires the waiver to be published,
│   docketed or served on anyone, and none requires that the voters in the
│   contest be told that a recount their state had ordered was called off at
│   the request of the person who lost it.
│
└─  ◈ THE OTHER THIRTY-NINE STATES. Eleven statutes are read on this page and
    the column is unmapped in the rest. That is a gap in the published record
    rather than in this page: the fifty-state recount tables in circulation
    carry triggers, thresholds, costs and completion deadlines, and the
    standard reference brief on recounts does not mention waiver at all.
