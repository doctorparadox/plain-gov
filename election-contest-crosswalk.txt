│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: THERE IS NO SUCH THING AS "A STATE'S CONTEST FORUM."
│  Virginia has four tribunals, Texas five, Pennsylvania five statutory classes —
│  and Texas and Pennsylvania sort them in OPPOSITE directions. Read Part A before
│  trusting any fifty-state contest table that prints one forum per state.
│
■ PART A — THE FORUM SPLITS BY OFFICE  (three states, worked all the way down)
│
├─▸ TEXAS † Tex. Elec. Code § 221.002 — five tribunals, one statute
│     ⚖ district court .................. EXCLUSIVE ORIGINAL JURISDICTION, default
│     ⚖ Senate + House in JOINT SESSION .. governor · lieutenant governor ·
│                                          comptroller · land commissioner · AG
│     ⚖ the SENATE alone ................. state senator  (general or special)
│     ⚖ the HOUSE alone .................. state representative
│     ⚖ the GOVERNOR ..................... PRESIDENTIAL ELECTORS
│     ✱ READ THAT LAST LINE AGAIN. The exclusive tribunal for a contest over the
│       election of presidential electors is ONE EXECUTIVE OFFICER, elected on the
│       same ballot being contested. No court has original jurisdiction over it.
│     ⚖ court of appeals ................. appellate only, over district-court cases
│
├─▸ PENNSYLVANIA † 25 P.S. § 3291 — every office pre-sorted into five classes
│     ▸ CLASS I ... governor · lieutenant governor
│         ⚖ "a committee to be selected from both houses of the General Assembly"
│           (25 P.S. § 3312) — a LEGISLATIVE tribunal
│     ▸ CLASS II .. presidential electors · all statewide Commonwealth officers ·
│                   nominations for U.S. Senator
│     ▸ CLASS III . judges of the several courts
│     ▸ CLASS IV .. state senators · state representatives ·
│                   nominations for U.S. Representative
│         ⚖ "shall be by the courts of law" (Pa. Const. art. VII § 13) — JUDICIAL
│     ▸ CLASS V ... every county, city, borough, township, ward, school district
│                   and other local office
│     ✱ THE INVERSION: Pennsylvania sends its own legislators' seats to JUDGES and
│       the governorship to LEGISLATORS. Texas does exactly the reverse. Both are
│       current law. Neither is the "normal" design, because there isn't one.
│
└─▸ VIRGINIA † Va. Code § 24.2-803 to -806 — four tribunals, four deadlines
      ⚖ House Committee on Privileges and Elections ...... General Assembly seats
                                                           (§ 24.2-803)
      ⚖ General Assembly, BOTH HOUSES in joint session ... governor · lieutenant
                                                           governor · AG (§ -804)
      ⚖ special 3-judge court, Circuit Court of the City of Richmond — the chief
        judge plus two circuit judges from NON-CONTIGUOUS circuits appointed by
        the Chief Justice ................................. presidential electors ·
                                                           statewide primaries
                                                           (§ 24.2-805)
      ⚖ special 3-judge circuit court where the candidate listed residency
        ................................................... U.S. House · state
                                                           legislative · local
                                                           (§ 24.2-806)
      ✱ The non-contiguous-circuit rule is the only venue provision found on this
        map that is explicitly designed to seat judges with no local stake.
│
■ PART B — WHO MAY FILE  (standing, and it is wider than "the loser")
│
├─▸ THE LOSING CANDIDATE ONLY ......... Virginia, every class † § 24.2-803 to -806
│     ✱ The narrowest standing on the map. A Virginia contest dies with the
│       candidate's decision not to bring it; no elector can revive it.
├─▸ CANDIDATE OR A VOTER .............. Nevada (candidate or registered voter) ·
│                                       Colorado ("qualified elector")
├─▸ CANDIDATE, VOTER, OR TAXPAYER ..... FLORIDA † Fla. Stat. § 102.168(1)
│     ✱ The widest standing found. A taxpayer who did not vote in the race, and
│       could not have, may contest its result.
├─▸ A GROUP OF VOTERS, BY COUNT ....... Pennsylvania — at least FIVE registered
│                                       electors who voted in that election
└─▸ VOTERS OR CANDIDATES, TO A BOARD .. North Carolina — the protest goes to the
                                        county or State Board of Elections FIRST,
                                        not to a court
      ✱ North Carolina is the one state here where the first tribunal is the
        election administrator itself, with Superior Court (Wake County) on appeal
        within 10 days. Everywhere else the contest starts outside the agency.
│
■ PART C — THE CLOCK  (and the Virginia problem)
│
├─▸ MEASURED FROM CERTIFICATION
│     ⏱ Virginia, electors / U.S. Senate .. 5:00 P.M. ON THE SECOND CALENDAR DAY
│                                           † § 24.2-805 — the tightest on the map
│     ⏱ Florida ........................... 10 days after midnight of the date of
│                                           certification † § 102.168(2)
│     ⏱ North Carolina .................... 5:00 p.m., 2nd business day after
│                                           canvass (other irregularities)
│     ⏱ Colorado .......................... 35 days for notice, then 35 more to
│                                           file the complaint
│     ⏱ Federal (U.S. House) .............. 30 days after the state authority has
│                                           DECLARED the result  → see Part F
├─▸ MEASURED FROM THE ELECTION (certification is irrelevant)
│     ⏱ Pennsylvania ...................... 20 days after the election, whether or
│                                           not a recount is running
│     ⏱ Nevada ............................ 14 days after the election, or 5 days
│                                           after a recount
│     ⏱ Virginia, General Assembly seats .. 30 days after the election, or 3 days
│                                           after a recount concludes, whichever
│                                           is later † § 24.2-803
│
└─▸ ✱ THE STRUCTURAL FAULT: THE CONTEST CLOCK AND THE RECOUNT CLOCK ARE NOT THE
    SAME CLOCK, AND IN VIRGINIA THEY RUN IN THE WRONG ORDER.
      · recount petition .... within 10 days from certification † § 24.2-801
      · contest notice ...... 5:00 p.m. on day 2 from certification † § 24.2-805
      · and § 24.2-805 provides that the contest "SHALL NOT WAIT UPON THE RESULTS
        OF ANY RECOUNT."
      → The right to contest expires EIGHT DAYS BEFORE the right to petition for a
        recount does. A Virginia candidate must decide whether to challenge the
        count without the one procedure that would tell them whether to.
      → Nevada and Virginia's § 24.2-803 are the counter-design: both run the
        contest clock from the RECOUNT's conclusion, so the instruments nest.
│
│  ⚑ AND THE CLOCK MOVED FOR THIS ELECTION WITHOUT ANY CONTEST LAW CHANGING.
│    Where the contest deadline runs from certification, moving certification moves
│    the contest window. Two states moved certification for 3 November 2026:
│      · IDAHO — H 873 moves the county canvass deadline from 10 days to NINE
│      · NEBRASKA — LB 1075 requires county results at the state by the third
│        Monday after Election Day, where PREVIOUSLY THERE WAS NO DEADLINE
│    [Wisconsin also changed its canvass law, but effective for 2028, to conform to
│     the Electoral Count Reform Act — not in force this November.]
│
■ PART D — THE STANDARD OF PROOF  (where the queue item said the variance would be,
│                                   and it was right)
│
├─▸ STRICTEST — the result must necessarily flip
│     ⊕ PENNSYLVANIA .... allegations that, if proven, would "DEFINITELY CHANGE
│                         THE RESULTS"
├─▸ MIDDLE — the result would have been different
│     ⊕ NEVADA ......... illegalities "sufficient to change the result"
│     ⊕ GENERAL RULE ... specific, credible evidence of fraud or irregularity "of
│                        such extent that it would actually have changed" the
│                        result; many jurisdictions require DIRECT EVIDENCE that
│                        the illegal votes went to the winner
├─▸ LOOSER — the result need only become unsafe
│     ⊕ FLORIDA ........ misconduct, illegal votes counted, or legal votes rejected
│                        "sufficient to change OR PLACE IN DOUBT the result"
│                        † § 102.168(3)(a),(c)
│     ✱ Florida is the only standard on this map a contestant can satisfy WITHOUT
│       showing who would have won. Doubt is enough to state the claim.
├─▸ LOOSEST ON ITS FACE — a threshold for pleading, not for winning
│     ⊕ VIRGINIA ....... "specific allegations which, if proven true, would have a
│                        PROBABLE IMPACT on the outcome" † § 24.2-807
└─▸ DIFFERENT QUESTION ENTIRELY
      ⊕ TEXAS .......... whether "the outcome ... is not the true outcome" because
                         illegal votes were counted, or an election officer
                         prevented voting, failed to count legal votes, or
                         "engaged in other fraud or illegal conduct or made a
                         mistake" † § 221.003
      ✱ Texas does not ask the contestant to prove they won. It asks the tribunal
        to find the true outcome — and § 221.011 lets it void the election
        "WITHOUT ATTEMPTING TO DETERMINE HOW INDIVIDUAL VOTERS VOTED" once the
        illegal votes equal or exceed the margin. That is the only standard here
        that does not require tracing a single ballot to a single candidate.
│
■ PART E — THE REMEDY  (four of them, and they are not interchangeable)
│
├─▸ ↪ DECLARE THE TRUE OUTCOME, OR VOID THE ELECTION
│     TEXAS † § 221.012 — "If the tribunal ... can ascertain the true outcome of
│     the election, the tribunal shall declare the outcome. The tribunal SHALL
│     DECLARE THE ELECTION VOID IF IT CANNOT ASCERTAIN THE TRUE OUTCOME."
│     ✱ The cleanest remedy on the map: a binary with no third option and no
│       discretion. Costs are then split equally between the parties (§ 221.013).
├─▸ ↪ OUSTER, EXECUTED BY THE EXECUTIVE
│     FLORIDA † § 102.1682 — "a judgment of ouster shall be entered"; on receiving
│     a certified copy, "the Governor shall revoke such commission and commission
│     the person found in the judgment to be entitled to the office."
│     ✱ The court decides; the GOVERNOR performs the remedy. The judgment is not
│       self-executing against the office.
├─▸ ↪ A MENU, CHOSEN BY A LEGISLATIVE BODY
│     VIRGINIA † § 24.2-803 — the House may declare the CONTESTANT elected, confirm
│     the CONTESTEE, void the election and order a new writ, or, on a tie,
│     DETERMINE THE WINNER BY LOT.
├─▸ ↪ A NEW ELECTION
│     NORTH CAROLINA — the board may authorize voters to re-cast, or order a NEW
│     ELECTION, where ineligible or prevented voters exceed the margin or
│     "irregularities taint the results."
└─▸ ⊘ NOTHING
      Where the term has substantially run before the proceeding ends, and where no
      statute provides a remedy for the office in question, a proven contest
      produces a declaration and no seat. [The map does not quantify this; see
      Note 9 — it is the largest unmeasured cell on the page.]
│
■ PART F — THE HOLE AT THE TOP  (the two federal offices on every November ballot)
│
│  ✱ A STATE CONTEST CANNOT FINALLY RESOLVE A SEAT IN CONGRESS. U.S. Const. art. I
│    § 5 cl. 1 makes each chamber "the Judge of the Elections, Returns and
│    Qualifications of its own Members." The state proceeding produces evidence;
│    the chamber produces the member.
│
├─▸ U.S. HOUSE — the Federal Contested Elections Act
│     ◇ a candidate who lost by the state-certified result
│     ⏱ notice of contest within 30 DAYS after the state election authority "has
│       declared the results"  [note: DECLARED, not certified — see Note 7]
│     ⏱ contestee's answer within 30 days; non-response "shall not be deemed an
│       admission of the truth"
│     ⚖ the Committee on House Administration takes jurisdiction and may create a
│       task force, direct an audit, or direct a RECOUNT — through the GAO
│     ⚖ the HOUSE ITSELF decides, by vote
│     ⊕ "The burden is upon contestant to prove that the election results entitle
│       him to contestee's seat"; the state certification is PRESUMPTIVE evidence
│       of validity that the contestant must overcome
│     ✱ FCEA contests rarely change results. The structural reason is in the
│       standard: the contestant must beat a presumption created by the very
│       count they say is wrong.
│
└─▸ U.S. SENATE — no FCEA analogue
      ⚖ the Senate, under the same art. I § 5 power, by its own procedure
      ? the Senate's modern practice is NOT mapped here — left [unconfirmed]
        rather than asserted. See Note 11.
│
│  ✱ SO THE CROSSWALK HAS A HOLE EXACTLY WHERE THE STAKES ARE HIGHEST. Of the
│    offices on a 3 November 2026 ballot, the ones a state contest cannot finish
│    are the federal ones — and for presidential electors, Part A shows that two
│    states hand even the state-side proceeding to a political officer.
