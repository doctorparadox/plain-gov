HOW A TIE VOTE IS BROKEN — three families, not two, and the method is a
function of the office                                        (as of 8 October 2026)
│  Legend:  ▸ family / lane   ⚡ what happens first   ◇ who decides
│           🎲 chance   🗳 another election   👤 a named officeholder chooses
│           ⊘ does not exist   ⚑ live in 2026   ✱ the structural point

│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: EVERY FIFTY-STATE TABLE OF THIS SUBJECT PRINTS TWO
│  COLUMNS — CHANCE AND A NEW ELECTION. THERE IS A THIRD, AND IN IT A SINGLE
│  PARTISAN OFFICEHOLDER PICKS THE WINNER OF A TIED ELECTION. See Part A ▸ 3,
│  then Part B before trusting ANY one-method-per-state table including this one.

■ PART A — THE THREE FAMILIES  (and the fourth state of having no rule at all)
│
├─▸ FAMILY 1. 🎲 CHANCE — "BY LOT" ................ ~28 states   ◈ see Note 1
│     ⚡ the canvass shows equal totals; in 7 states a recount runs first
│     ◇ an election officer or canvassing board, with NO discretion as to outcome
│     → the worked statutes:
│       VIRGINIA ....... electoral board for local office; STATE BOARD for General
│                        Assembly, U.S. CONGRESS and PRESIDENTIAL ELECTORS
│                        [Va. Code § 24.2-674]
│       PENNSYLVANIA ... by lot at NOON ON THE THIRD FRIDAY after the election —
│                        the only statutory clock-time found  [25 P.S. § 3168]
│       IDAHO .......... "a toss of a coin" before the Secretary of State within
│                        TWO DAYS of the canvass  [Idaho Code § 34-1216]
│       IOWA ........... names DRAWN FROM PAPER before the canvassers
│                        [Iowa Code § 50.44]
│       HAWAII ......... by lot, chief election officer (state) or county clerk;
│                        the 2019 Act 136 rewrite replaced an older formula
│                        [Haw. Rev. Stat. § 11-157]  ⚑ USED TWICE IN 2026 — Part E
│       WASHINGTON ..... "by public lot" by the official issuing the certificate
│                        [Wash. Rev. Code § 29A.60.221]
│       ALABAMA ........ Secretary of State draws lots PUBLICLY, IN FRONT OF THE
│                        GOVERNOR  [Ala. Code § 17-12-23]
│       also AZ · CA · CO · FL · IL · KS · KY · MI · MN · MS · NM · OH · OK · OR
│            · UT · WI · WY, and AK · NE · ND · SD after a recount
│     ✱ ONLY TWO OF THESE STATUTES NAME THE INSTRUMENT. Idaho says coin, Iowa
│       says paper; the rest say "lot" and stop. The randomizer is therefore an
│       administrative invention made on the day — see Part C, where the list of
│       what has actually been used includes dice that failed twice.
│     ✱ THIS IS THE ONLY FAMILY THAT CANNOT BE WON BY THE BETTER-RESOURCED
│       CAMPAIGN. It is also the only one that produces a winner on a known date
│       with no further voting, which is the entire argument for it.
│
├─▸ FAMILY 2. 🗳 ANOTHER ELECTION ................. ~12 states   ◈ see Note 1
│     ⚡ the tie voids the result, or is treated as a vacancy
│     ◇ THE VOTERS, a second time — the only family that returns the question
│       to the electorate rather than taking it away
│     → ARKANSAS ....... special runoff FOUR WEEKS after the election
│                        [Ark. Code Ann. § 7-5-704]
│     → GEORGIA ........ runoff 28 days later  [Ga. Code Ann. § 21-2-501]
│     → LOUISIANA ...... runoff the FOURTH SATURDAY after the tie
│                        [La. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 18:512]
│     → MASSACHUSETTS .. runoff the FOURTH MONDAY OF NOVEMBER
│                        [Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 50, § 2; Mass. Const. amend. art. XV]
│     → RHODE ISLAND ... runoff the TENTH DAY after the tie is declared
│                        [R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-3-3]
│     → SOUTH CAROLINA . runoff TWO WEEKS after the election
│                        [S.C. Code Ann. § 7-13-2210]
│     → CONNECTICUT .... recount first; special election THREE WEEKS later
│                        [Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 9-311b, 9-332]
│     → DELAWARE ....... the tie is TREATED AS A VACANCY; special election
│                        [Del. Code tit. 15, §§ 5708, 7101]
│     → VERMONT ........ recount; then a COURT-ORDERED runoff within three weeks
│                        [Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 17, § 2602k]
│     → also IN · ME · MD
│     ✱ THE COST OF THIS FAMILY IS A WHOLE ELECTION, AND THE BENEFIT IS THE ONLY
│       DEMOCRATIC ANSWER AVAILABLE. Both are why the family is small: a state
│       weighing finality against legitimacy has usually chosen finality.
│
├─▸ FAMILY 3. 👤 A NAMED OFFICEHOLDER CHOOSES ..... THE MISSING CATEGORY
│     ⚡ nothing random, nothing re-voted
│     ◇ A PERSON OR BODY WITH A PARTY, EXERCISING A CHOICE
│     → MONTANA ........ "the governor shall appoint one of those candidates to
│                        the office" — for a MEMBER OF THE LEGISLATURE, a DISTRICT
│                        JUDGE, or a JUSTICE OF THE SUPREME COURT. The Secretary
│                        of State merely certifies the totals to the Governor.
│                        [Mont. Code Ann. § 13-16-503]
│                        ✱ A TIED MONTANA SUPREME COURT ELECTION IS FILLED BY THE
│                          GOVERNOR'S PICK. The judiciary's one check against
│                          executive appointment — standing for election — fails
│                          closed, into appointment, at an exact tie.
│     → TENNESSEE ...... the STATE ELECTION COMMISSION "shall cast the deciding
│                        vote," with four exceptions by office — see Part B
│                        [Tenn. Code Ann. § 2-8-111]
│     → NEVADA ......... general election, federal/statewide/district offices and
│                        district judges: "the Legislature shall, by joint vote of
│                        both houses, elect one of those persons to fill the
│                        office"  [Nev. Rev. Stat. § 293.400(1)(a)]
│     → NEW HAMPSHIRE .. state senate ties: joint vote of both houses
│                        [N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 660:24; N.H. Const. pt. 2, art. 34]
│     → WEST VIRGINIA .. the county canvassers choose a winner; IF THEY FAIL
│                        WITHIN 30 DAYS THE GOVERNOR DECIDES  [W. Va. Code § 3-6-12]
│     → MISSOURI ....... hybrid: the GOVERNOR orders a special election, but the
│                        candidates MAY AGREE to settle it by lot instead
│                        [Mo. Rev. Stat. § 115.517]
│     → TEXAS .......... hybrid: recount, then special election, OR lot by
│                        agreement, OR one candidate simply withdraws
│                        [Tex. Elec. Code Ann. § 2.002]
│     ✱ THIS FAMILY IS WHY A ONE-METHOD-PER-STATE TABLE MISLEADS. A tracker
│       sorting states into "chance" and "new election" has nowhere to put
│       Montana, and the usual resolution is to file it under "other" or to
│       print the method for whichever office the tracker happened to check.
│     ✱ AND IT INVERTS THE QUEUE ITEM'S PREMISE FOR THESE STATES. The premise
│       was that a tie is where the law concedes that process cannot produce an
│       answer. Montana, Tennessee and Nevada concede nothing: they treat the
│       tie as having TRANSFERRED the appointment power, from the electorate to
│       a governor, a commission, or a legislature. The electorate did not fail
│       to decide — it decided, exactly evenly, and the statute reads that as a
│       vacancy to be filled.
│
└─▸ FAMILY 0. ⊘ NO TIE PROVISION AT ALL ........... NEW JERSEY · NEW YORK
      ⊘ no statute names a method for a tied election
      → NEW JERSEY ..... the remedies are the general recount and contest
                         provisions  [N.J. Rev. Stat. §§ 19:28-1 et seq., 19:29-1
                         et seq.]
      → NEW YORK ....... no tie statute; what exists is a MANDATORY RECOUNT at a
                         margin of 20 votes or fewer, or 0.5% or less
                         [N.Y. Elec. Law § 9-208]
      ✱ A MANDATORY RECOUNT IS NOT A TIE-BREAKER. New York's provision guarantees
        that a tie will be re-counted and confirmed, and then says nothing. The
        route out is a court — which is to say the one state with the largest
        legislature in this family resolves a tied legislative election by
        litigation on a judge's calendar — see the state election contest
        crosswalk, linked below.

■ PART B — THE FINDING: THE METHOD IS A FUNCTION OF THE OFFICE
│
│  Two statutes do the whole taxonomy in one section. Read either one and the
│  idea of "a state's tie-break method" stops being a coherent unit.
│
├─▸ TENNESSEE, Tenn. Code Ann. § 2-8-111 — FIVE OFFICES, FIVE DECIDERS
│     default ................ 👤 the STATE ELECTION COMMISSION casts the
│                                 deciding vote
│     county office .......... 👤 the county legislative body casts it —
│                              🗳 OR calls a runoff BY RESOLUTION
│     municipal office ....... 👤 the municipal legislative body casts it —
│                              🗳 OR calls a runoff BY RESOLUTION
│     U.S. HOUSE ............. 👤 THE GOVERNOR casts the deciding vote
│     U.S. SENATE ............ 🗳 the election is VOID; the Governor orders a
│                                 special election
│     GOVERNOR ............... 👤 the GENERAL ASSEMBLY in joint convention
│     ✱ TWO FEDERAL SEATS ON THE SAME BALLOT, TIED THE SAME NIGHT, GET OPPOSITE
│       TREATMENT: the House seat is filled by one person's choice and the Senate
│       seat is voided and re-run. Nothing in the statute explains the asymmetry,
│       and the Senate rule is the more cautious of the two by a wide margin.
│     ✱ NOTE ALSO THAT TWO OF THE FIVE ROUTES ARE ELECTIVE: a Tennessee county or
│       municipal body may cast the deciding vote OR order a runoff, and the
│       choice between a self-interested vote and an election is made by
│       RESOLUTION, after the tie, by the body that stands to benefit.
│
├─▸ NEVADA, Nev. Rev. Stat. § 293.400 — BY OFFICE, AND ALSO BY STAGE
│     GENERAL, U.S. Senator · U.S. Representative · statewide and district state
│       officers · district judges · multi-county district officers
│       ......................👤 THE LEGISLATURE, by joint vote of both houses
│     PRIMARY, the same offices
│       ......................🎲 the SECRETARY OF STATE determines it BY LOT
│                                (the GOVERNOR, if the tie is for Secretary of
│                                State — the one anti-self-dealing clause found
│                                anywhere on this map, mirrored for county clerk)
│     PRIMARY/GENERAL, partisan county · township · city · single-county district
│       ......................🎲 the COUNTY CLERK determines it by lot
│     NONPARTISAN PRIMARY, tie at the TOP
│       ......................⊘ NO TIE-BREAK. ALL TIED CANDIDATES ARE DECLARED
│                                NOMINEES and go on the general-election ballot.
│     ✱ THE SAME TIE, THE SAME SEAT, THE SAME TWO CANDIDATES: CHANCE IN THE
│       PRIMARY, A PARTISAN LEGISLATIVE VOTE IN THE GENERAL. The method is not a
│       property of the state or even of the office — it is a property of the
│       date.
│     ✱ AND (d)(1) IS THE ONLY METHOD IN AMERICAN ELECTION LAW THAT RESOLVES A
│       TIE BY NOT RESOLVING IT. The ballot grows instead. This is available only
│       because a nonpartisan primary is a winnowing rather than a nomination —
│       which is exactly why no general-election rule can copy it.
│
└─▸ VIRGINIA, Va. Code § 24.2-674 — BY LEVEL, AND THE CONGRESSIONAL REACH
      county · city · town · district office ... 🎲 the ELECTORAL BOARD, by lot
      GENERAL ASSEMBLY · U.S. CONGRESS ·
        PRESIDENTIAL ELECTORS .................. 🎲 the STATE BOARD, by lot
      ✱ A TIED SLATE OF VIRGINIA PRESIDENTIAL ELECTORS IS RESOLVED BY DRAWING
        LOTS AT THE STATE BOARD OF ELECTIONS. See the Electoral College process
        flow map, linked below, for what that collides with downstream.

■ PART C — WHAT "BY LOT" ACTUALLY WAS  (the delegation, worked)
│
│  The statutes in Family 1 command randomness. Twenty-six of twenty-eight do not
│  say how. What the canvassing authorities chose:
│
├─ film canisters in a bowl ....... VIRGINIA, House District 94, 4 Jan 2018
├─ playing cards, ACE HIGH, suits
│  ranked in advance ............. BRADENTON BEACH, FLA., mayor, Oct 2016
├─ a "Vegas-style" card draw ..... CRIPPLE CREEK, COLO., council
├─ coffee straws ................. MISSISSIPPI, state legislative contest, 2015
├─ a coin toss ................... NEW MEXICO, state legislative race, 2014
├─ a commemorative walrus-and-
│  seal coin ..................... ALASKA, state House primary, 2006
├─ a board-game piece from a bag . COOK COUNTY, MINN., commissioner
└─ DICE, RE-ROLLED TWICE after
   two failed attempts ........... WEBSTER, TEX., city council, 2012
   ✱ THE DICE ARE THE IMPORTANT ENTRY. The randomizer failed, twice, and there
     was no rule for its failure — so the authority re-rolled, which is a
     decision about method made after the method had started. A statute that
     delegates the instrument also delegates every question about what counts as
     a valid draw, and none of them are written down anywhere.
   ✱ NOTE THE PATTERN: THE SMALLER THE OFFICE, THE MORE INFORMAL THE DEVICE.
     Nothing in any statute scales the solemnity of the procedure to the stakes,
     so a city council seat and a chamber majority can be settled by the same
     bowl. The 2018 Virginia drawing — Part E — was for control of a legislature.

■ PART D — WHERE THE TIE SITS RELATIVE TO THE RECOUNT  (an ordering inversion)
│
├─▸ RECOUNT FIRST, THEN THE TIE-BREAK ..... ALASKA · NEBRASKA · NORTH DAKOTA ·
│     SOUTH DAKOTA · TEXAS · VERMONT · CONNECTICUT
│     the recount is a condition precedent: the tie-break procedure only runs
│     if the recount CONFIRMS the tie
│     [Alaska Stat. §§ 15.15.460, 15.20.530 · Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-1122 ·
│      N.D. Cent. Code §§ 16.1-15-30, 16.1-11-38 · S.D. Codified Laws § 12-21-43 ·
│      Tex. Elec. Code § 2.002 · Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 17 § 2602k ·
│      Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 9-311b, 9-332]
│
├─▸ TIE-BREAK FIRST, THEN THE RECOUNT ..... VIRGINIA
│     § 24.2-674 applies where the tie exists "prior to a recount," and the
│     candidate who LOSES THE DRAW "may petition for a recount pursuant to
│     Article 1 (§ 24.2-800 et seq.)"
│     ✱ SO IN VIRGINIA THE LOT IS DRAWN BEFORE ANYONE RE-COUNTS THE BALLOTS, AND
│       THE RECOUNT IS A REMEDY AGAINST THE LOT. The two instruments run in
│       exactly the opposite order from the seven states above, and the practical
│       consequence is that a Virginia candidate must decide whether to seek a
│       recount while already holding a certificate of loss issued by a bowl.
│
└─▸ THE RIGHT EXPRESSLY PRESERVED ......... NEVADA
      § 293.400(3): "The right to a recount extends to all candidates in case of
      a tie." ✱ Note the breadth — ALL candidates, including the one the lot or
      the Legislature just declared the winner.
      ⊘ NO STATE FOUND MAKES THE TIE-BREAK ITSELF REVIEWABLE. A recount attacks
        the count that produced the tie; nothing attacks the draw. A bowl is not
        a tribunal and there is nothing in it to appeal from.

■ PART E — ⚑ LIVE IN 2026, AND THE ONE CASE THAT DECIDED A CHAMBER
│
├─⚑ HAWAII, 15 AUGUST 2026 — TWO PRIMARY TIES BROKEN BY LOT ON ONE DAY
│    Chief Election Officer Scott Nago drew names from a box under
│    Haw. Rev. Stat. § 11-157 to resolve two tied 2026 primaries:
│      · Sen. Kurt Fevella v. Bob McDermott ....... McDermott drawn; advances
│      · Kanani Souza v. Sheila Medeiros .......... Medeiros drawn; succeeded Souza
│    Medeiros, having WON the draw, said the process "disenfranchises the
│    electorate" and announced legislation for the session convening January 2027.
│    ✱ THE OBJECTION CAME FROM THE WINNER, which is the strongest available
│      evidence that the complaint about lot is about legitimacy and not outcome.
│    ✱ AND NOTE WHAT A TIED PRIMARY COSTS: in Hawaii the draw replaces a
│      nomination, so the general-election voters never see the losing candidate
│      — whereas Nevada's nonpartisan rule at Part B would have advanced both.
│      Two states, the same tie, and one of them has an answer that costs nothing.
│
└─ VIRGINIA HOUSE DISTRICT 94 — 11,607 TO 11,607, DRAWN 4 JANUARY 2018
     The names went into film canisters, the canisters into a bowl, and the draw
     seated the Republican incumbent. Republicans kept the chamber; the other
     result was a 50-50 House of Delegates.
     ✱ THE TIE WAS MANUFACTURED BY A MARK-VALIDITY RULING. The race was not tied
       until a recount court re-examined one ballot, found the mark next to the
       Democrat's name SLASHED THROUGH, and read it as a vote for the Republican.
       One voter's ambiguous paper, read under the standards mapped on the
       ballot marking validity crosswalk, created the tie; a bowl resolved it;
       and control of a state legislature turned on both.
     ✱ AND THE VIRGINIA ORDERING FROM PART D WAS LIVE HERE. The losing candidate
       could have petitioned for a recount AFTER the draw, under § 24.2-674. What
       stopped it was not the statute but the legislature's own calendar: the
       session convened 10 January, six days later, and no recount panel could be
       convened in time. ✱ A STATUTORY RIGHT DEFEATED BY A SITTING DATE.

■ PART F — WHAT NO STATE TIE-BREAK CAN REACH
│
├─ U.S. Const. art. I § 5 cl. 1 makes EACH CHAMBER OF CONGRESS the judge of the
│  elections and returns of its own members.
│  ✱ SO TENNESSEE'S PROVISION GIVING A TIED U.S. HOUSE SEAT TO THE GOVERNOR, AND
│    VIRGINIA'S AND NEVADA'S PROVISIONS RESOLVING TIED CONGRESSIONAL SEATS BY LOT
│    OR BY LEGISLATIVE VOTE, PRODUCE A CERTIFICATE AND NOT A MEMBER. The House
│    seats whom it chooses to seat, and may decline. The state procedure is
│    evidence of a result, which art. I § 5 leaves the chamber free to weigh.
│  → the federal track is the Federal Contested Elections Act; the state-side
│    forum map is the state election contest crosswalk. Both linked below.
│  ⊘ NO REPORTED CASE FOUND in which a chamber refused to seat the winner of a
│    state tie-break. [unconfirmed whether one exists] — see Note 8.
│
└─ AND THE PRIOR QUESTION THIS MAP DOES NOT ANSWER: whether the totals were
   right. A tie is a number, and every instrument that produces or checks that
   number sits upstream — how a ballot is counted · ballot marking validity ·
   post-election audits · recount triggers · certification. All linked below.
   ✱ A TIE IS THE ONE RESULT WHERE EVERY UPSTREAM ERROR IS DECISIVE, because the
     margin of error exceeds the margin by definition. Which is the real argument
     against the bowl, and nobody's statute makes it.
