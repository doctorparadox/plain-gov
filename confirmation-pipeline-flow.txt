THE CONFIRMATION PIPELINE — ADVICE AND CONSENT, END TO END   (as of July 2026)
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path   └─▶ branch/bypass   ◇ decision   ✕ nomination dies   ★ confirmed
│            EXEC = executive-branch track   JUD = Article III track   BYPASS = no Senate vote
│
■ 0. THE UNIVERSE OF SEATS  (Art. II, § 2, cl. 2)
│     "...shall nominate, and by and with the Advice and Consent of the Senate, shall appoint
│      Ambassadors, other public Ministers and Consuls, Judges of the supreme Court, and all
│      other Officers of the United States..."
│     ├─ ~1,200 PAS positions (Presidential Appointment, Senate confirmation)
│     ├─ ~800 PA positions — presidential appointment, NO Senate vote (White House staff,
│     │    most of the Executive Office of the President: chief of staff, NSC, counsel)
│     ├─ ~4,000 political appointees in total, incl. Schedule C and non-career SES
│     └─ INFERIOR OFFICERS: Congress may vest their appointment in the President alone, the
│          courts, or a department head (Art. II, § 2) — the constitutional seam every
│          "acting official" fight is fought over
│
├─◆ 1. SELECTION  —  before the Senate ever sees a name
│     ├─ White House Presidential Personnel Office (PPO) sources and vets the candidate
│     ├─ outside pipelines supply the names: Federalist Society and allied networks for the
│     │    bench; think tanks, donors, and industry for the agencies; loyalty screening is
│     │    the explicit second-term criterion
│     ├─ FBI background investigation (SF-86) · Office of Government Ethics financial
│     │    disclosure (SF-278) and ethics agreement (recusals, divestitures)
│     └─ ◇ home-state consultation? ──yes──▶ smooths the blue-slip gate below
│                                  └─no───▶ a district-judge or U.S.-attorney pick can be
│                                            dead on arrival in a blue state
│
├─◆ 2. NOMINATION FORMALLY TRANSMITTED to the Senate ──▶ REFERRED to committee
│     │    (referral is automatic, by jurisdiction — the parliamentarian, not a vote)
│     ├─ Judiciary → all Article III judges, DOJ, U.S. attorneys, U.S. marshals, FBI
│     ├─ Armed Services → DoD civilians + every military promotion above O-6
│     ├─ Foreign Relations → ambassadors, State  ·  Finance → Treasury, HHS, IRS, CMS
│     ├─ Banking → Fed governors, SEC, FDIC  ·  HSGAC → DHS, OMB, OPM, GSA
│     └─ ...and so on across the standing committees (see the Committees Tree)
│
├─◆ 3. COMMITTEE STAGE  —  the first and now the FIRMEST chokepoint
│     ├─ questionnaire returned (Senate Judiciary Questionnaire for judges) + ABA rating
│     │    [the second-term White House stopped giving the ABA pre-nomination access]
│     ├─ ◇ BLUE SLIP  (Judiciary only — a courtesy, not a rule; the chair sets the policy)
│     │    ├─ DISTRICT judges, U.S. attorneys, U.S. marshals: chair Chuck Grassley still
│     │    │    HONORS the blue slip — either home-state senator can withhold it
│     │    │      └─▶ ✕ no hearing, no vote  (the single most effective minority tool left;
│     │    │            it has killed or forced the withdrawal of multiple U.S.-attorney
│     │    │            picks in blue states, and drew public presidential attacks on
│     │    │            Grassley in 2025 — he did not abandon the practice)
│     │    └─ CIRCUIT judges: blue slip ABANDONED since 2017 — home-state objection is
│     │         noted and ignored
│     ├─ HEARING (usually public; judges answer in writing afterward — "questions for the
│     │    record") ──▶ committee vote to REPORT
│     │    ├─ reported favorably ──────────────────────▶ to the Executive Calendar
│     │    ├─ reported without recommendation ─────────▶ to the Executive Calendar
│     │    └─ ◇ tie or majority against ──▶ ✕ dies in committee (the chair can also simply
│     │         never schedule it — the pocket kill; no rule compels a hearing)
│     └─ [the chair's scheduling power IS the gate: it is what a committee majority is FOR]
│
├─◆ 4. THE FLOOR  —  where the friction used to be, and mostly no longer is
│     │
│     ├─ EXEC ▸ ◇ UNANIMOUS CONSENT? ──yes──▶ voice-voted through, no roll call
│     │              (still how the large majority of nominees are actually confirmed)
│     │         └─no──▶ a HOLD is in effect ──▶ the majority leader must burn floor time
│     │
│     ├─ ▸ THE HOLD — any single senator, anonymously, indefinitely. Not a rule; a threat
│     │      to withhold unanimous consent. Post-2025 it no longer stops a nominee — it
│     │      only makes them expensive. Blanket holds on whole categories (e.g. military
│     │      promotions) are the modern escalation.
│     │
│     ├─ ▸ CLOTURE — motion filed ──▶ ripens ──▶ ◇ SIMPLE MAJORITY (51)
│     │      ├─ 1789–2013 ................. 60 votes (the nominations filibuster)
│     │      ├─ Nov 2013 nuclear option ... 51 — executive + lower-court judges (Reid)
│     │      ├─ Apr 2017 nuclear option ... 51 — Supreme Court too (McConnell/Gorsuch)
│     │      └─ 2019 rule change .......... post-cloture debate cut 30 hrs ──▶ 2 hrs for
│     │           most executive and district nominees (a time change, not a vote change)
│     │
│     ├─ ▸ EN BLOC BATCHING — the Sept 11, 2025 nuclear option (53–45)
│     │      ├─ an UNLIMITED number of executive nominees may be bundled into ONE cloture
│     │      │    vote and ONE confirmation vote, by simple majority
│     │      ├─ first use Sept 18–19, 2025: 48 nominees confirmed in a single vote
│     │      ├─ COVERS: sub-cabinet, ambassadors, U.S. attorneys, mid-level agency officials
│     │      └─ DOES NOT COVER: Article III judges, or cabinet secretaries
│     │           [so the judicial track below still runs one nominee at a time]
│     │
│     ├─ JUD ▸ ARTICLE III — no batching. Each judge: cloture (51) ──▶ up to 2 hrs debate
│     │      (30 hrs for circuit + SCOTUS) ──▶ ◇ CONFIRMATION VOTE, simple majority
│     │      └─▶ ◇ 50–50 tie? ──▶ the VICE PRESIDENT breaks it (used for Cabinet and for
│     │            judges — the tie-break is why a 50-seat majority is a working majority)
│     │
│     └─ ◇ CONFIRMATION VOTE ──pass──▶ ★ commission signed, oath taken, officer seated
│                              └─fail──▶ ✕ returned to the President
│
├─◆ 5. WHEN THE PIPELINE IS BYPASSED ENTIRELY
│     │
│     ├─ BYPASS ▸ FEDERAL VACANCIES REFORM ACT of 1998 (5 U.S.C. § 3345)
│     │      ├─ the President may install an ACTING official with NO Senate vote:
│     │      │    (a) the first assistant, by default · (b) any other PAS officer already
│     │      │    confirmed to some other post · (c) a senior employee of that agency
│     │      │        (GS-15+, 90 days' service in the prior year)
│     │      ├─ TIME LIMIT: 210 days from the vacancy — but the clock RESETS while a
│     │      │    nomination is pending, and resets again after a failed nomination, so a
│     │      │    serial-nomination strategy can hold an acting official in place for years
│     │      ├─ ENFORCEMENT: an action taken by an improperly serving official is void —
│     │      │    but someone must sue, and standing is hard. The check is a lawsuit, not a
│     │      │    vote, which is why the acting-official pattern keeps growing.
│     │      └─ agency-specific succession statutes can override the FVRA entirely
│     │
│     ├─ BYPASS ▸ RECESS APPOINTMENT (Art. II, § 2, cl. 3) — dormant, and deliberately so
│     │      ├─ a recess appointee serves without any vote through the end of the NEXT
│     │      │    session of Congress (up to ~2 years)
│     │      ├─ NLRB v. Noel Canning (2014, 9–0): the recess must run at least ~10 DAYS
│     │      ├─ ◇ so: is the Senate ever in recess for 10 days?  ──▶ NO. PRO FORMA SESSIONS
│     │      │    every 3 days keep it technically in session. Thune scheduled them through
│     │      │    the Aug 2025 state work period and again around the spring 2026 break —
│     │      │    the Senate blocking its OWN majority's president, on purpose.
│     │      └─ ◇ the Art. II, § 3 ADJOURNMENT POWER — if the two chambers disagree on
│     │           adjournment, the President "may adjourn them" and manufacture the recess.
│     │           NEVER USED in American history. Requires the House to first pass an
│     │           adjournment resolution the Senate refuses. Repeatedly floated 2024–2026.
│     │
│     └─ BYPASS ▸ THE NON-CONFIRMED POWER CENTER — the structural answer to a slow Senate:
│            move the real authority to seats that need no confirmation at all — White
│            House czars, the EOP, senior advisers, and "special government employees" —
│            and leave the Senate to confirm the figurehead. Advice and consent is a check
│            on OFFICES, not on POWER; power migrates to the offices it can't reach.
│
└─◆ 6. WHAT HAPPENS TO NOMINATIONS THAT DON'T MAKE IT
      ├─ RETURNED at the end of a session or on a recess >30 days (Senate Rule XXXI, ¶ 6)
      │    ──▶ the President may resubmit; most are, by unanimous consent
      ├─ WITHDRAWN by the President — the quiet exit; the second term has set the modern
      │    record for first-year withdrawals, most of them blue-slip and vetting casualties
      └─ NEVER SCHEDULED — the most common death of all, and it leaves no record
