THE PARDON AND CLEMENCY PIPELINE  —  the formal track, the informal track, and the missing checks
│   Legend: ──▶ main path  └─▶ branch  ◇ decision  ★ operative act  ✕ closed  ⚑ structural point
│   Process evergreen · seats and dispositions verified 2026-09-11 · cites to 28 C.F.R. Part 1
│
■ 0. THE FLOOR  —  one sentence of constitutional text, and four limits inside it
│
│   Art. II, §2, cl. 1: the President "shall have Power to grant Reprieves and Pardons for
│   Offences against the United States, except in Cases of Impeachment."
│
│   ⚑ FOUR LIMITS LIVE IN THAT SENTENCE — AND THEY ARE THE ONLY ONES.
│   ├─ 1. FEDERAL ONLY. "Offences against the United States" excludes every state crime.
│   │       A pardoned defendant remains exposed to state prosecution for the same conduct.
│   │       ✕ No president can reach a state charge, a state conviction, or a state fine.
│   ├─ 2. NOT IMPEACHMENT. The one express carve-out. A pardon cannot stop, undo, or
│   │       reverse an impeachment, a Senate trial, or a judgment of disqualification.
│   ├─ 3. PAST CONDUCT ONLY. The offence must already have been committed. A pardon
│   │       may issue BEFORE charge, indictment, or conviction (Ex parte Garland, 1866;
│   │       the Nixon pardon, 1974) — but it cannot license future conduct.
│   └─ 4. CRIMINAL ONLY. It reaches criminal penalties. It does NOT reach civil liability,
│           private suits, professional discipline, immigration consequences in every case,
│           or (in the ordinary case) a civil judgment owed to a victim.
│
│   ⚑ EVERYTHING ELSE PEOPLE BELIEVE IS A LIMIT IS ACTUALLY A REGULATION.
│   The waiting period, the application form, the FBI check, the "acceptance of
│   responsibility" criterion, the Pardon Attorney's recommendation — all 28 C.F.R.
│   Part 1, all internal to DOJ, all waivable by the President in any case, silently.
│
■ 1. THE FIVE FORMS  —  what the power can actually issue
│
│   ├─ FULL PARDON .............. forgives the offence; restores civil rights (vote, firearms
│   │                             under federal law, office); does NOT expunge the conviction
│   │                             and does NOT erase the record.
│   ├─ COMMUTATION ............. shortens or ends the SENTENCE; leaves the conviction intact
│   │                             and does NOT restore civil rights.
│   ├─ REMISSION ............... cancels a fine, forfeiture, or restitution obligation.
│   │                             ★ This is the form that moves money. See block 7.
│   ├─ REPRIEVE ................ postpones execution of a sentence (temporary, not final).
│   └─ BLANKET / CATEGORICAL ... a proclamation covering a defined CLASS rather than named
│                                individuals (the J6 proclamation, Jan. 20, 2025; Carter's
│                                1977 draft-evader amnesty). No petitions, no files, no names.
│
│   ◇ Preemptive (pre-charge) clemency? ──yes──▶ lawful; no petition exists to process;
│     Track A has no role at all. This is the purest form of Track B.
│
■ 2. TRACK A  —  THE FORMAL ROUTE  (28 C.F.R. §§ 1.1–1.11)
│
│   ◆ 2a. ELIGIBILITY GATE  (§§ 1.1–1.2) — a filing rule, not a power limit
│   │   ├─ Pardon: wait ≥ 5 YEARS from release from confinement, or from conviction if
│   │   │     no prison term was imposed (§ 1.2).
│   │   ├─ Generally no petition while on probation, parole, or supervised release.
│   │   ├─ Commutation: petition while still serving (§ 1.3); exhaust other remedies first.
│   │   └─ ⚑ WHO IS EXEMPT FROM THE FIVE-YEAR RULE: everyone the President wants to be.
│   │         The waiting period binds the CLERK, not the PRESIDENT. A grant that skips it
│   │         is not irregular and not reviewable — it simply never entered Track A.
│   │
│   ◆ 2b. PETITION FILED  (§ 1.1) — addressed to the President, delivered to the Pardon
│   │       Attorney. Sworn. Offence, sentence, restitution status, employment, references.
│   │
│   ◆ 2c. OFFICE OF THE PARDON ATTORNEY — investigation and recommendation
│   │   ├─ FBI background investigation · court and BOP records pulled
│   │   ├─ Views solicited from the prosecuting U.S. Attorney's office (§ 1.6)
│   │   ├─ Victim input where applicable
│   │   ├─ Merit criteria applied: post-conviction conduct, seriousness and recency of the
│   │   │     offence, acceptance of responsibility, need for relief, official recommendations
│   │   └─ ★ WRITTEN RECOMMENDATION prepared for the Deputy Attorney General (§ 1.6)
│   │         ⚑ Advisory only. § 1.11 says so in terms: the regulations are "advisory only"
│   │           and create NO enforceable rights in any person.
│   │
│   ◆ 2d. DEPUTY ATTORNEY GENERAL  (28 C.F.R. § 0.35–0.36) — the DOJ signature layer
│   │   └─▶ transmits the recommendation, or does not. A recommendation that stops here
│   │         is not appealable and the petitioner is not told why.
│   │
│   ◆ 2e. WHITE HOUSE COUNSEL'S OFFICE — the real chokepoint of Track A
│   │   └─▶ staff review → Counsel to the President → Chief of Staff controls whether
│   │         the case ever reaches the Oval Office at all.
│   │
│   ◆ 2f. ★ THE PRESIDENT SIGNS — or does nothing, forever. Denial and inaction are
│           indistinguishable to the petitioner. § 1.8: notice of denial issues, and the
│           petitioner may refile after two years. No reasons are given.
│
│   ⚑ TRACK A'S THROUGHPUT IS A POLICY CHOICE, NOT A CAPACITY LIMIT.
│   ≈14,000 clemency applications were pending before DOJ as of Aug. 2026 (House Judiciary
│   Cmte. minority staff report, Aug. 21, 2026). The backlog had been cut from ≈18,000 to
│   ≈3,000 under the career Pardon Attorney removed in March 2025, with ≈6,000+ pending on
│   the day of that removal. The office is staffed at roughly 40 attorney and non-attorney
│   personnel. Nothing in Track A is fast, and nothing in Track B has to be.
│
■ 3. TRACK B  —  THE INFORMAL ROUTE  (no regulation governs this and none can)
│
│   ENTRY POINTS ──▶ ◆ direct access to the President (rally, club, dinner, phone)
│                ──▶ ◆ the White House "pardon czar" — a staff role created Feb. 2025 with
│                │        no statutory basis, no confirmation, and no place in Part 1
│                ──▶ ◆ the White House Counsel's office (staff attorney → Counsel)
│                ──▶ ◆ the Chief of Staff, who controls the Oval Office schedule
│                ──▶ ◆ family members and longtime allies outside the government
│                ──▶ ◆ retained advocates: lawyers, lobbyists, former officials
│
│   ├─ NO petition required.          ├─ NO five-year wait.
│   ├─ NO FBI investigation.          ├─ NO prosecutor's views solicited.
│   ├─ NO victim notice.              ├─ NO DOJ file — so no FOIA-able record of the ask.
│   └─ NO written recommendation, therefore nothing later to compare the grant against.
│
│   ◇ What makes a Track B case succeed? Two things, per every account of the operation:
│     └─▶ ACCESS (who can physically get the case in front of the President), and
│     └─▶ NARRATIVE (whether the case can be framed as political persecution — a
│           weaponized prosecution, a hostile judge, a Biden-era target).
│
│   ⚑ TRACK B IS NOT A LOOPHOLE. IT IS THE CONSTITUTIONAL DEFAULT.
│   Track A is the thing that was built on top. When the two disagree, Track A loses,
│   and there is no forum in which to say so.
│
│   ⚑ THE MARKET THAT FORMS AROUND AN UNREVIEWABLE POWER.
│   Because access is the scarce input, access is what gets priced. Advocacy fees in the
│   millions have been reported by people inside the process, including fees taken from
│   defendants on whose behalf nothing was ever done — a market with no disclosure regime,
│   no registration requirement that clearly covers it, and no remedy for nonperformance.
│
■ 4. THE ACT ITSELF  —  what it takes to make a pardon legally effective
│
│   ├─ FORM: the Constitution specifies none. The Fourth Circuit has held that a pardon
│   │     need not be written or signed to be effective — the text imposes no such limit.
│   │     ⚑ This is why the autopen argument fails on its own terms: if no signature is
│   │       required at all, the manner of signature cannot be what voids a grant. The
│   │       live question is AUTHORIZATION (did the President decide?), not INK.
│   ├─ DELIVERY AND ACCEPTANCE: a pardon must be accepted to take effect as to the
│   │     recipient (Burdick v. United States, 1915 — a pardon may be REFUSED, and Burdick
│   │     refused one to keep his Fifth Amendment privilege). A commutation, by contrast,
│   │     may be imposed without consent.
│   └─ ✕ NO REVOCATION. Once delivered and accepted, a pardon is done. A later President
│         cannot void a predecessor's grant by proclamation, post, or order. An assertion
│         that prior grants are "null and void" has no operative effect on its own; it
│         would have to be tested by charging someone and litigating the defence.
│
■ 5. WHAT A PARDON DOES AND DOES NOT DO
│
│   DOES ──▶ ends the punishment · restores federal civil rights (vote in federal elections
│        where state law follows, hold federal office, federal firearms disability lifted)
│        · removes the conviction as a bar to many federal benefits and licenses
│
│   DOES NOT ──▶ ✕ expunge or seal the conviction — the record survives
│            ──▶ ✕ establish innocence — acceptance has long been read as carrying an
│                    imputation of guilt (Burdick)
│            ──▶ ✕ reach state charges arising from the same conduct
│            ──▶ ✕ automatically restore state-law rights (voting, firearms, licensure)
│            ──▶ ✕ bar a congressional subpoena — and, once criminal exposure is gone,
│                    the Fifth Amendment privilege against testifying largely goes with it
│
■ 6. THE CHECKS  —  drawn as a list of things that are not there
│
│   ✕ NO Senate vote.            ✕ NO judicial review of a grant or a denial.
│   ✕ NO statutory criteria.     ✕ NO disclosure requirement for who asked, or was paid.
│   ✕ NO reversal.               ✕ NO conflict-of-interest rule that reaches the President.
│   ✕ NO appeal from a denial.   ✕ NO recusal mechanism for a self-interested grant.
│
│   WHAT ACTUALLY REMAINS:
│   ├─ IMPEACHMENT — the only formal remedy, and the one proceeding a pardon cannot touch
│   ├─ CONGRESSIONAL OVERSIGHT — hearings, staff reports, subpoenas (enforcement is weak)
│   ├─ BRIBERY PROSECUTION of the INTERMEDIARIES — the pardon is unreviewable; a payment
│   │     made to obtain one is still a federal crime for everyone below the President
│   ├─ STATE PROSECUTORS — the jurisdiction no pardon reaches
│   └─ ELECTIONS
│
│   ◇ Can the President pardon himself? ──▶ UNRESOLVED. Never done, never litigated,
│     never decided by any court. A 1974 DOJ Office of Legal Counsel memorandum concluded
│     he could not, on the nemo iudex principle; OLC binds the executive, not a court.
│     ⚑ The map states this as open. Anyone who tells you it is settled is wrong in both
│       directions.
│
■ 7. ⚑ THE MONEY DOOR  —  remission, and why it is the part that matters most
│
│   A full pardon in the ordinary case does not cancel a restitution obligation already
│   reduced to a civil judgment — but a REMISSION expressly can, and a commutation can be
│   drafted to relieve the balance of a financial penalty along with the sentence.
│
│   ──▶ fine remitted ──▶ forfeiture remitted ──▶ restitution obligation lifted
│       └─▶ the victims' claim is extinguished by an act no victim can contest,
│             in a proceeding with no notice, no hearing, and no record.
│
│   SCALE (attributed, not settled): the House Judiciary Committee minority staff put the
│   second-term total of erased restitution, fines, and forfeitures at ≈$1.7 BILLION as of
│   Aug. 21, 2026, up from its own ≈$1.3 billion estimate a year earlier. These are the
│   minority's figures and include contested valuation choices; no neutral official total
│   exists, because no office is required to compute one.
│
│   ⚑ THIS IS THE STRUCTURAL POINT OF THE WHOLE MAP. The clemency power is generally
│   discussed as mercy toward a prisoner. Its largest measurable effect in 2025–26 has
│   been the transfer of money away from crime victims and the Treasury — through the
│   one instrument in American government with no review, no disclosure, and no appeal.
│

■ 9. WHAT THE SHAPE SHOWS
│
│   1. TRACK A IS A COURTESY. Every step in it is regulatory, every regulation is
│      advisory by its own terms (§ 1.11), and none of it binds the one person deciding.
│   2. THE FIVE-YEAR RULE IS A FILING DEADLINE FOR STRANGERS. It screens the people
│      who have to apply. It has never screened anyone who could telephone instead.
│   3. THE GATEKEEPERS ARE SCHEDULERS. Chief of Staff, Counsel, czar — the contest is
│      over whose case gets in front of the President, not over whether it qualifies.
│   4. THE ABSENCE OF REVIEW CREATES A MARKET. An unreviewable, undisclosed, unappealable
│      power with a scarce input (access) prices that input. That is not a scandal about
│      individuals; it is what this structure does.
│   5. THE ONLY REAL LIMITS ARE JURISDICTIONAL. State charge, impeachment, future conduct.
│      A state attorney general is a harder constraint on this power than a federal judge,
│      because a federal judge has no role in it at all.
