■ STEP 1 · THE DECISION                                    (FOMC · 8 scheduled meetings a year)
│   Inputs ...... staff Tealbook · Beige Book · CPI and PCE · payrolls · financial conditions
│   Vote ........ 12 votes = 7 governors + New York + 4 rotating Reserve Bank presidents
│   Output ...... a TARGET RANGE for the federal funds rate + a directive to the NY Desk
│                 [Jul 28–29, 2026: HELD at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent on a 9–3 vote]
│                 [all three dissents wanted a HIKE — Hammack, Kashkari, Logan]
│   Also issued . statement language · SEP and dot plot (4x/yr) · chair press conference
│   ⏱ effect on the money market: SAME DAY
│
├──▶ STEP 2 · THE ADMINISTERED RATES  (the target range is a goal; these are the actual levers)
│    │   In an AMPLE-RESERVES regime the Fed does not ration reserves — it sets prices.
│    ├─ IORB ................ 3.65 %   interest on reserve balances, paid to banks; the rate
│    │                                 every other overnight rate is arbitraged against
│    ├─ ON RRP .............. 3.50 %   overnight reverse repo, open to money funds and GSEs;
│    │                                 the FLOOR for lenders with no Fed account
│    │                                 [per-counterparty cap $160B/day]
│    ├─ Standing repo ....... 3.75 %   the Fed lends against Treasuries at a fixed rate;
│    │                                 the CEILING that caps repo-market spikes
│    ├─ Discount window ..... 3.75 %   primary credit — same rate, but stigmatized in use
│    └─ Open-market ops ..... reserve-management T-bill purchases (plumbing, not stimulus)
│                             [runoff ENDED Dec 1, 2025 · assets $6.76T · reserves ~$3.1T]
│   ⏱ SAME DAY → the effective fed funds rate and SOFR settle inside the corridor
│
├──▶ STEP 3 · MONEY-MARKET AND BANK FUNDING COSTS                                   ⏱ DAYS
│    ├─ SOFR · EFFR · commercial paper · Treasury bills all reprice off the corridor
│    ├─ Deposit and CD rates follow — SLOWLY and INCOMPLETELY (the "deposit beta" gap)
│    └─ The PRIME RATE moves mechanically: fed funds target + 3 percentage points
│         └─▶ credit cards · HELOCs · small-business and floating-rate corporate loans
│
├──▶ STEP 4 · THE YIELD CURVE AND EXPECTATIONS    ⏱ IMMEDIATE, but only partly controllable
│    │   A long rate = the average expected short rate over its life + a TERM PREMIUM.
│    │   The Fed sets the first term. It does NOT set the second.
│    ├─ Tools aimed at the first term: forward guidance · the dot plot · QE and QT
│    ├─ ◇ Does the market believe the path? ──yes──▶ long rates follow the policy rate
│    │                                       └─no──▶ long rates DECOUPLE
│    └─ [AUG 2026 — DECOUPLED. Policy sits at 3.50–3.75 %, the 10-year near 4.65 %, and
│        the 30-year auction cleared 5.22 %, the highest since 2001. That gap is term
│        premium — supply, foreign demand, and credibility — not the expected rate path.]
│
├──▶ STEP 5 · THE FIVE CHANNELS  (where policy actually reaches households and firms)
│    │
│    ├─◆ INTEREST-RATE / COST-OF-CAPITAL ..... hurdle rates rise → capex, housing, durables
│    │      └─▶ MORTGAGES key off the 10-year, NOT off the fed funds rate
│    │          [30-yr fixed 6.65 % on Aug 20, 2026 — roughly 200bp over the 10-year]
│    ├─◆ BANK-LENDING / CREDIT .............. funding costs + capital + risk appetite →
│    │      └─▶ tighter standards and thinner credit for bank-dependent borrowers
│    │          (small firms feel this; investment-grade issuers route around it)
│    ├─◆ ASSET-PRICE / WEALTH ............... discount rates up → equities and property down
│    │      └─▶ spending falls, concentrated in the households that own the assets
│    ├─◆ EXCHANGE-RATE ...................... relative rates → capital flows → the dollar
│    │      └─▶ dollar up = cheaper imports (disinflation) + dearer exports (demand down)
│    │          [muted in 2026: tariffs push import prices the OTHER way]
│    └─◆ EXPECTATIONS ........................ the channel with no plumbing —
│           └─▶ credibility alone. If households and firms believe 2 %, wage- and
│               price-setting does the Fed's work for it. If they don't, nothing
│               else on this list is large enough to substitute.
│
├──▶ STEP 6 · AGGREGATE DEMAND                                            ⏱ 6–12 MONTHS
│    Investment · consumption · net exports · hiring, job postings, quits
│         └─▶ the output gap opens or closes
│
└──▶ STEP 7 · THE DUAL MANDATE   (12 U.S.C. § 225a)                       ⏱ 12–24 MONTHS
     ├─ MAXIMUM EMPLOYMENT ..... [unemployment 4.1 % · payrolls −23,000 in July 2026,
     │                            with −103,000 in revisions to the two prior months]
     └─ STABLE PRICES (2 %) .... [headline CPI 3.4 % y/y · core CPI 2.5 % y/y, July 2026]
          └─▶ back to STEP 1 — the Fed steers a car whose wheels turn a year later

WHERE THE CHAIN BREAKS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ✕ THE LONG END     The Fed owns the overnight rate outright and the 30-year not at all.
                    Term premium is priced by supply, foreign demand, and credibility.
 ✕ THE LAGS         "Long and variable." By the time the data show whether a decision was
                    right, three or four more decisions have already been made.
 ✕ SUPPLY SHOCKS    Rates work on DEMAND. A tariff, an oil disruption, or a war raises
                    prices with no excess demand to remove — so the only way to hit the
                    inflation leg of the mandate is to damage the employment leg.
 ✕ FISCAL FLOW      Deficits, tariffs, and Treasury issuance move prices and the curve
                    directly, with no FOMC vote involved anywhere in the process.
 ✕ CREDIBILITY      The expectations channel is not a mechanism, it is a BELIEF — and it
                    is the only channel that can be destroyed by a personnel decision.
