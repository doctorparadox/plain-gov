RECOUNT TRIGGER CROSSWALK — the three tiers, and why "0.5%" is five different
numbers                                                       (as of 5 October 2026)
│  Legend:  ▸ tier / column   ⚡ trigger   ◇ who must act   $ who pays
│           ↩ what earns a refund   ⚖ does it replace the certified result
│           ⊘ does not exist   ⚑ changed for November 2026
│           ◈ count is a definitional artifact   † statute over tracker
│
│  THE ONE-LINE VERSION: THERE ARE THREE TIERS, NOT TWO. The middle one — the loser
│  must ask, the state must grant, the public pays — is where every published
│  disagreement about this subject lives. See Part A, then Part B before trusting
│  ANY threshold on this or any other fifty-state recount table.
│
■ PART A — THE THREE TIERS  (who has to act, and who pays for it)
│
├─▸ TIER 1. AUTOMATIC / MANDATORY ............... 26 states + DC   ◈ see Note 1
│     ⚡ the certified margin falls inside a statutory threshold
│     ◇ NOBODY. No candidate files, no official exercises discretion, no court
│       orders it. The canvassing authority's duty attaches on the arithmetic.
│     $ the state or the county, always
│     ✱ THIS IS THE ONLY TIER THAT PROTECTS A LOSER WHO HAS RUN OUT OF MONEY,
│       RUN OUT OF LAWYERS, OR CONCEDED. It is also the only tier that cannot be
│       waived — which is why the 2024 Pennsylvania Senate recount had to be
│       affirmatively stopped after the trailing candidate conceded, rather than
│       simply never starting.
│     ⚑ GEORGIA ADDED A SECOND TIER-1 TRIGGER FOR THIS ELECTION. SB 3EX (2026
│       special session) requires a FULL STATEWIDE HAND RECOUNT at a 0.5% margin
│       for eight statewide offices — Governor, Lt. Governor, Secretary of State,
│       Attorney General, School Superintendent, and the Commissioners of
│       Insurance, Agriculture and Labor — but ONLY where one of them appears
│       FIRST OR SECOND on the ballot. The hand count becomes the official result;
│       counties finishing within 17 days of the order are reimbursed.
│       ✱ A BALLOT-POSITION CONDITION ON A RECOUNT TRIGGER EXISTS NOWHERE ELSE
│         IN AMERICAN ELECTION LAW. It is a workload cap written as a rule about
│         typography, and it means two races at identical margins get different
│         procedures depending on where they were printed.
│
├─▸ TIER 2. MANDATORY ON REQUEST, PUBLICLY FUNDED ... the missing category
│     ⚡ the margin falls inside a threshold AND the trailing candidate files
│     ◇ THE LOSER — but the authority has NO DISCRETION TO REFUSE
│     $ the state or the county. No bond, no deposit, nothing at risk.
│     → the worked statutes:
│       MINNESOTA ...... ≤0.25% federal and statewide · ≤0.5% legislative · or a
│                        10-vote margin where 400 or fewer votes were cast; the
│                        request is due within TWO BUSINESS DAYS of the canvass
│                        [Minn. Stat. § 204C.35 subd. 1 — "shall" on request]
│       MONTANA ........ ≤0.25% state-funded on request; >0.25% but ≤0.5% on the
│                        candidate's own money — ONE STATUTE, TWO TIERS, and the
│                        ONLY variable between them is the margin
│       WISCONSIN ...... free to the requester at ≤0.25%
│       CALIFORNIA ..... state pays below 1,000 votes or 0.5%
│       IOWA ........... state pays below 0.5%
│     ✱ THIS TIER IS WHY THE PUBLISHED COUNTS DISAGREE BY EIGHT STATES. A tracker
│       that asks "does a close margin get you a recount you do not pay for?"
│       counts Minnesota as automatic. A tracker that asks "does the recount
│       happen if nobody files?" does not. BOTH ANSWERS ARE CORRECT TO THEIR OWN
│       QUESTION, and neither tracker states which question it asked.
│     ✱ THE PRACTICAL DIFFERENCE FROM TIER 1 IS ONE THING ONLY: a two-day filing
│       deadline. A tier-2 right is extinguished by a trailing candidate who
│       concedes early, travels, or simply does not know the rule. Tier 1 is not.
│
└─▸ TIER 3. DISCRETIONARY, AT THE REQUESTER'S EXPENSE ... the default everywhere
      ⚡ nothing. The margin is outside every threshold.
      ◇ THE LOSER, and the gate is money rather than arithmetic
      $ the requester, up front, by deposit or bond
      → TEXAS ......... $60 per precinct on paper ballots, $100 per precinct on
                        electronic systems  [Tex. Elec. Code § 212.112]
      → PENNSYLVANIA .. $50 cash or a $100 bond PER ELECTION DISTRICT, and the
                        petition must come from three qualified electors of that
                        district — a CANDIDATE HAS NO STANDING TO ASK AT ALL
                        [25 P.S. §§ 3261(b), 3263(a)(1)]
      → MINNESOTA ..... a bond for the full expense  [§ 204C.35 subd. 2]
      → MICHIGAN ...... a per-precinct deposit scaled to the margin itself, so the
                        further behind the petitioner is, the more it costs
                        [Mich. Comp. Laws §§ 168.867, 168.881]
      ✱ THE QUEUE ITEM'S PREDICTION IS CORRECT ABOUT THIS TIER AND ONLY THIS TIER:
        a tier-3 recount is a resource check, and the better-funded campaign wins
        it by default. Trump's 2020 Wisconsin county recounts cost $3 million and
        moved 87 votes the other way. What the prediction missed is that tier 3 is
        not the other half of a two-part system — it is the residue left after two
        other tiers have already handled every margin close enough to matter.
│
├─▸ AND A FOURTH GATE THAT IS NEITHER MARGIN NOR MONEY: THE MERITS
│     MICHIGAN requires a petitioning candidate to state "a good-faith belief
│     that, but for error in the canvass or returns of the votes, the candidate
│     would have had a reasonable chance of winning"  [MCL § 168.862]
│     PENNSYLVANIA requires an allegation of specific fraud or error WITH
│     SUPPORTING EVIDENCE only for a limited recount; a full-district recount
│     needs none  [25 P.S. § 3263(a)(1)]
│     ✱ A MERITS GATE IS A DIFFERENT ANIMAL FROM A MARGIN GATE. A margin is
│       arithmetic anyone can check. "A reasonable chance of winning" is a
│       judgment, made by the body whose own count is being questioned.
│
├─▸ TIER 0. NO ADMINISTRATIVE RECOUNT AT ALL ...... ILLINOIS · MISSISSIPPI ·
│     TENNESSEE                                      ◈ contested — see Note 4
│     ⊘ there is no recount to trigger, request or pay for
│     → the only route to a second count is an ELECTION CONTEST — a lawsuit, on a
│       court's calendar, under a standard of proof set by state law
│     ✱ THESE THREE STATES ARE THE AUDIT FINDING'S MIRROR IMAGE. The audit map
│       found states where a check runs and cannot change the number. Here no
│       check runs at all, and the number can only be changed by litigation.
│
■ PART B — THE DENOMINATOR PROBLEM  (why no column on any such table is comparable)
│
│  Every fifty-state recount table prints a percentage column. The percentages are
│  not percentages of the same thing. FIVE DIFFERENT DENOMINATORS ARE IN USE:
│
├─▸ 1. ALL VOTES CAST IN THE CONTEST .............. the modal form
│     ALABAMA "no more than .5% of the votes cast" · HAWAII "of the total number
│     of votes cast for the contest" · MICHIGAN "of the total number of votes cast
│     in a statewide election" · PENNSYLVANIA "of votes cast"
│
├─▸ 2. THE TOP TWO CANDIDATES' COMBINED TOTAL
│     ARIZONA "the difference in total votes cast for the top two candidates" ·
│     OREGON "no more than .2% of total votes for both candidates"
│     ✱ in a three-way race this is a SMALLER denominator than form 1, so the same
│       stated percentage is a TIGHTER threshold — fewer races qualify.
│
├─▸ 3. THE WINNING CANDIDATE'S OWN TOTAL
│     COLORADO "less than or equal to one-half of one percent OF THE HIGHEST VOTE
│     CAST in that election contest"  [C.R.S. 1-10.5-101(1)(b)]
│     ✱ in a two-way race this is roughly HALF the denominator of form 1, so
│       Colorado's "0.5%" is approximately a 0.25% threshold in form-1 terms.
│       It is printed in the same column as Alabama's.
│
├─▸ 4. THE LOSING CANDIDATE'S OWN TOTAL
│     TEXAS "less than 10 percent of THAT CANDIDATE'S number of votes" — the
│     petitioner's own total  [Tex. Elec. Code § 212.022]
│     ✱ THIS IS NOT A MARGIN THRESHOLD AT ALL. It is a ratio between two
│       candidates, and it is enormously wider than it looks: a candidate who
│       loses 55–45 is inside it. Texas's eligibility rule is the loosest in the
│       country and its automatic trigger is the narrowest possible — a tie.
│
└─▸ 5. NO PERCENTAGE. A RAW NUMBER OF VOTES.
      MICHIGAN ...... state senate ≤75 votes · state representative ≤25 votes
      CONNECTICUT ... fewer than 20 votes  ·  NEW YORK ... 20 votes or fewer
      DELAWARE ...... fewer than 1,000 votes or 0.5%, WHICHEVER IS LESS
      HAWAII ........ 100 votes or 0.25%, WHICHEVER IS GREATER †
      WASHINGTON .... fewer than 2,000 votes AND less than 0.5% — a CONJUNCTION,
                      so a large close race can fail the vote test and not recount
      NEW YORK ...... or fewer than 5,000 votes where 1,000,000+ ballots were cast
      ✱ "WHICHEVER IS LESS", "WHICHEVER IS GREATER" AND "AND" ARE THREE DIFFERENT
        LOGICAL OPERATORS JOINING THE SAME TWO TESTS, and they point in opposite
        directions. Delaware's pair narrows the trigger as the electorate grows;
        Hawaii's widens it; Washington's requires both and is therefore the
        narrowest of the three. No table in circulation marks the operator.
│
■ PART C — WHO MAY ASK  (standing, which is not the same question as the margin)
│
├─▸ A DEFEATED CANDIDATE ........................ the near-universal rule
│
├─▸ NO CANDIDATE MAY ASK — AUTOMATIC TRIGGER ONLY ... 7 states
│     ARIZONA · CONNECTICUT · FLORIDA · HAWAII · NEW YORK · PENNSYLVANIA ·
│     SOUTH CAROLINA
│     ✱ IN THESE SEVEN STATES A LOSER OUTSIDE THE THRESHOLD HAS NO RECOUNT AT ANY
│       PRICE. The margin is not a cost gate, it is the whole of the law, and the
│       only remaining route is a contest in court.
│     ◈ one tracker prints this list as NINE by folding in Illinois, Mississippi
│       and Tennessee — which have no recount at all, a different category — and
│       DROPPING Pennsylvania, where candidates genuinely cannot ask. See Note 3.
│
├─▸ VOTERS OR ELECTORS, BY PETITION
│     PENNSYLVANIA ... three qualified electors per election district — the ONLY
│                      route in the state, and it is organised by district rather
│                      than by race, so a statewide recount is assembled district
│                      by district  [25 P.S. § 3261]
│     MASSACHUSETTS .. 1,000 or more voters
│     TEXAS .......... 25 or more voters jointly, ballot measures only
│     also: ALABAMA · ALASKA · CALIFORNIA · NEVADA · NEW JERSEY · SOUTH DAKOTA
│     ✱ A PETITION THRESHOLD IS AN ORGANISING TEST, NOT A LEGAL ONE. Three
│       electors is a phone call; 1,000 voters is a campaign. The same nominal
│       right is available to an individual in one state and only to an
│       organisation in another.
│
├─▸ A POLITICAL PARTY ... COLORADO · INDIANA · MICHIGAN · OREGON · SOUTH DAKOTA ·
│                         WASHINGTON
│
└─▸ AN ELECTION OFFICIAL ... CALIFORNIA · GEORGIA · OREGON · WYOMING
      ✱ worth naming because it is the one route that does not require a loser at
        all — the administrator can order a recount of a race nobody contested.
│
■ PART D — WHO PAYS, AND WHAT EARNS THE MONEY BACK
│
│  The deposit is the famous part. The REFUND CONDITION is the part that decides
│  whether the deposit is a fee or a wager, and it varies in KIND, not just degree:
│
├─▸ REFUNDED IF THE OUTCOME CHANGES ............... the standard rule
│     TEXAS [§§ 215.003–004] · MICHIGAN [§§ 168.867(9), 168.881(17)] · and most
│     tier-3 states
│     ✱ A WAGER ON A ONE-IN-TWO-THOUSAND EVENT. Of 6,929 statewide general
│       elections from 2000 to 2023, 36 went to a recount and THREE reversed.
│
├─▸ REFUNDED IF THE OUTCOME CHANGES *OR* IF AN AUTOMATIC RECOUNT WOULD HAVE BEEN
│     WARRANTED ANYWAY ... COLORADO [C.R.S. 1-10.5-106]
│     ✱ the only design on the map that refunds for being RIGHT ABOUT THE MARGIN
│       rather than right about the winner.
│
├─▸ REFUNDED IF FRAUD OR SUBSTANTIAL ERROR IS DISCOVERED ... PENNSYLVANIA
│     [25 P.S. § 3261(d)]
│     ✱ A DIFFERENT BET ENTIRELY. Petitioners can be completely vindicated on the
│       facts — real error found, result unchanged — and still get their money
│       back, which no outcome-based rule allows.
│
├─▸ REFUNDED IF THE DISCREPANCY EXCEEDS A STATED SIZE ... MINNESOTA, where the
│     jurisdiction pays if the recount changes the winner OR shows a discrepancy
│     "greater than two votes and greater than one-quarter of one percent"
│     [§ 204C.35 subd. 2]
│     ✱ the only refund rule keyed to the ACCURACY OF THE ORIGINAL COUNT rather
│       than to the identity of the winner. It is an audit test wearing a recount's
│       clothes — and it is the one provision on this map that pays a candidate for
│       finding an error that did not help them.
│
└─▸ ⊘ NO REFUND AVAILABLE AT ALL ... reported for KENTUCKY · NORTH DAKOTA ·
      RHODE ISLAND · SOUTH DAKOTA  ◈ this list is tracker-sourced and the trackers
      disagree with each other; it is NOT verified against the statutes here and
      should be checked before citation. See Note 5.
│
■ PART E — METHOD, AND WHETHER THE SECOND NUMBER IS THE OFFICIAL ONE
│
├─▸ WHO CHOOSES HAND OR MACHINE
│     TEXAS .......... THE PETITIONER CHOOSES; absent a request, ballots are
│                      recounted electronically "using the same program as the
│                      original count"  [Tex. Elec. Code § 214.042(c)]
│                      ✱ A MACHINE RECOUNT ON THE SAME PROGRAM CANNOT DETECT A
│                        PROGRAMMING ERROR. It re-runs the arithmetic, not the
│                        reading of the ballot. It is a check on tabulation and
│                        nothing else — the default in most states.
│     FLORIDA ........ machine first; if the margin is then under 0.25%, a SECOND
│                      recount BY HAND of overvotes and undervotes only
│     DELAWARE ....... machine first; hand recount if still under 0.25%
│     WASHINGTON ..... separate thresholds for a machine recount and a MANUAL one
│     GEORGIA ........ ⚑ full hand recount, no machine stage, for the eight
│                      offices in Part A  [SB 3EX, 2026]
│
└─▸ ⚖ DOES THE RECOUNT REPLACE THE CERTIFIED RESULT? YES — AND THIS IS THE WHOLE
      DIFFERENCE FROM AN AUDIT. A recount produces the official tally; Georgia's
      2026 statute says so in terms, that the hand-counted results "become the new
      official results." The post-election audit crosswalk found that in most
      states an audit has NO mechanism to amend a certificate. The recount needs
      no such mechanism because it IS the count, not a check on one.
      ✱ THE TWO INSTRUMENTS ARE THEREFORE NOT REDUNDANT AND NOT SUBSTITUTES. An
        audit samples to detect error and mostly cannot fix it; a recount re-tallies
        to replace the number and mostly is not triggered. A state can have both
        and still have no working remedy, because the audit that could detect the
        problem does not bind and the recount that would bind does not fire.
│
■ PART F — WHAT NO RECOUNT ON THIS MAP REACHES
│
├─  ELIGIBILITY, AGAIN. A recount re-counts the ballots that were accepted. It
│   does not revisit acceptance. The sharpest case of the cycle is INDIANA SENATE
│   DISTRICT 23, 2026: the state Recount Commission discarded ballots lacking a
│   clerk's signature and flipped a three-vote race; the Indiana Supreme Court
│   restored them on 10 September 2026 and flipped it back, 6,335–6,332. The
│   recount turned entirely on WHICH BALLOTS COUNT, which is not a counting
│   question, and a court had to answer it.
│
├─  ⊘ FEDERAL LAW. THERE IS NO FEDERAL RECOUNT REQUIREMENT, exactly as there is
│   no federal audit requirement. The Help America Vote Act regulates the voting
│   SYSTEM and commands no second count of any result. The one federal clock that
│   constrains recounts — the deadline for a state to resolve a presidential
│   contest before the electors meet — DOES NOT APPLY TO A MIDTERM. On
│   3 November 2026 the binding deadlines are state deadlines and the seating
│   dates of the chambers, and nothing federal shortens them.
│
├─  THE MARGIN THAT NEVER GETS COUNTED. Every tier on this map is keyed to a
│   margin, which means the entire system is designed around the possibility that
│   a CLOSE count is wrong and is silent on the possibility that a LOPSIDED one
│   is. No state recounts a race because the result was surprising.
│
├─  THE DOWN-BALLOT MAJORITY. The percentage triggers are written for statewide
│   contests; Michigan's are the rare office-specific ones. A county commission
│   race decided by four votes is inside almost no state's automatic trigger as a
│   percentage of a small electorate, and its loser is the least likely of anyone
│   on the ballot to be able to fund a tier-3 recount.
│
└─  THE CERTIFIED RESULT THAT IS WRONG FOR A REASON A COUNT CANNOT FIND. Where the
    objection is eligibility, procedure, or fraud rather than arithmetic, the
    recount is the wrong instrument and the election contest is the only one left.
    That is the next map in this lane, and the three court-only states in Part A
    are where it is the FIRST instrument as well as the last.
