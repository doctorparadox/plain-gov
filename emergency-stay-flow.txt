HOW AN EMERGENCY STAY APPLICATION MOVES THROUGH THE COURTS      (as of 14 September 2026)

│            20 Jan 2025 — 23 at least partly FOR the administration, 7 against, and 7
│            with no written explanation at all; oral argument ordered in 5.

■ 0. THERE HAS TO BE AN ORDER TO STAY   (a stay suspends; it does not decide)
│     A district judge enters something that stops the government: a temporary restraining
│     order, a preliminary injunction, an APA vacatur of a rule, or a final judgment.
│     ◇ is it appealable?
│     ├─ TRO ──▶ generally NOT appealable, so the government's first argument is usually
│     │    that the TRO is "really" a preliminary injunction in disguise
│     └─ PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION ──▶ appealable of right — 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1)
│     [ Since Trump v. CASA (27 June 2025) the order below is narrower by default.
│       Universal injunctions are out; relief now runs through named plaintiffs,
│       certified classes, or APA vacatur — and the stay fight moved with it. ]
│
├─◆ 1. FIRST STOP: THE COURT THAT ISSUED THE ORDER   (FRAP 8(a)(1))
│     The movant must ordinarily ask the district court to stay its own injunction before
│     asking anyone else. In practice this is satisfied in a paragraph and denied in a day.
│     └─▶ its real function is not review but TIMESTAMPING: it starts the clock and
│          creates the record the circuit will say it is reviewing.
│
├─◆ 2. THE COURT OF APPEALS — THE MOTIONS PANEL   ⚑ where the case is usually decided
│     A three-judge motions panel, assigned by rotation, on paper briefing, no argument.
│     Three different things can come out of it, and only one has a written standard:
│     │
│     ├─ 2a. ⚑⚑ THE ADMINISTRATIVE STAY — the door before the door
│     │     A short order freezing the district court's order "pending consideration" of
│     │     the stay motion. It is NOT a ruling on the stay factors and says so.
│     │     ✕ NO STATUTORY BASIS · ✕ NO NAMED FACTORS · ✕ NO WRITTEN TIME LIMIT
│     │     ✕ NO REQUIRED EXPLANATION · ✕ NOT ITSELF APPEALABLE IN MOST CIRCUITS
│     │     └─▶ Because it needs no findings, it is the fastest instrument in the system,
│     │          and because it has no built-in expiry it can outlast the dispute.
│     │     [ Justice Barrett, concurring in United States v. Texas on 19 March 2024,
│     │       wrote that an administrative stay "should last no longer than necessary to
│     │       make an intelligent decision on the motion for a stay pending appeal." That
│     │       is a norm, not a rule, and nothing enforces it. Two recent examples of the
│     │       instrument at the top of the system: Justice Alito, 21 Nov 2025 (Texas map
│     │       litigation), later extended by the full Court; Chief Justice Roberts,
│     │       21 Aug 2026 (White House ballroom construction), stay granted 5–4 ten days
│     │       later. In both, the temporary order set the facts on the ground. ]
│     │
│     ├─ 2b. THE STAY PENDING APPEAL — the four Nken v. Holder factors (556 U.S. 418, 2009)
│     │     1. a STRONG SHOWING that the applicant is likely to succeed on the merits
│     │     2. IRREPARABLE INJURY to the applicant absent a stay
│     │     3. SUBSTANTIAL INJURY to the other parties if the stay issues
│     │     4. where the PUBLIC INTEREST lies
│     │     ★ factors 3 and 4 MERGE when the government is the opposing party — which
│     │       means that in every case on this map the four-factor test is a three-factor
│     │       test, and the government's institutional interest absorbs the public's.
│     │     ◇ granted? ──▶ the injunction is frozen for the life of the appeal (stage 7)
│     │     ◇ denied?  ──▶ up to stage 3 or straight to stage 4
│     │
│     └─ 2c. INJUNCTION PENDING APPEAL — All Writs Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)
│           When there is no order to suspend — the movant LOST below and wants the
│           status quo frozen anyway — the request is affirmative relief, not a stay.
│           Higher bar in theory; the same motions panel, the same week, in practice.
│
├─◆ 3. EN BANC — the panel is not the last word in the circuit
│     The full court of appeals can vacate a motions-panel stay without any Supreme Court
│     involvement. Rarely used, slow, and it cuts both ways: an en banc court can restore
│     an injunction a panel froze, or freeze one a panel left standing.
│     └─▶ a party that loses en banc has lost the circuit entirely ──▶ stage 4
│
├─◆ 4. THE CIRCUIT JUSTICE   (Supreme Court Rules 22 and 23; 28 U.S.C. § 2101(f))
│     An application goes to the justice allotted to that circuit under 28 U.S.C. § 42.
│     Rule 23.3: relief must have been sought below first — the one hard gate on the route.
│     ├─ ACT ALONE ──▶ possible, and how in-chambers opinions are written; now uncommon
│     └─ REFER TO THE FULL COURT ──▶ the norm in any case with public stakes ──▶ stage 5
│     ◇ denied by one justice? ──▶ may be renewed to another, but a renewed application
│        is referred to the entire Court — so justice-shopping buys days, not outcomes.

├─◆ 5. THE FULL COURT   ⚑ five votes, not four
│     ★ THE ASYMMETRY THAT DEFINES THE TRACK: certiorari takes FOUR votes; interim relief
│       takes FIVE. The merits docket can be opened by a minority. The emergency docket
│       can only be moved by a majority — so it is structurally the majority's instrument.
│     Briefing is compressed to days or weeks. There is normally no argument and no
│     conference deliberation. Six things can come out:
│     ├─ ✕ APPLICATION DENIED ──▶ the order below stands; usually no reasons given
│     ├─ ★ STAY GRANTED ──▶ the injunction is frozen pending appeal and any cert petition
│     ├─ ⚑ ADMINISTRATIVE STAY ──▶ the same short-order instrument as 2a, now at the top
│     ├─ PARTIAL STAY ──▶ the order below is narrowed rather than suspended (the CASA form)
│     ├─ CERT BEFORE JUDGMENT ──▶ the application is converted into a merits case and the
│     │    court of appeals is skipped entirely
│     └─ ◆ SET FOR ARGUMENT ──▶ rare and getting less rare: argument has been ordered in
│          5 emergency matters since Jan 2025, including Trump v. Cook, argued January
│          2026 and decided 29 June 2026 in a signed 5–4 opinion with five separate
│          writings — an emergency application resolved exactly like a merits case.
│
├─◆ 6. WHAT THE RESULTING ORDER IS — AND IS NOT
│     NOT a merits judgment. NOT a final ruling. Frequently NOT signed and NOT explained
│     (7 of the 30 tracked decisions carry no reasoning whatsoever).
│     AND YET, from the Court's own words in 2025:
│     ├─ interim orders are "not conclusive as to the merits," but they "inform how a
│     │    court should exercise its equitable discretion in like cases"
│     │    — Trump v. Boyle, 23 July 2025
│     └─ "when this Court issues a decision, it constitutes a precedent that commands
│          respect in lower courts" — Justice Gorsuch, NIH v. APHA, 21 August 2025
│     ⚑ So the least-explained order in the system is also binding downward. A district
│       judge must now follow reasoning that was never written.
│
├─◆ 7. THE CLOCK — why the interim order is usually the final one
│     A stay runs until the court of appeals decides the appeal AND any certiorari
│     petition is resolved. That is months to years. During it the policy operates.
│     ├─▶ people are removed, employees are separated, money is spent or not spent,
│     │    an election is held under the challenged rule
│     └─▶ by the time the merits arrive, the remedy that would have mattered is gone,
│          and the case is often moot. THE STAY IS THE DECISION.

      ⚑⚑ WHAT THE SHAPE SHOWS
      1. THE ONLY STAGE WITH A WRITTEN STANDARD IS THE ONE MOST EASILY SKIPPED. Stage 2b
         has four named factors. Stage 2a has none — and 2a is what stops the order.
      2. EXPLANATION FALLS AS AUTHORITY RISES. District judge: findings required. Panel:
         four factors. Administrative stay: nothing. Supreme Court: often nothing, and it
         binds everyone. The obligation to justify runs opposite to the power to decide.
      3. FOUR VOTES OPEN THE SLOW DOOR; FIVE OPEN THE FAST ONE. A docket the majority
         alone can move will be used more as the majority and the applicant converge.
      4. THE MERGER CLAUSE DOES REAL WORK. Because Nken factors 3 and 4 merge when the
         government is a party, the harm to the people the injunction protected is
         weighed inside the government's own interest rather than against it.
      5. THE PIPELINE REWARDS WHOEVER CAN LOSE FAST. A litigant who can be in the circuit
         within a day of an injunction, and at the Supreme Court within a week, converts
         every district-court defeat into a stay application. That is a capacity, not a
         doctrine — and only one repeat player in American law has it in every circuit.
