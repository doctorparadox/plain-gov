HOW A FEDERAL CONTRACTOR IS SUSPENDED AND DEBARRED              (as of 10 November 2026)
│   Legend:  ──▶ main path  └─▶ branch or bypass  ◇ decision  ✕ out of federal work
│            ★ the step that decides it   ⚑ load-bearing stage, where the attrition is
│            ☐ a deadline or period the company can count in advance
│            ◎ one agency employee decides alone, with no hearing and no vote
│            § the rule is in statute, not the regulation   ⊘ a right nobody counts
│            ◈ a consequence written in no rule   ≠ unequal process, same contract
│            [ ] dated note
│   Scale:   Across the 24 largest federal agencies in fiscal year 2023: 2,018 cases
│            referred · 423 suspensions · 1,165 proposed debarments · 1,009 debarments
│            · 97 show-cause letters · 56 declinations · 15 voluntary exclusions · and
│            47 administrative agreements, entered by 10 agencies. Proposed debarments
│            outnumber suspensions nearly three to one, and the negotiated exits are
│            rarer than either — about one administrative agreement for every 21
│            debarments.

■ 0. TWO INSTRUMENTS, TWO STANDARDS OF PROOF        (FAR 9.406-2 · 9.407-2)   ⚑
│     The subpart holds two tools that look alike and are not. Everything downstream
│     — how fast it bites, what the company gets to say, how long it lasts — follows
│     from which one is used.
│
├─◆ SUSPENSION · "suspected, upon adequate evidence"              (FAR 9.407-2)
│     ☐ Temporary, pending an investigation and any legal proceedings that follow.
│     § An INDICTMENT is by itself adequate evidence. Nothing more is required.
│     The lowest evidentiary bar in the subpart, and the fastest instrument.
│
└─◆ DEBARMENT · a conviction, a civil judgment, or a preponderance  (FAR 9.406-2)
      ☐ A fixed period, ended by the calendar rather than by an investigation.
      Three tracks into it:
        (a) a CONVICTION or CIVIL JUDGMENT — contract fraud, antitrust bid-rigging,
            embezzlement, forgery, bribery, falsified records, false statements, tax
            evasion, receiving stolen property, a false "Made in America" label, or
            "any other offense indicating a lack of business integrity."
        (b) a PREPONDERANCE OF THE EVIDENCE — no court involved at all. Covers wilful
            non-performance or a history of failure to perform; drug-free workplace
            violations; unfair trade practices; delinquent federal taxes over $10,000;
            and the disclosure rule below.
        ◈ (b)(vi) is the one most contractors are surprised by: a principal's KNOWING
            FAILURE to disclose credible evidence of fraud, bribery, conflict of
            interest, a False Claims Act violation or a significant overpayment — a
            duty that runs until three years after final payment. Staying quiet is
            itself a cause.
        (c) the CATCH-ALL — "any other cause of so serious or compelling a nature
            that it affects the present responsibility of the contractor." No list,
            no court, no ceiling.
│
■ 1. WHO DECIDES            (FAR 2.101 · 9.406-1 · 9.407-1)      ◎ ⚑ ★
│     ONE PERSON. The suspending and debarring official — "a designee authorized by
│     the agency head to impose a suspension and/or a debarment." Not a board, not a
│     judge, not a commission. An employee of the agency doing the excluding, holding
│     no fixed term and no removal protection, deciding in the first instance and
│     with no appeal inside the subpart.
│     ◎ The title was unified in 2025: before then the regulation named a separate
│       "debarring official" and "suspending official."
│       [ FAR Case 2019-015, final rule effective 17 Jan. 2026 — see stage 13. ]
│     § Once a cause exists, THE BURDEN FLIPS. The company must demonstrate that it
│       is presently responsible and that exclusion is not necessary. The government
│       does not have to show that it is.      (FAR 9.406-1(b))
│     Where two agencies both have an interest, they coordinate through the
│     Interagency Suspension and Debarment Committee to pick the official best
│     situated to act; occasionally two act jointly. The committee is an unfunded
│     interagency body created by Executive Order 12549, and it has no power to
│     decide a case itself.
│

■ 2. THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS, WHICH IS THE WHOLE POINT       ⚑ ≠
│     This is where the two instruments split, and it is the stage a reader should
│     slow down on.
│
├─◆ 2a. SUSPENSION — EFFECT FIRST, NOTICE SECOND     (FAR 9.407-3(b)-(c))   ✕
│     │  The suspension is imposed. The company "shall be immediately advised" —
│     │  afterwards. The opportunity to oppose it comes "following the imposition
│     │  of suspension."
│     │  ☐ The notice gives 30 days to respond.
│     │  ◈ The notice describes the conduct "without disclosing the Government's
│     │    evidence." The company is told what it is accused of and not what the
│     │    accusation rests on.
│     └─▶ The company is out of new federal work from this moment, before it has
│         said a word.
│
└─◆ 2b. DEBARMENT — NOTICE FIRST, BUT THE NOTICE ALREADY EXCLUDES  (FAR 9.406-3(c))
      │  A notice of PROPOSED debarment goes to the company and any named affiliates,
      │  stating the reasons and the cause relied on.
      │  ☐ 30 days to submit information and argument in opposition — in writing, in
      │    person, or through a representative.
      │  ✕ ◈ A PROPOSED debarment carries the same exclusionary effect as a finished
      │      one. A company under proposal is already off the eligible list while the
      │      proposal is pending. The word "proposed" does not soften anything.
      │      (FAR 9.405(a) · and see stage 7)
      └─▶ A pre-notice letter — a warning shot that excludes nobody — exists and is
          optional: "A pre-notice letter is not required to initiate debarment."
          97 were sent government-wide in FY2023, against 1,165 proposed debarments.
│
│     ──▶
│
■ 3. THE 30 DAYS         (FAR 9.406-3(b)(1), (c) · 9.407-3(b)(1), (c)(5))   ☐
│     Both tracks converge on one written submission, and the regulation sets the
│     register deliberately low: the procedures are to be "as informal as is
│     practicable, consistent with principles of fundamental fairness."
│     ⚑ The single most consequential sentence for a company drafting that letter:
│       "A general denial is insufficient to raise a genuine dispute over facts
│       material to the proposed debarment."   (FAR 9.406-3(c)(3)(viii)(A))
│       A denial is not a dispute. Specificity in these thirty days is what decides
│       whether stage 4 ever opens.
│
│     ──▶

■ 4. ◇ THE HEARING THAT USUALLY DOES NOT HAPPEN   (FAR 9.406-3(b)(2) · 9.407-3)  ⚑ ★
│     Everything that looks like a trial in this subpart lives behind two gates, and
│     most cases never reach them.
│
│     ◇ GATE ONE — IS THE ACTION BASED ON A COURT'S WORK?
│     ├─▶ YES (a conviction · a civil judgment · for suspension, an indictment)
│     │     ✕ No fact proceedings. The decision is made "on the basis of all the
│     │       information in the administrative record." The company's 30-day letter
│     │       is in that record; nothing else is added.
│     │     § This is the hinge of the whole design: an indictment is adequate
│     │       evidence for a suspension (stage 0), and an indictment-based action is
│     │       also exempt from fact proceedings. A grand jury's finding, reached
│     │       without the company present, both triggers the exclusion and forecloses
│     │       the hearing about it.
│     │
│     └─▶ NO ──▶ ◇ GATE TWO — DID THE LETTER RAISE A GENUINE DISPUTE OVER FACTS
│                   MATERIAL TO THE ACTION?
│           ├─▶ NO  ✕ Administrative record again. No hearing.
│           └─▶ YES ──▶ ★ THE FULL PROCEDURE OPENS, and it is substantial:
│                 appear with counsel · submit documentary evidence · present
│                 witnesses · "confront any person the agency presents" · a
│                 transcribed record · and WRITTEN FINDINGS OF FACT.
│                 ◈ A fact-finder's findings bind the deciding official, who may
│                   reject them "only after specifically determining them to be
│                   arbitrary and capricious or clearly erroneous."
│                 ⊘ For SUSPENSION there is a third gate on top of these two: the
│                   Department of Justice or the responsible prosecutor may determine
│                   that substantial interests in a related proceeding would be
│                   prejudiced — and the fact proceedings do not happen at all. The
│                   company's right to a hearing can be switched off by a party that
│                   is not the deciding official and not reviewable in the
│                   proceeding.          (FAR 9.407-3(c)(6) · (d)(1))
│
│     ──▶
│
■ 5. THE DECISION       (FAR 9.406-3(d) · 9.407-3(d))      ★
│     ☐ Where no suspension is already in effect and the case is decided on the
│       record, the debarment decision is due within 45 days of the record closing,
│       extendable for good cause. Where a suspension IS in effect, the clock in this
│       stage does not bind — the company stays excluded while the official takes
│       longer.
│     For a debarment not based on a conviction or judgment, cause "must be
│     established by a preponderance of the evidence." For a suspension the standard
│     never rises above "adequate evidence."
│     ◈ A suspension may be modified, terminated, or left in force — and termination
│       is "without prejudice" to a later suspension or debarment by ANY agency. A
│       company that wins is not cleared; it is returned to the queue.
│
│     ──▶
│
■ 6. THE EXCLUSION, GOVERNMENT-WIDE AND IMMEDIATE      (FAR 9.404 · 9.405)   ✕ ⚑
│     § One agency's decision binds the whole executive branch. A debarment or
│       proposed debarment "shall be effective throughout the executive branch of the
│       Government." Agencies "shall not solicit offers from, award contracts to, or
│       consent to subcontracts with these contractors."
│     ☐ The record goes into the exclusions section of the System for Award
│       Management within 3 working days of the action taking effect, carrying the
│       agency, the cause, the effect and the termination date. Contracting officers
│       must re-check it before award.
│     What the exclusion reaches, beyond new prime contracts:
│       · the company may not act as an agent or representative of another contractor
│         in dealings with the government
│       · it may not act as an individual surety
│       · its bids are rejected and its proposals are not evaluated — no discussions
│         are even held — absent a written compelling-reason determination
│       · other contractors are barred from subcontracting with it above $45,000
│       · ordering activities may not place new orders, place schedule orders, or add
│         work or options under existing vehicles
│     └─▶ ◎ THE ONE DOOR THAT STAYS OPEN: the agency head, or a designee, may state
│         in writing a COMPELLING REASON to keep dealing with an excluded company.
│         Existing contracts also continue by default "unless the agency head directs
│         otherwise." So the instrument that binds every agency at once can be
│         unbound, one agency at a time, by one official with a memo.
│         ⊘ No public source found in this survey counts how often that happens.
│

■ 7. HOW LONG       (FAR 9.406-4 · 9.407-4)      ☐ §
│
├─◆ SUSPENSION — THE ONLY HARD CEILING IN THE SUBPART
│     ☐ If legal proceedings are not begun within 12 MONTHS of the suspension notice,
│       the suspension "shall be terminated" — unless a prosecuting office asks for
│       more, in which case it "may be extended for an additional 6 months."
│     ☐ "In no event may a suspension extend beyond 18 months, unless legal
│       proceedings have been initiated within that period."
│     § The suspending official must notify the Justice Department or the responsible
│       prosecutor at least 30 days before the 12 months run out, so that office can
│       ask for the extension. The deadline comes with a built-in reminder to the
│       party most likely to want it extended.
│     ◈ AND THERE THE CEILING STOPS. Once proceedings are begun inside the 18 months,
│       the suspension runs "pending the completion of an investigation and any
│       ensuing legal proceedings," with no further outer limit written anywhere. The
│       temporary instrument becomes open-ended at the moment a case is filed.
│
└─◆ DEBARMENT — A PRESUMPTION, NOT A LIMIT
      ☐ "Generally, debarment should not exceed 3 years," and must be "commensurate
        with the seriousness of the cause(s)." Three statutory departures:
          · drug-free workplace violations — up to 5 years
          · immigration-law determinations by Homeland Security or the Attorney
            General — 1 year, extendable in further one-year periods, and that
            determination "is not reviewable in the debarment proceedings"
          · false arms-control certifications — NOT LESS THAN 2 years, a floor rather
            than a ceiling, counting any suspension already served
      ☐ Time served under a suspension counts toward the debarment period.
      ◈ Both directions are open afterwards. The official MAY EXTEND a debarment to
        protect the government's interest — but "may not be extended solely on the
        basis of the facts and circumstances upon which the initial debarment action
        was based," so an extension needs something new. And the official MAY REDUCE
        it on the company's documented request: newly discovered evidence, a reversed
        conviction, or "a bona fide change in ownership or management."
      ⚑ That last clause is the most consequential sentence in the stage. Debarment
        attaches to present responsibility, and present responsibility can be
        acquired by replacing the people who lacked it. A change of management is a
        route out that a fine or a prosecution has no equivalent of.

■ 8. THE FOUR EXITS, AND THEY ARE NOT EQUIVALENT    (FAR 9.406-3(f)-(g) · 9.406-4)
│
├─◆ 8a. THE PERIOD EXPIRES ──▶ the SAM record carries a termination date; the
│       company becomes eligible again with no further decision required.
├─◆ 8b. REDUCTION OR EARLY TERMINATION ──▶ the official shortens it on request
│       (stage 7), or terminates a suspension outright — without prejudice.
├─◆ 8c. VOLUNTARY EXCLUSION ──▶ the company agrees to be excluded rather than
│       litigate. § It "must have Governmentwide effect," and it is entered in the
│       EXCLUSIONS SECTION OF SAM. 15 government-wide in FY2023.
└─◆ 8d. ADMINISTRATIVE AGREEMENT ──▶ ⚑ ★ the negotiated settlement in which the
        company accepts compliance obligations — monitors, certifications, reporting
        — and is NOT suspended and NOT debarred. It keeps selling. 47 entered by 10
        agencies in FY2023, against 1,009 debarments.
        ◈ This is the outcome a well-advised large contractor is working toward from
          the first notice, and the only exit that leaves the customer relationship
          intact.
│
│     ──▶

■ 9. WHO CAN SEE IT        (FAR 9.404 · 9.406-3(f) · 41 U.S.C. § 2313)     ⚑ ◈ ≠
│     The four exits land in two different databases with two different readerships,
│     and this is the sharpest asymmetry on the page.
│
├─◆ SUSPENSIONS · DEBARMENTS · VOLUNTARY EXCLUSIONS ──▶ the exclusions section of
│     the System for Award Management. Agency, cause, effect, termination date. This
│     is the list every contracting officer must check, and it is the list that makes
│     an exclusion a public fact.
│
└─◆ ADMINISTRATIVE AGREEMENTS ──▶ the Federal Awardee Performance and Integrity
      Information System, reached through the contractor performance reporting
      system, within 3 working days.
      § The filing is not discretionary. Congress required it: the database must
        include "each Federal administrative agreement entered into by the person and
        the Federal Government" to resolve a suspension or debarment proceeding.
        (41 U.S.C. § 2313(c)(4))
      ⚑ AND THE READERSHIP IS NOT THE PUBLIC. The current statute makes the database
        available to acquisition officials of federal agencies, to other government
        officials as the General Services Administration determines appropriate, and
        on request to the chairman and ranking member of the relevant congressional
        committees. It contains no public-access provision.   (§ 2313(e)(1))
      [ The predecessor statute did. Former 41 U.S.C. § 417b directed that the
        information be posted, "excluding past performance reviews, on a publicly
        available Internet website." That language survives in the current code only
        as an editorial note to the section that replaced it. ]
      ◈ So the exit that ends the relationship is published, and the exit that
        preserves it is filed where the public has no statutory right to look. The
        47 agreements of FY2023 are known as a COUNT, from the committee's report to
        Congress, and not as DOCUMENTS.

■ 10. ⊘ CAN THE COMPANY SUE — AND THE HONEST ANSWER IS PARTLY UNSETTLED
│     Yes, and the route is sixty years old: a debarment is reviewable under the
│     Administrative Procedure Act.   [ Gonzalez v. Freeman, 334 F.2d 570 (D.C. Cir.
│     1964). ] Courts have also set a due-process floor: absent an indictment, a
│     hearing should follow a suspension within thirty days [ Horne Bros. v. Laird,
│     463 F.2d 1268 (D.C. Cir. 1972) ], and an agency that excludes a company in
│     practice without saying so owes it notice of the charges [ Old Dominion Dairy
│     Prods. v. Sec'y of Def., 631 F.2d 953 (D.C. Cir. 1980) ].
│     ⊘ THREE THINGS THIS MAP DOES NOT ASSERT, BECAUSE THEY COULD NOT BE ESTABLISHED
│       TO TWO SOURCES HERE:
│         · WHICH COURT. Challenges have been entertained in federal district court
│           and in the Court of Federal Claims, and the sources surveyed do not
│           establish an exclusive forum. [ District court: Agility Defense & Gov't
│           Servs. v. DoD (N.D. Ala. 2012), rev'd, 739 F.3d 586 (11th Cir. 2013);
│           Friedler v. GSA (D.D.C. 2017). Court of Federal Claims: Inchcape Shipping
│           Servs. Holdings v. United States, No. 13-953 (Fed. Cl. 2 Jan. 2014). ]
│         · THE STANDARD. The Administrative Procedure Act confines a reviewing court
│           to "the whole record or those parts of it cited by a party" and permits
│           fresh factfinding only where a statute provides for trial de novo — and
│           no statute provides for one here. (5 U.S.C. § 706) Whether and how
│           closely courts in fact scrutinise these decisions is contested in the
│           literature and is not settled on this page.
│         · HOW OFTEN IT WORKS. No source found in this survey publishes a rate at
│           which suspensions or debarments are overturned. The government counts the
│           actions it takes and nobody appears to count the ones that do not hold.

■ 11. ≠ THE MIRROR, WHICH IS WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
│     The same alleged fraud, two parties, two procedures.
│
│     THE EMPLOYEE WHO REPORTS IT            │  THE COMPANY ACCUSED OF IT
│     (41 U.S.C. § 4712)                     │  (FAR Subpart 9.4)

│       investigate                          │    decides alone
│     ☐ 180 days to a determination or       │  ☐ 30 days to write a letter
│       report, extendable by agreement      │
│     § agency head must decide within 30    │  ◇ a hearing only if the action rests
│       days of the report                   │    on no court finding AND the letter
│                                            │    raises a genuine factual dispute
│     ☐ after 210 days, remedies are deemed  │  ✕ for a suspension, prosecutors can

│       either party, be tried by the court  │    success rate is uncounted
│       with a jury"                         │
│     ☐ 3 years to complain · 2 years to sue │  ◈ exclusion begins before any of it
│                                            │
│     ◈ The asymmetry is not an accident of drafting. The employee's remedy is a
│       statute Congress wrote to create a private right of action. The company's
│       is a regulation the executive branch wrote to protect its own purchasing.
│       One is designed to be used by the party it protects; the other is designed
│       to be used ON the party it binds.

■ 12. WHAT IS PENDING          [ as of 10 November 2026 ]
│     Subpart 9.4 is inside the government-wide rewrite of the acquisition rules. The
│     proposal for Part 9 was published on 18 September 2026 and comments closed on
│     19 October 2026. For a reader the two things worth knowing are:
│       ☐ IT KEEPS THE SUBPART. Debarment, suspension and ineligibility survive the
│         rewrite with their section numbers, and the preamble says the Part 9
│         streamlining does "not substantively change policy or procedures in the
│         part." The deadlines, periods and procedures mapped above are the current
│         rules and are proposed to stay.
│       ◈ IT ADDS A CAUSE. The proposal would make non-compliance with a new clause
│         on diversity-related discrimination by federal contractors a cause for both
│         debarment and suspension, inserted into the lists at stage 0.
│       [ FAR Case 2026-011, RIN 9000-AO84, 91 Fed. Reg. 59584 (18 Sept. 2026),
│         proposed rule; the added cause implements Executive Order 14398 of 26 March
│         2026, 91 Fed. Reg. 16147 (31 Mar. 2026). A proposed rule is not law; no
│         final rule had issued as of this page's date. ]
