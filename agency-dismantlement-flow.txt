HOW A FEDERAL AGENCY IS DISMANTLED — THE 2025-26 PLAYBOOK AS A PROCESS  (as of August 24, 2026)
│  ──▶ main path   └─▶ branch   ◆ tool   ◇ decision point   ✕ stopped   [ ] live 2026 status
│  Names inside this fence are plain text; the wikilinks are in the prose below.
│
■ STAGE 0 · THE AUTHORITY PROBLEM        (what the whole playbook is built to work around)
│   An agency created by statute can be abolished only by statute. The President's standing
│   REORGANIZATION AUTHORITY — the fast-track power to send Congress a reorganization plan
│   that took effect unless Congress objected — LAPSED IN 1984 and was never renewed.
│   ⇒ Nothing below abolishes an agency. Everything below EMPTIES one.
│     Every tool on this map is execution-side: personnel, money, and paperwork.
│
├──▶ STAGE 1 · DRAIN THE HEADCOUNT           (voluntary exits — no RIF procedure applies) ⏱ MONTHS
│    ├─◆ Hiring freeze + 4-to-1 rule .. EO 14210 (Feb 11, 2025): one hire per four departures
│    │                                  [exempt: public safety, immigration, law enforcement]
│    ├─◆ Deferred resignation ........ "resign now, stay on payroll" — full pay, no duties
│    │                                  [~154,000 drew full salary for 6+ months; agencies
│    │                                   reported ~144,000 more approved to exit by end-2025]
│    ├─◆ Probationary terminations ... employees inside the 1-2 year window have almost no
│    │                                  appeal rights; the cheapest cut in the entire playbook
│    └─◆ Early retirement (VERA/VSIP) . the pre-existing, lawful, uncontroversial tool
│         └─▶ NET EFFECT [Dec 2024 → Jan 2026]: about -256,000 employees, roughly -11 to -12 %
│             — the largest federal workforce reduction since the 1990s, and a civilian
│             headcount near 2.0-2.1 million, the lowest since the mid-1960s
│
├──▶ STAGE 2 · THE REDUCTION IN FORCE            (the involuntary layer — 5 CFR Part 351) ⏱ 60+ DAYS
│    ├─ EO 14210 orders agencies to "promptly undertake preparations to initiate large-scale
│    │  RIFs," prioritizing functions NOT mandated by statute and staff not deemed essential
│    │  in the agency's own SHUTDOWN CONTINGENCY PLAN — the plan repurposed as a target list
│    ├─ OMB + OPM joint memo (Feb 26, 2025) → Agency RIF and Reorganization Plans due Mar 13
│    ├─ Mechanics: define the COMPETITIVE AREA (drawn narrow, the outcome is decided here) →
│    │  build the retention register → rank → issue 60-day specific notice → separate
│    ├─◇ Retention order — WHAT RANKS FIRST?
│    │    ├─ Old rule ....... tenure group → veterans' preference → LENGTH OF SERVICE → rating
│    │    └─ New rule ....... tenure group → PERFORMANCE (prior 3 years) → seniority as tiebreak
│    │       [OPM final rule published Aug 3, 2026 · EFFECTIVE SEPTEMBER 2, 2026 — also
│    │        rewrites RPL, CTAP, ICTAP and transfer-of-function rules, and moves RIF,
│    │        suitability and probationary APPEALS from MSPB adjudication to OPM]
│    └─◇ Can a court stop it? — see STAGE 7. [SCOTUS stayed the government-wide injunction
│         8-1 on Jul 8, 2025, expressly reserving judgment on any individual agency plan]
│
├──▶ STAGE 3 · MOVE THE FUNCTIONS OUT           (the paperwork layer — no statute repealed) ⏱ MONTHS
│    │   A statutory program cannot be cancelled, so its DAY-TO-DAY ADMINISTRATION is
│    │   contracted out to another department by INTERAGENCY AGREEMENT. The program still
│    │   exists on paper, at the original agency, funded by the original appropriation.
│    ├─◆ Interagency agreement ....... signed by two secretaries; no vote, no rulemaking,
│    │                                 no public comment, no Federal Register notice required
│    ├─◆ Transfer of function ........ employees follow the work, or don't; either way the
│    │                                 sending agency's org chart hollows out
│    └─ THE WORKED EXAMPLE — Department of Education
│         ├─ Mar 20, 2025 EO: close the Department "to the maximum extent appropriate and
│         │  permitted by law," while continuing delivery of programs Congress authorized
│         ├─ [as of Jul 1, 2026: 14 published interagency agreements moving day-to-day
│         │   management of ~150 programs to SIX agencies — Labor, Interior, State, HHS,
│         │   Treasury, DOJ. Labor now oversees K-12 grants, including Title I]
│         ├─ [the reimbursements are trivial: up to $262,000 in FY2025, ~$807,000 in FY2026
│         │   — the transfers are not being priced as if they were transfers]
│         ├─ ✕ Congress FULLY FUNDED the Department in the FY2026 bills. The transfers
│         │   continued anyway. Funding an agency does not staff it.
│         └─ [Jul 15, 2026: House Education & Workforce approved 10 bills to CODIFY the
│             dispersal, near party-line — the first attempt to make any of it law]
│
├──▶ STAGE 4 · CHOKE THE MONEY          (the appropriation stays enacted; the cash stops moving)
│    ├─◆ Grant termination ......... 2 CFR 200.340(a)(4) — terminate when an award "no longer
│    │                               effectuates the program goals or agency priorities"
│    │     ✕ [D. Mass., Jul 17, 2026 — 20 states, 3 governors and D.C.: the Termination
│    │        Clause "does not permit agencies to terminate grants based on program goals and
│    │        agency priorities identified AFTER grants were awarded." Declaratory judgment
│    │        only; no terminated grant was reinstated. OMB has proposed rewriting the
│    │        regulation, which would move the ground out from under the ruling.]
│    ├─◆ Contract termination ...... "for convenience" clauses, FAR Part 49 — always lawful,
│    │                               remedy is money later, never the work continuing now
│    ├─◆ Apportionment footnotes ... OMB releases the money slowly, or conditionally, or in a
│    │                               way that guarantees it cannot be obligated in time
│    ├─◆ Pocket rescission ......... propose a rescission inside the last 45 days of the
│    │                               fiscal year, then sit on the funds until they expire
│    │     [SCOTUS allowed ~$4B in foreign aid to lapse this way, 6-3 on the emergency
│    │      docket, Sept 2025 — Jackson, Sotomayor and Kagan dissenting]
│    └─◆ Impoundment ............... simply not spending it
│          [GAO was reviewing ~39 possible Impoundment Control Act violations as of Apr 2025
│           and has issued violation decisions on DOT/NEVI, HHS Head Start, NIH grants,
│           IMLS, and DOE school funds. GAO's remedy is a REPORT and a referral. That is all.]
│
├──▶ STAGE 5 · RECLASSIFY THE SURVIVORS         (make the remaining workforce removable) ⏱ ONGOING
│    ├─◆ Schedule Policy/Career .... moves career positions that are "policy-influencing" out
│    │                               of the competitive service and into the excepted service,
│    │                               stripping adverse-action and appeal protections
│    │     [EO 14410, Jun 3, 2026 — about 8,000 positions, from a 229-page appendix of
│    │      ~4,800 position descriptions. OPM's own earlier estimate ran up to 50,000.]
│    ├─◆ Performance system ........ forced distributions in the current ratings cycle — which
│    │                               now feeds directly into RIF retention under the new rule
│    └─◆ Return-to-office + relocation .. attrition by working condition, no procedure at all
│
├──▶ STAGE 6 · WHAT CONGRESS CAN ACTUALLY DO        (three real tools, one of them brand new)
│    ├─◆ Appropriations riders ..... THE ONE THAT BIT. The FY2026 minibus (enacted Jan 2026)
│    │     bars covered agencies from spending funds to reorganize without ADVANCE NOTICE to
│    │     the Appropriations Committees — reprogramming to create or end a program, moving
│    │     an office, or cutting >5 % of a program's staff or funding — and requires
│    │     45 DAYS' PRIOR NOTICE OF ANY RIF. [Covers DOJ, Interior, Commerce, Energy, EPA,
│    │     NSF, NASA and more; reprogramming requests were due by Jun 30, 2026.]
│    ├─◆ The ICA / GAO referral .... GAO finds the violation; the Comptroller General may sue.
│    │                               No automatic penalty, no automatic release of funds.
│    ├─◆ Authorizing legislation ... abolish it properly, or restore it properly. Needs 60 in
│    │                               the Senate, which is why it is the least-used tool here.
│    └─◆ Oversight and confirmation  hearings, holds, subpoenas — leverage, not remedy
│
└──▶ STAGE 7 · WHAT COURTS HAVE ACTUALLY DONE        (four tracks, and only two of them work)
     ├─ TRACK A · APA "arbitrary and capricious" ....... the strongest theory, slowest relief
     │    ✓ Voice of America / USAGM — Mar 17, 2026, D.D.C.: dismantlement unlawful and
     │      arbitrary and capricious; 1,042 employees ordered back to work Mar 23
     │      ✕ [appeals panel STAYED that order Mar 31, 2026 pending appeal]
     ├─ TRACK B · Tucker Act channelling .............. the theory that KILLED most funding
     │    suits: a grant termination is in substance a contract claim, so it belongs in the
     │    Court of Federal Claims — which can award DAMAGES but cannot order the money to
     │    keep flowing. [Dept. of Education v. California, Apr 2025 · NIH v. APHA, Aug 2025]
     ├─ TRACK C · Civil-service channelling ........... RIF challenges belong in the MSPB/FLRA
     │    system, not district court — and OPM's new rule moves that adjudication in-house
     │    ✕ [SCOTUS stay, Jul 8, 2025, 8-1 · Ninth Circuit vacated the 22-agency injunction
     │       Sept 19, 2025 · but the D.C. Circuit sitting EN BANC reinstated the CFPB
     │       injunction, argued Feb 24, 2026 — the one agency-wide block still standing]
     └─ TRACK D · Breach of an appropriations DEAL ..... the narrowest and most effective
          ✓ After the 43-day Oct-Nov 2025 shutdown, the reopening deal barred shutdown-period
            RIFs. The court held every RIF taken from Oct 1 NULL AND VOID, ordered rescission
            and full back pay, restored employees to their Sept 30, 2025 status, and barred
            further RIF steps at State, Education, GSA and SBA through Jan 30, 2026.
            ⇒ A CONTRACT theory beat every constitutional one on the board.

THE FIVE STRUCTURAL FACTS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ① THE APPROPRIATION IS NOT THE CONTROL.  Congress fully funded the Department of Education
   and the dispersal continued. Money is authority to spend, not a duty to staff.
 ② NARROW THE COMPETITIVE AREA AND YOU HAVE DECIDED THE RIF.  Everything after that is
   arithmetic. The contested judgment happens before any employee is ranked.
 ③ THE REMEDY IS THE WHOLE FIGHT.  Damages later ≠ the program running now. Tucker Act
   channelling did not say the terminations were lawful — it said the courthouse was wrong.
 ④ PROCEDURE BEATS PRINCIPLE.  The tools that actually stopped things were an appropriations
   rider and a reopening deal. The separation-of-powers arguments are still pending.
 ⑤ NONE OF IT IS PERMANENT, AND THAT IS THE POINT.  No statute was repealed, so every agency
   on this map still legally exists. Rebuilding institutional capacity, however, is not a
   paperwork step — and unlike the dismantlement, it has no fast track.
